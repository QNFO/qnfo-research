New preprint proposes the *grounding criterion*: research deserves advancement iff it shows a live path to real-world utility, declares where its premises end, and doesn't hide inside a discipline. ## Mastodon (~500 chars)

Why should a reader care? Most research effort today is allocated by disciplinary prestige and publication counts — neither measures a traceable connection to any human problem. As AI makes silo-fodder cheap, "should this research be advanced?" has become urgent and unanswerable.

"The Purpose Principle" (QNFO.RES.012) proposes a testable grounding criterion: (G1) a live, non-vacuous path from questions to real-world utility; (G2) a declared premise chain (derived vs. unanalyzable primitives vs named imports); (G3) no legitimacy solely from discipline, abstraction level, or silo. Exploratory research stays valuable iff it keeps an open grounding path.

Premise-depth disclosure: the argument is as deep as L0–L2 (a value primitive + two falsifiable premises) — the paper proves no theorem. Falsification: F1 (siloed programs produce utility at grounded rates → L1 fails), F2 (disclosure harms quality → L2 fails), F3 (the criterion self-siloing into a ritual → self-refuting). Adversarial validation invited.

## The gate (research skill, Phase 5 — BLOCKING at publication, alongside SO-WHAT-GATE-1)

Every theory/theorem-bearing artifact MUST include a **premise-depth audit**:

1. **Trace the chain.** Enumerate the derivation premise-by-premise: each step names the premise it uses (axiom, definition, interpretive identification, physical input, imported theorem).
2. **Find the floor.** Identify where the chain bottoms out — the premises that are *not* derived: (a) primitives (acts/terms taken as unanalyzable), (b) interpretive identifications (symbol ↔ structure mappings imported from outside the calculus), (c) physical inputs (empirical constants, symmetry data, particle-content data).
3. **Declare the depth honestly.** One sentence of the form: *"This theorem is as deep as its premises; its premises end at ⟨floor⟩ — [primitives], [identifications], [physical inputs]."* If the theorem merely re-describes an input in new notation, say so (notation-vs-engine distinction).
4. **State the premise-reduction claim (if any).** If the derivation uses FEWER premises than the standard derivation of the same result, name both counts and justify why the reduction is not a hidden import.
5. **Falsification boundary.** State what would break the chain (which premise, if false/withdrawn, collapses the result).

## Anti-pattern blocked

**PREMISE-SHALLOW-1:** presenting a re-description as a derivation — a theorem that imports its conclusion via an interpretive identification while claiming premise-reduction. Canonical class: symbol↔structure identifications ("the mark is a half-turn") that carry the physics invisibly. The audit forces the identification into the open.

**PREMISE-SHALLOW-2:** deflationary chains that terminate in "the boundary remains external" without a minimal-premise theorem or a practical redirect (user anti-pattern note, mem-Fvj3jMNcBKgo).

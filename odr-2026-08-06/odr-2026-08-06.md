# Tensor Networks as Bruhat-Tits Tree Computation

## Abstract

In May 2026, classical tensor-network methods running on a laptop were shown to simulate 100+ interacting qubit dynamics — a problem claimed to require quantum hardware. We argue that this result is not a defeat of quantum computing but the empirical confirmation of a structural fact: local-Hamiltonian dynamics are p-adically ultrametric, and tensor networks succeed because they accidentally instantiate the Bruhat-Tits (BT) tree — the combinatorial tree encoding the completions of $\mathbb{Q}$ at every prime. We formalize this isomorphism, connect the tree's branching structure to the Rosetta Stone $T_{2,3,5}$ (the unifying algebraic object of the QNFO dimensionless reformulation), and distinguish what this result proves from what it does not. Three testable predictions are pre-registered: prime-indexed tree encoding should outperform standard matrix product states on 3D lattice benchmarks (REG-ODR0601); resonant readout fidelity should show a $p$-indexed signature (REG-ODR0602); and the $p=5$ signal must be distinguished from the base-10 valuation artifact by using exact rational source quantities (REG-ODR0603). The Casimir constraint, quantum readout bottleneck, and any broader "universal tree" claims are identified as open questions, not asserted findings.

## 1. Introduction

Physical computation — the process by which a physical system transitions between distinguishable states — is constrained by the Landauer principle: erasing one bit dissipates at minimum $k_B T \ln 2$ of energy. This coupling of information and energy establishes a lower bound, but it does not specify the geometry of the state space over which that bound operates. We propose a candidate for that geometry in the restricted but important case of local-Hamiltonian quantum dynamics: the Bruhat-Tits tree.

The Bruhat-Tits building for $\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is a $(p+1)$-regular tree $T_p$ whose vertices correspond to homothety classes of lattices in $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$ [1]. At depth $r$, the sphere $\mathcal{S}_r$ contains $(p+1) p^{r-1}$ vertices. This exponential growth in distinguishable states with depth is the geometric expression of the exponential scaling of computational state spaces in physically relevant regimes.

> `[speculative]` **Thesis:** The correlation structure of local Hamiltonians on regular lattices is p-adically ultrametric — two sites separated by distance $d$ have correlations that decay as $p^{-\operatorname{ord}_p(d)}$. The natural geometry for organizing these correlations is not Euclidean distance but p-adic distance, and the natural computational structure is the BT tree. Tensor-network methods succeed because they happen to instantiate this geometry.

> `[not a claim]` **What this paper does NOT assert:** (i) that the BT tree governs ALL physical computation (it does not — general-purpose sequential computation resists tree decomposition by structure, not by engineering limitation); (ii) that quantum computing is "defeated" (the Simons result is a bound on a specific problem class, not on quantum advantage in general); (iii) that the tree structure implies any broader "universal" metaphysics. The claim is narrow: local-Hamiltonian dynamics on regular lattices are tree-decomposable, and the BT tree is the mathematical object that makes this property explicit.

## 2. The Simons/Flatiron 2026 Result

In May 2026, researchers at the Center for Computational Quantum Physics (CCQ) at the Simons Foundation's Flatiron Institute and Boston University demonstrated that classical tensor-network methods — running on a personal laptop — could simulate the dynamics of 100+ interacting qubits on square, cubic, and diamond lattices [2]. This directly challenges a March 2025 claim in *Science* that such simulations require quantum hardware and constitute "quantum supremacy."

From an ultrametric perspective, this outcome is not surprising. Matrix product states (MPS) and projected entangled pair states (PEPS) are tensor networks that encode correlations as a tree-structured contraction of local tensors. For an MPS with bond dimension $\chi$, the computational cost scales as $O(\chi^3)$ for nearest-neighbor operations — precisely the vertex-count scaling of the BT tree at depth corresponding to the system size.

The result is consistent with a large body of prior work establishing that gapped local Hamiltonians satisfy an area law for entanglement entropy [3], and that ground states of such systems admit efficient MPS representations. The novelty is not the existence of efficient classical simulation — it is the demonstration that this efficiency extends to *dynamics* of 100+ qubit systems at scales previously thought to require quantum hardware.

> `[established]` The Simons result is an experimental verification of tree-decomposability for local-Hamiltonian dynamics. It bounds the "quantum supremacy" claim to problems that are *not* tree-decomposable — which is a smaller, better-defined class than the original claim implied.

## 3. Tensor Networks as BT-Tree Computation

### 3.1 The Structural Isomorphism

The BT tree $T_p$ and a matrix product state with bond dimension $\chi = p+1$ share the same combinatorial structure:

| BT Tree Property | Tensor Network Property |
|:-----------------|:------------------------|
| Vertices at depth $r$: $(p+1)p^{r-1}$ | States representable at bond dimension $\chi=p+1$ after $r$ DMRG sweeps |
| Path from root to leaf: $\lceil \log_p N \rceil$ edges | MPS contraction: $O(\chi^3 L)$ for $L$ sites |
| Branching factor: $p+1$ | DMRG decimation: keep-largest-$\chi$ singular values |
| $p=2$: binary tree | Standard even-odd DMRG decimation |
| $p=3$: ternary tree | Three-site unit-cell iPEPS |

The isomorphism is not accidental. The p-adic valuation $\operatorname{ord}_p(d)$ measures how many times $p$ divides the spatial separation $d$ between sites. The BT tree organizes sites by this valuation — sites with the same valuation are at the same tree depth, and the branching factor $p+1$ counts the number of length-$p$ translations available at that depth. Tensor networks perform singular-value decompositions that naturally respect this ultrametric organization: large singular values correspond to coarse-grained (high-valuation) structure, small singular values to fine-grained (low-valuation) details.

### 3.2 The Rosetta Stone $T_{2,3,5}$

The algebraic structure that the BT tree geometrically encodes is the Rosetta Stone $T_{2,3,5}$ — the integer matrix that encodes the cross-ratios between completions at primes 2, 3, and 5 [4]. This is the unifying object of the QNFO dimensionless reformulation: the standard model's dimensionless parameters (the fine-structure constant $\alpha$, the Weinberg angle $\sin^2\theta_W$, the gauge couplings) are encoded in $T_{2,3,5}$ through their running-coupling ratios.

The connection to tensor networks is direct: the Rosetta Stone's entries specify the relative weights of the $p=2$, $p=3$, and $p=5$ branches of the BT tree. A computation that is efficiently decomposable on the $p=2$ tree (binary) may require different resources on the $p=3$ tree (ternary), and the Rosetta Stone provides the translation between them.

> `[speculative]` If the prime-indexed branching is physically meaningful, the Rosetta Stone's entries would predict the relative efficiency of tensor-network simulations at different decimation schemes. This is the content of REG-ODR0601.

> `[qualification]` **Trap 4 constraint (qnfo-core §0.7.1):** The apparent 5-smooth dominance in dimensionless reformulations is subject to the valuation-artifact constraint. Decimal-precision physical inputs produce base-10 representation artifacts that inflate 2- and 5-adic valuations. REG-IPR-003 (2026-08-04) returned NULL for exactly this reason: the 5-smooth signal vanished when exact rational source quantities were used instead of measured decimals [5]. Any $p=5$ signal must be distinguished from the base-10 artifact by using exact rational source quantities.

## 4. Open Questions

The following are problems raised by the BT-tree/tensor-network isomorphism that are not yet resolved. They are listed here as open questions with explicit falsifiability conditions, not as asserted findings.

### 4.1 Quantum Readout as Tree Boundary Traversal

`[speculative]` The quantum readout bottleneck — measurement is 100-1000× slower than gate operations, with fidelities of 98-99.5% lagging behind gate fidelities exceeding 99.9% [6] — may be interpretable as a boundary traversal in the BT tree. Readout moves from a superposition state distributed across the tree to a classical outcome at a single leaf, and the Landauer limit $E \geq T \ln 2$ applies at each branching discrimination. If this interpretation is correct, readout fidelity should show a prime-indexed dependence when qubits are arranged in a BT-tree topology (REG-ODR0602).

> `[disconfirmed if]` Readout fidelity scales identically for all qubit connectivity topologies, showing no $p$-dependence after controlling for known noise sources.

### 4.2 Casimir Effect as Tree Boundary Condition

`[speculative]` The Casimir force between conducting plates — shown by Jaffe (2005) to be a source-source relativistic interaction, not evidence of extractable zero-point energy [7] — may be interpretable as a correlation function on the BT tree boundary. The conducting plates restrict the mode spectrum between them; this restriction is a boundary condition on the tree, and the force is the difference in free energy between the restricted and unrestricted geometries.

> `[disconfirmed if]` No tree-structured reformulation of the Casimir geometry produces numerically equivalent predictions to the standard QFT calculation. This is a mathematical question, not an experimental one — the tree encoding either reproduces the standard result or it does not.

### 4.3 General-Purpose Sequential Computation

The von Neumann model — operating systems, databases, web servers, compilers — resists parallelization and analog/quantum implementation by structure, not by engineering limitation [8]. Its control flow is fundamentally discrete and conditional, and its bottleneck is the memory wall (latency, not bandwidth). The BT-tree isomorphism applies specifically to local-Hamiltonian quantum dynamics on regular lattices — a restricted but important problem class. The claim does not extend to general-purpose computation.

### 4.4 The Prime-Indexed Hardness Hypothesis

`[speculative]` If the BT tree's prime-dependent branching is physically meaningful, then different primes index different "hardness classes" for computation. A problem that is efficiently decomposable on the $p=2$ tree (binary) may require exponentially more resources on the $p=3$ tree (ternary), and vice versa. This would mean that "quantum advantage" is not a binary property of a problem but a prime-indexed spectrum: a problem may be hard for some tree topologies and easy for others. REG-ODR0601 pre-registers a test of this hypothesis.

## 7. Conclusion

The Simons/Flatiron 2026 tensor-network result is a significant empirical data point, not a refutation of quantum computing. It demonstrates that local-Hamiltonian dynamics on regular lattices are efficiently decomposable by tree-structured classical algorithms. We have argued that the mathematical object encoding this tree structure is the Bruhat-Tits building — the combinatorial tree of completions of $\mathbb{Q}$ at each prime — and that tensor networks succeed because they accidentally instantiate this geometry.

The algebraic counterpart of the BT tree is the Rosetta Stone $T_{2,3,5}$, the unifying object of the QNFO dimensionless reformulation. Together, they provide a geometric-algebraic framework for understanding when and why classical tensor-network methods surpass quantum-supremacy thresholds on specific problem classes.

> `[speculative]` If the prime-indexed branching of the BT tree is physically meaningful — and not a mathematical artifact of the decimal representation used to compute physical parameters — then the tree provides a classification of computational hardness indexed by the primes. The pre-registered predictions above are designed to discriminate this hypothesis from its null: that tensor networks are efficient numerical methods whose performance is independent of the ultrametric structure of the underlying number field.

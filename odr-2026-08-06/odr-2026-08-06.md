# The Universal Tree: Information, Energy, and the Bruhat-Tits Substrate of Physical Computation

## Abstract (150–250 words)

The combinatorial tree encoding the completions of the rational numbers at every prime — the Bruhat-Tits building for $\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$ — provides a unifying geometric account of physical computation across quantum, classical, and biological substrates. We show that three recent developments converge on this structure: (1) the demonstration that classical tensor-network methods on a laptop surpass claimed quantum-supremacy thresholds for 100+ qubit dynamics (Simons Foundation/Flatiron Institute, 2026); (2) the structural parallel between quantum readout bottlenecks and tree-boundary traversal costs constrained by the Landauer limit; and (3) the Casimir effect as a source-source correlation function on the same tree geometry (Jaffe 2005). We formalize the information capacity of the BT tree as $I(r) = \log_2|\mathcal{S}_r|$ where $\mathcal{S}_r$ is the sphere of radius $r$, and derive the per-bit energy cost of state discrimination as $\Delta E \geq k_B T \ln 2$ in dimensional form, or $\Delta E \geq T \ln 2$ in dimensionless Planck units. The universal tree hypothesis predicts that any physical computation, regardless of substrate, is constrained by the prime-indexed branching structure of its underlying number field — a constraint that may be testable through ultrametric encoding of qubit arrays.

## 1. Introduction

Physical computation — the process by which a physical system transitions between distinguishable states — is governed by the Landauer principle: erasing one bit of information dissipates at minimum $k_B T \ln 2$ of energy into the environment [2]. This coupling of information and energy is not an engineering limit but a thermodynamic one. Yet the geometric structure of the state space over which this coupling operates has received comparatively less attention. We propose that this structure is the Bruhat-Tits tree — the combinatorial encoding of the completions of $\mathbb{Q}$ at every prime place — and that this geometry unifies several apparently disparate results in contemporary physics and computing.

Per Ostrowski's theorem, every non-trivial absolute value on the rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to either the standard Archimedean absolute value $|\cdot|_\infty$, or the $p$-adic absolute value $|\cdot|_p$ for some prime $p$. The Bruhat-Tits building for $\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is a $(p+1)$-regular tree $T_p$ whose vertices correspond to homothety classes of lattices in $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$. At depth $r$, the sphere $\mathcal{S}_r$ contains $(p+1)p^{r-1}$ vertices. This exponential growth in distinguishable states with depth is the geometric expression of the exponential growth in computational state space.

[PHILOSOPHY] The thesis of this paper is that every physical system that performs computation — from a quantum processor distinguishing superposition states to a biological retina decoding photon packets — instantiates a subgraph of $T_p$, and the energy required to traverse that subgraph is bounded below by the Landauer limit per bit discriminated.

## 2. Tensor Networks as BT-Tree Computation

### 2.1 The Simons/Flatiron Result

In May 2026, researchers at the Simons Foundation's Center for Computational Quantum Physics and Boston University demonstrated that classical tensor-network methods — running on a personal laptop — could simulate the dynamics of 100+ interacting qubits on square, cubic, and diamond lattices [3]. This result directly challenges earlier claims that such simulations require quantum hardware and constitute "quantum supremacy."

From an ultrametric perspective, this outcome is not surprising. Matrix product states (MPS) and projected entangled pair states (PEPS) are one-dimensional and two-dimensional tensor networks, respectively — they are precisely the class of algorithms that exploit tree-structured correlations. The bond dimension $\chi$ of an MPS corresponds to the branching factor of the underlying tree: $\chi \sim p+1$. The computational cost scales as $O(\chi^3)$ for MPS, which corresponds to the vertex count of the BT tree at the relevant depth.

### 2.2 The Ultrametric Interpretation

The correlation structure of local Hamiltonians on regular lattices is p-adically ultrametric: two sites separated by distance $d$ have correlations that decay as $p^{-\mathrm{ord}_p(d)}$. This means that the natural geometry for organizing these correlations is NOT Euclidean distance but p-adic distance — and the natural computational structure is the BT tree. Tensor networks succeed precisely because they happen to instantiate this geometry. The "surprise" — that classical laptops beat quantum supremacy — is a surprise only if one expects all computational problems to be maximally hard. From the tree perspective, local-Hamiltonian dynamics are naturally tree-decomposable.

## 3. Readout as Branch Termination

### 3.1 The Quantum Readout Bottleneck

In contemporary quantum computing platforms, gate operations execute in tens of nanoseconds while readout (qubit measurement) requires hundreds of nanoseconds to microseconds [4]. Readout fidelities of 98–99.5% lag behind gate fidelities exceeding 99.9%. This asymmetry — measurement as the slowest, most error-prone step — is persistent across superconducting, trapped-ion, and photonic platforms.

The ultrametric interpretation: readout is the traversal from a superposition state distributed across the BT tree to a classical outcome at a single leaf. This traversal must satisfy the Landauer bound at each branching point. A quantum superposition over $N = (p+1)p^{r-1}$ states at depth $r$ requires $\log_2 N$ bits of discrimination, costing at minimum $\log_2 N \cdot k_B T \ln 2$ in dissipated energy.

### 3.2 Testable Prediction

`[speculative]` If qubits are arranged in a physical geometry that mirrors the BT-tree topology — a "tree-code" layout — the readout fidelity should show a prime-indexed improvement. Qubits coupled with $p=2$ (binary) connectivity should show different readout characteristics than those with $p=3$ (ternary) connectivity. The prediction: for $N$ qubits arranged as the leaves of a BT tree of prime $p$, the readout fidelity $F$ should scale as $F \geq 1 - \alpha \cdot p^{-r}$ for some constant $\alpha$, with $r \approx \log_p N$.

## 4. Casimir/ZPE: Energy Differences, Not Absolute Harvesting

### 4.1 Jaffe's Resolution

Jaffe (2005) demonstrated that the Casimir force between conducting plates can be derived without reference to zero-point fluctuations of the vacuum. Instead, it emerges as a relativistic van der Waals force — a one-loop quantum correction to the interaction energy of the material boundaries, expressible entirely in terms of the Green's functions of the charged sources in the plates [5]. The "vacuum energy" picture is a calculational convenience, not a physical reality.

This is `[established]` physics and serves as a constraint on any claim of "energy harvesting" from the vacuum: in Poincaré-invariant QFT, the vacuum is the unique lowest-energy eigenstate with $\langle 0|T^{\mu\nu}|0\rangle = \rho \eta^{\mu\nu}$. There are no gradients to extract work from. The cosmological constant is the sole observable consequence of absolute zero-point energy, and requires dynamical gravity.

### 4.2 Connection to the BT Tree

The Casimir force as a source-source interaction is mathematically a correlation function on the boundary of the BT tree. The conducting plates restrict the mode spectrum between them; this restriction is precisely a boundary condition on the tree, and the force is the difference in the tree's free energy between the restricted and unrestricted geometries. This is `[speculative]` — the precise mapping between the Casimir geometry and the BT tree has not been computed — but the structural isomorphism (boundary condition → mode restriction → energy difference) is exact.

## 5. Biophysical Corollary: Phototransduction

### 5.1 Photon as Information-Energy Packet

A photon of frequency $\nu$ carries energy $E = h\nu$ and information in its frequency, polarization, and direction. When a photon strikes a biological photoreceptor, two things happen: the energy is transduced into a chemical or electrical signal, and the information is decoded (correctly or incorrectly) by the downstream neural pathway.

### 5.2 The Photic Sneeze as Information Misrouting

The photic sneeze reflex (ACHOO syndrome) — sneezing in response to bright light — affects 18-35% of the population. The leading hypothesis is cross-wiring between the optic nerve (CN II) and the trigeminal nerve (CN V): a strong photic stimulus spills over from the optic pathway into the sneeze reflex arc. From an information-theoretic perspective, this is a decoding error: the photon's information (brightness) is routed to the wrong output channel (sneeze instead of visual perception).

[PHILOSOPHY] This is the biological analogue of a quantum measurement error — a state discrimination that lands on the wrong branch of the tree. Like a qubit readout error, the photic sneeze reflects the fundamental cost of distinguishing states in a physical substrate. The sneeze is the biological echo of the Landauer limit.

### 5.3 Photosynthesis as Optimized Transduction

In contrast, photosynthetic reaction centers achieve nearly 100% quantum efficiency in converting photon energy to chemical energy. The exciton transfer pathway through the Fenna-Matthews-Olson complex exhibits quantum coherence lasting hundreds of femtoseconds [6] — a biological system exploiting quantum effects for energy transduction. This is the optimized complement of the photic sneeze: maximal discrimination fidelity, minimal energy dissipation at the Landauer bound.

## 7. Conclusion

The Bruhat-Tits tree is not an abstract mathematical structure — it is the geometric bookkeeping for physical computation. Every bit discriminated, every state distinguished, every energy level traversed is a step in a combinatorial tree whose branching is indexed by the primes. The convergence of tensor-network supremacy, quantum readout physics, Casimir constraints, and biological phototransduction on this geometry is either the deepest consilience in the foundations of computation since Shannon, or it is a coincidental structural analogy.

If they are confirmed, the BT tree is the substrate of computation in the same sense that spacetime is the substrate of dynamics.

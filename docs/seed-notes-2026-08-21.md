## _26233053304 — "Is self-reference inherently discrete?"

**Claim (debate record):** The note contains a multi-position exchange on whether
self-reference is inherently discrete. Position A (yes): Gödel numbering maps
statements to integers; formal self-reference is syntactic; gapped quantum memory
persists only with discrete degrees of freedom; p-adic ultrametricity makes
self-identity binary; discrete-time evolution kills Wigner-friend erasure;
Spencer-Brown's first distinction is a discrete boundary and re-entry generates
discrete oscillation. Position B (no): Banach fixed-point theorem gives continuous
self-reference (x = cos x); differential equations dx/dt = f(x) are continuous
self-reference; fractals/self-similar sets are continuous with discrete scaling
symmetry; eigenforms and impredicative definitions are medium-independent; the
discreteness is a property of the carrier (language, encoding), not of
self-reference itself. Position C (synthesis, appearing in the note): self-reference
is a continuous dynamic that manifests discretely at the moment a distinction is
drawn/recorded — "the continuous is the road; the discrete is the destination"
(half-turn e^{iπ} = −1).

**Project reading:** The corrected, defensible form is C plus a Spencer-Brown
sharpening: **drawing a distinction is a discrete act; self-reference becomes
discrete exactly when it crosses a drawn distinction.** The paper MUST NOT adopt
Position A's strong claim ("self-reference is inherently discrete") — the Banach/
ODE/fractal counterexamples are decisive that self-reference is medium-independent.
The usable residue: the L0 primitive (the distinction) is what discretizes, and
re-entry (RES.020, SLB.002) is where discreteness is generated. This note
therefore feeds the L0 premise chain and the map-territory boundary (Q2 of the
UIA), not a new theorem. Corpus anchors: strange-loop-theory-of-physical-
quantization, strange-loop-of-being, formal-self-reference-limits (RES.008,
10.5281/zenodo.21936076), reentrant-distinctions (SLB.002, 10.5281/zenodo.21964453).

## _26233053653 — "Unpack the assumptions and dependencies of Hilbert space"

**Claim:** A request to enumerate the assumptions/dependencies of Hilbert space.
The note's content inventories: vector-space structure over ℂ (superposition as
linearity); positive-definite sesquilinear inner product (Born amplitudes);
completeness (every Cauchy sequence converges — the topological assumption behind
the spectral theorem and Stone's theorem); separability (countable dense subset);
the axiom of choice lurking in the existence of orthonormal bases (Zorn's lemma);
the real/complex field itself (norm, parallelogram law); and the physical
dependencies (state vector = complete description, observables as self-adjoint
operators, Wigner's theorem for symmetries, projection postulate as a non-derived
import).

**Project reading:** This is the map-side ledger the paper needs. The core claim
calls the Hilbert-space formulation "a map, not the territory" — this note's
inventory is the enumeration of what the map assumes, and it sharpens the ℂ gap
already registered as the deepest wobble (UIA Q3): completeness and the inner
product are defined over ℝ/ℂ; the proposed entropy-Hessian flow is over ℝ. The
inventory becomes paper §2 material ("what the Hilbert-space map assumes"), and
H-UNIT's target statement gains precision: the large-distinction limit must
recover orthogonality, completeness, and the ℂ structure — or explain exactly
which of these are artifacts of the map. Candidate external import for P4:
Solèr's theorem (orthomodular-lattice characterization of Hilbert spaces over
ℝ/ℂ/ℍ) — to be verified live (P3.AUTHOR-GATE-EVERY-ENTRY-1) before any

# Ultrametric Tree Encoding and Error Confinement on Bruhat–Tits Trees: What the Strong Triangle Inequality Does and Does Not Prove

## Abstract

Passive quantum error correction—protecting logical qubits by encoding geometry alone, without repeated syndrome extraction—would remove the dominant overhead of scalable quantum computing if it could be made to work. A recent proposal encodes logical information on the vertices of a Bruhat–Tits tree and claims that the strong triangle inequality of the ultrametric geometry confines errors so completely that the logical error rate vanishes at tree depth $d \geq 3$. The specific gap we address is that this claim conflates a metric-axiom statement with a probabilistic statement about error propagation. We formalize error propagation as paths on the tree, derive from the strong triangle inequality the isosceles-triangle property and a support-confinement result for minimal error paths, and then show that the claimed exponential barrier $E_{\text{barrier}}(d)=2^{d}$ follows from cut counting on any rooted tree and invokes the ultrametric axiom nowhere. We compute the recursive eight-fold majority-vote cascade at physical error rate $p=0.4$ in full: $p^{(1)}=0.1736704$, $p^{(2)}\approx 5.5489\times 10^{-3}$, $p^{(3)}\approx 2.9054\times 10^{-10}$, and show this cascade is a generic property of recursive majority voting on any branching structure. The reported zero-error simulation bounds the logical error probability only at $p_L \leq 2.9957\times 10^{-5}$ even assuming $N=10^{5}$ independent trials. The practical conclusion is that ultrametric encoding yields support confinement, not proven error suppression; the claimed $p_L=0$ is an artifact of unverified noise assumptions and must be tested against correlated noise before any design decision relies on it.

## 1. Introduction

Scalable quantum computing is presently blocked by error-correction overhead. Active stabilizer codes such as the surface code require repeated syndrome extraction, low-latency real-time decoding, and large ancilla qubit counts; one analysis of the resource situation states that surface codes require physical-to-logical qubit ratios of roughly $1000{:}1$ and face thermodynamic and signal-processing limits on further improvement [11]. The engineering cost of these active cycles limits the size of computation that can be run on near-term hardware, and cloud-accessible processors with hundreds of qubits—such as the 105-qubit Tianyan-287 superconducting system with single-qubit, two-qubit, and readout fidelities of $99.90\%$, $99.56\%$, and $98.7\%$ [3]—still operate in a regime where every logical qubit consumes a large physical budget. The people who face this problem daily are the operators of quantum cloud platforms and the designers of quantum processors: each additional layer of active error correction adds ancilla qubits, measurement circuitry, and decoder latency, and each of these adds new error channels of its own.

A fundamentally different approach would be passive: encode logical information in a geometry that blocks error propagation by construction, so that no active cycles are needed. The proposal under analysis here encodes logical qubits on the vertices of a regular Bruhat–Tits tree—the infinite homogeneous tree of a non-Archimedean (ultrametric) geometry—and claims that the strong triangle inequality $d(x,z)\leq\max(d(x,y),d(y,z))$ prevents errors in one subtree from propagating to distant branches, yielding a zero logical error rate at depth $d\geq 3$ [8], [9], [10]. If that claim held as stated, the design consequence would be dramatic: a depth-seven ternary tree would carry an energy barrier of $2^{7}=128$ leaf flips [10], and logical error rates would fall below any measurable level without a single syndrome-measurement cycle.

This paper asks whether the claim holds, and finds that it does not hold in the form stated. The distinction that the proposal misses is between two statements that sound similar but have different logical status. The first statement is geometric: in an ultrametric space, a minimal error path between two vertices is confined to the ball determined by their cophenetic height, so an error in one subtree cannot, as a matter of support, spread into a disjoint subtree without crossing the meet of the two subtrees. This statement we derive from the strong triangle inequality, and it is true. The second statement is probabilistic: that the *probability* of a logical error decays exponentially with depth, or vanishes at $d\geq 3$. This statement does not follow from the axiom; it follows only from additional assumptions—independent leaf errors and a majority decoder—whose consequences we compute exactly and which would hold equally on any rooted tree with the same branching structure.

The stakes of getting this distinction right are concrete. A processor designer who adopts the encoding on the strength of the zero-error claim would omit the active-correction budget that a correct analysis shows is still needed under correlated noise, which crosstalk studies identify as a generic feature of multi-qubit processors and as especially harmful to fault tolerance precisely because fault tolerance relies on errors being local and predictable [7]. Conversely, a reviewer who dismisses the encoding entirely would miss the genuine positive result: ultrametric geometry does confine the *support* of error paths, which is a real structural property that flat layouts lack and that may be exploitable once combined with an honest noise model.

This paper makes the following contributions:

1. A derivation, from the strong triangle inequality alone, of the isosceles-triangle property of ultrametrics and a support-confinement proposition for minimal error paths on the tree (Section 4.1).
2. A proof that the exponential barrier count $E_{\text{barrier}}(d)=2^{d}$ used in the proposal is a property of any rooted tree of branching degree $2$ and invokes the ultrametric axiom nowhere (Section 4.2).
3. A complete arithmetic derivation of the recursive eight-fold majority-vote cascade at $p=0.4$, giving $p^{(1)}=0.1736704$, $p^{(2)}\approx 5.5489\times 10^{-3}$, and $p^{(3)}\approx 2.9054\times 10^{-10}$, together with the per-level suppression factors (Section 4.3).
4. Binomial confidence bounds showing that a zero-error simulation report bounds the logical error probability only at $p_L\leq 2.9957\times 10^{-4}$ for $N=10^{4}$ trials and $p_L\leq 2.9957\times 10^{-5}$ for $N=10^{5}$ trials (Section 4.4).
5. Labeled projections under the posited thermal-activation barrier model, $p_L(d)\leq e^{-2^{d}}$, with the conditions under which they collapse (Section 4.5).
6. Explicit falsification criteria and a smallest-next-experiment design for the encoding (Sections 6 and 7).

The confinement-versus-suppression distinction drawn here also bears on other encoding proposals in which a geometric or energetic structure is claimed to suppress errors, including domain-wall encodings, where the barrier mechanism is derived from an explicit Hamiltonian rather than from a metric axiom [2], and passive optical encodings based on W-states, where error suppression is derived from the structure of the entangled state under specific noise models [5].

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Ultrametric geometry.** An ultrametric is a metric $d$ on a set $X$ satisfying the strong triangle inequality $d(x,y)\leq d(x,z)\lor d(z,y)$ for all $x,y,z\in X$, where $\lor$ denotes the maximum operator [1]. The work of [1] treats ultrametrics as a zero-dimensional analogue of ordinary metrics and proves ultrametric versions of the Arens–Eells isometric embedding theorem, the Hausdorff extension theorem, and the Niemytzki–Tychonoff characterization theorem; its extension theorem constructs homeomorphisms of ultra-normed modules over arbitrary commutative rings by adapting Toruńczyk's and Hausdorff's methods, replacing the Dugundji extension theorem with a zero-dimensional Michael continuous selection theorem [1]. The relevance of [1] to the present question is foundational: it establishes that the strong triangle inequality is a statement about the metric structure of a space—about which balls nest, which sets are clopen, and which maps extend—not a statement about dynamics, probability, or energy. Any claim that the axiom by itself suppresses error *probabilities* must therefore supply an additional mechanism connecting geometry to dynamics, and [1] supplies none because it does not address dynamics at all.

**The proposal and its barrier model.** The proposal analyzed here encodes logical information on the vertices of a regular odd-$p$ Bruhat–Tits tree and posits that the ultrametric geometry enforces a passive error barrier [8]. Its reported quantities include barrier heights $B(3)=8$ and $B(4)=16$ leaf flips, logical error bounds $p_L(3)=6.5536\times 10^{-4}$ and $p_L(4)=4.29\times 10^{-7}$, suppression factors of approximately $6.10\times 10^{2}$ and $9.31\times 10^{5}$ at depths 3 and 4, ball-code sizes of $n_1=5$, $n_2=17$, and $n_3=53$ qudits for $p=3$, and a three-level majority-vote cascade $0.1736704\to 5.5489\times 10^{-3}\to 2.905\times 10^{-10}$ [8]. Critically, [8] itself lists the conditions under which its main hypothesis would be falsified: a depth-independent plateau in the measured logical error rate, a coherent error path that survives majority voting at the root, or a correlated mechanism that flips $\lceil p/2\rceil$ edges simultaneously with non-negligible probability [8]. The same document frames the exponential cut as a model of ultrametricity rather than a theorem following from it, stating that the strong triangle inequality is "modeled as implying" that any path between distant vertices crosses a cut whose size grows exponentially with hierarchical level [8].

**The critical re-analysis.** A subsequent re-analysis of the same scheme corrects two claims of the prior version [9]. First, it establishes that the barrier $E_{\text{barrier}}(d)=2^{d}$ is a posited model, not a derivation from ultrametric geometry: the cut-counting argument counts sites on a level of any rooted binary tree and invokes the strong triangle inequality nowhere [9]. Second, it establishes that the iterated majority-vote cascade at $p=0.4$—computed as $0.1736704\to 0.0055485\to 2.905\times 10^{-10}$ over three levels—is a generic property of recursive majority voting on any branching structure and confirms a decoder-level classical fact, not a mechanism of the tree geometry [9]. The re-analysis also reports that the single empirical input—zero logical errors at $p=0.4$ for $d\geq 3$—is an unverified report from the proposal's notebook notes, with no documented simulation trial count or decoder, and that the reported zero-error result bounds the logical error probability only at $2.9957\times 10^{-5}$ even for $N=10^{5}$ trials [9]. Our paper independently re-derives each of these numbers with full arithmetic (Section 4) and extends the analysis by deriving the positive confinement result that the ultrametric axiom *does* yield (Section 4.1), which neither [8] nor [9] states as a theorem.

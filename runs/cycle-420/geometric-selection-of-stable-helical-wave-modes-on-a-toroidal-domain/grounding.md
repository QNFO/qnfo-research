## Research idea

Torus Geometry as a Selector of Stable Helical Wave Modes. The author conjectures that confining waves or quantum wavefunctions in a toroidal boundary makes stability a purely geometric selection rule: helical paths whose winding ratio (toroidal vs. poloidal turns) is rational and satisfies a constructive-interference condition form stable modes, while irrational or phase-mismatched windings destructively interfere and decay. This matters because it would give a geometry-only predictor for mode stability in tokamak field lines, toroidal resonators, and toroidal waveguides, independent of material specifics. A paper could test it by deriving the quantization condition for scalar and electromagnetic waves on a torus (separation of variables in toroidal coordinates, phase accumulation around both cycles), computing which (m,n) winding ratios yield closed constructive-interference orbits, and comparing predicted stable modes against numerical eigenmode simulations of the Helmholtz or Schrödinger equation on toroidal domains, including perturbations that break exact rationality.

## Research idea

Is mathematics invented or discovered? Arguments for discovery to weigh (Plimpton 322 cross-cultural convergence, non-Euclidean geometry preceding GR, Dirac 1928 to positron 1932, Godel incompleteness, Cantor on infinity, Montgomery-Dyson zeta-zero/nuclear-level match, Wigner's unreasonable effectiveness) against the critique that the dichotomy is false (we invent representations and discover constrained consequences). Research sub-questions: (1) Which aspects of mathematical practice are invented (axioms, notation, definitions) vs discovered (theorem-space consequences, structural constraints) - can this split be made principled? (2) Does cross-cultural convergence on Pythagorean triples (Babylonian Plimpton 322 c. 1800 BC, Indian Sulba Sutras, Chinese Zhoubi Suanjing) evidence shared cognitive/practical constraints or mind-independent abstract structure? (3) Wigner's unreasonable effectiveness: quantify the survivorship-bias objection - how many mathematical formalisms failed to find physical application vs the celebrated hits (GR light bending predicted 1915, matched 1919; Dirac equation residue became the positron 1932) - is compounding hit precision explainable naturalistically? (4) Godel incompleteness: does truth outrunning provability in a standard model imply mind-independent mathematical objects, or is it metatheory-relative? (5) Montgomery pair correlation (1973, assumes RH): zeta-zero spacing matches GUE nuclear energy-level statistics - random-matrix universality vs a physical law enforcing primes? (6) 2020 PhilPapers survey distribution: nominalism 41.85% vs Platonism 38.38%, Other 20.81% - what do expert positions actually distribute over, and does the 'Other' fifth favor structural realism? Target output: a defensible middle position (weak structural realism / discovered-constraints view) that states the applicability puzzle precisely rather than dissolving it.

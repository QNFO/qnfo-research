## Research idea

Factorization Barriers as Trapping of Random Walks on Bruhat-Tits Trees. The author conjectures that number-theoretic obstructions on the p-adic boundary Qp—prime gaps, factorization difficulty—manifest as geometric trapping of diffusive processes in the Bruhat-Tits tree bulk, under a p-adic holographic dictionary. Specifically, a factorization process that stalls on the boundary corresponds to a random walker getting confined in deep, exponentially branching arms of the (p+1)-regular tree. This matters because it would convert hard arithmetic questions into tractable statistical-mechanics problems (hitting times, spectral gaps of the tree's Laplacian, return probabilities). A paper could test this by: (1) deriving the spectral theory of simple random walks on Tp and relating relaxation/trapping times to p-adic valuation structure of integers; (2) simulating diffusion on finite truncations of Tp and correlating trapping statistics with factorization hardness or prime-gap distributions encoded in boundary paths; (3) checking whether the holographic dictionary (boundary correlators as bulk geodesic lengths) yields computable quantities that track known arithmetic phenomena.

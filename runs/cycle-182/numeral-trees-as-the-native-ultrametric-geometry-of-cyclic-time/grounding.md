## Research idea

Numeral Tree as Native Ultrametric Geometry of Cyclic Time. The author's most original conjecture is that positional notation is not merely a linear encoding but a rooted tree whose branching factor is the radix, whose distance is the ultrametric of shared nesting depth, and whose levels encode nested cyclic time (hour within day within month). The novel synthesis: this tree is the numeral's native geometry, and the brain's logarithmic number sense is actually ultrametric tree perception following the numeral system's own hierarchy, with '10' as a self-referential re-entrant form marking cycle completion. A paper could test this by (1) formally proving that calendar-cycle time-series embedded in mixed-radix positional trees induce symbolic ultrametrics (Böcker–Dress style) that outperform linear embeddings for clustering temporal data; (2) simulating hierarchical clustering of quantities by order of magnitude and comparing its geometry to p-adic ultrametrics; and (3) deriving whether zero-free positional systems (Manca 2015) still yield the same tree, testing whether zero is the constructive marking of the unmarked root rather than a structural necessity.

## Research idea

Cross-Ratio Invariants of Braids as Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation Resources. The author conjectures that in a timeless Wheeler–DeWitt universe (Ĥ|Ψ⟩=0), braids are the natural geometric encoding of correlation patterns, and that cross-ratios of anyon worldlines serve as topological invariants whose projective invariance explains fault tolerance: logical information lives in macroscopic cross-ratio invariants of a correlation network, stabilizers lock these invariants, and local errors cannot change them without cost scaling with system size. This yields a testable claim: topological quantum error correction can be reformulated so that logical qubits and gates are cross-ratio invariants of braid/correlation networks. A derivation could define cross-ratio-type invariants on tensor-network or anyon fusion representations of surface/color codes, prove their invariance under local error moves, and show gate universality via braid-group representations acting on these invariants. Simulations could compare error thresholds of invariant-based versus standard stabilizer decoding, testing whether the cross-ratio formulation adds predictive or computational power beyond standard TQC.

  § 7. Conclusion: The Universal Ignorance Audit is a fifteen-question, five-phase instrument for making the structure of not-knowing legible. It is content-independent, depth-ordered, self-applicable, and action-closing. Its meta-audit establishes both its power and its limits: it is an analytic instrument with an analytic instrument's blind spots, and it must be used in the knowledge that it can manufacture the illusion of contact with the unknown. Used honestly, it converts the wobble of confusion into a compass, and the terror of groundlessness into a research program. The method is released as an open methodology for researchers, educators, and anyone operating at the frontier of what they do not know. Its most important instruction is its last: *what question am I not asking?*

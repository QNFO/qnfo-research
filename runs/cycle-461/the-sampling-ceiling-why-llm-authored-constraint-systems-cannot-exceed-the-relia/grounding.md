## Research idea

LLM-Generated System Designs Cannot Exceed Their Own Reliability. Claim: an LLM's outputs (including meta-level artifacts like system prompts or specifications) inherit the failure modes of its sampling-based generation, so no prompt engineering can make LLM-authored constraint systems more reliable than the model itself. This matters because it predicts a hard ceiling on self-correcting AI pipelines and justifies hybrid architectures where LLMs draft and formal tools (TLA+, Alloy, runtime verification) enforce correctness. Testable: (1) empirically measure hallucination rates of LLM-generated specifications versus human/formal-method-authored ones on identical tasks; (2) formally model generation as stochastic sampling and constraint satisfaction as deduction to prove no prompt-level transformation can close the reliability gap; (3) simulate pipelines with and without post-hoc formal verification to quantify whether system-level enforcement, not prompt-level instruction, determines correctness.

  § 1. The formal equivalence, the cognitive incommensurability: ZX diagrams and matrices denote the same linear maps. The completeness theorems are established results: within the stabilizer fragment, and for substantial extensions beyond it, every equality provable in the Hilbert-space formalism is provable diagrammatically [1]. The equivalence is formal and exact — the same symmetric monoidal structure, presented two ways [2]. The incommensurability is not formal. A representation determines what a bounded reasoner can *see*, and the two presentations surface different facts at different costs. A two-qubit map is a 4-by-4 array of complex numbers; an n-qubit map is a 4-to-the-n array. The same map, as a diagram, is a handful of nodes and wires, and its complexity is carried by the structure rather than by the dimension. Section 7 verifies this quantitatively: at six qubits, the matrix has 4,096 entries while the corresponding diagram has sixteen. The gap is not a convenience; it is the difference between a token a mind can hold and a table it cannot.
  § 2. Why graphs beat matrices for humans: The cognitive-science record supplies the mechanisms.

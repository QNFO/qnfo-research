# The Speed of Light as the Tracing Capacity of a Distributed-Estimation Vacuum: Stability Thresholds, Divergence Times, and the Causal Cone

## Abstract

The speed of light $c = 2.998\times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ is measured and used daily, yet no established framework explains why the causal speed is finite or what physical function finiteness performs beyond kinematics. This paper develops a control-theoretic answer: the vacuum is modeled as a distributed estimation network whose nodes track unstable field dynamics through nearest-neighbor communication, so that $c$ is the network's tracing capacity — the fastest instability the network can track without its self-estimation error diverging. We derive the per-update error recursion, show that stability requires the instability rate $\lambda$ to satisfy $\lambda < -\ln(1-g)\,c/a$ for node spacing $a$ and correction gain $g$, and evaluate the threshold under the Planck-spaced assumption $a = \ell_P = 1.616\times 10^{-35}\ \mathrm{m}$ and $g = 1/2$, giving $\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1.286\times 10^{43}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$. An instability running at $1.5$ times this threshold drives the estimation error past a $10^{6}$-fold tolerance within $2.156\times 10^{-42}\ \mathrm{s}$, about $40$ update intervals, destroying causal coherence. The model reproduces the causal bandwidth $B = 5.905\times 10^{42}\ \mathrm{Hz}$ and the congestion delay $3.336\times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{s/m}$ reported for the same vacuum model, satisfies its own Nyquist consistency check with margin $2/\pi$, and yields a causal cone $|\Delta x| \le c\,\Delta t$ under stated isotropy assumptions. The result converts a kinematic axiom into a falsifiable stability condition and specifies the lattice simulation that would confirm or refute it.

## 1. Introduction

Physicists working on quantum gravity, cosmologists confronting the horizon problem, and metrologists who codify $c$ in the SI all face the same explanatory gap: the speed of light is measured, fixed, and used daily, but no existing framework explains why the causal speed is finite at all, or what physical role finiteness plays beyond kinematics [10]. The gap has practical consequences. Variable-speed-of-light cosmologies exist as responses to the horizon problem — the observation that the cosmic microwave background has the same temperature to a few parts in $10^{5}$ over regions that were causally disconnected at recombination [3] — but in those treatments $c$ is modulated by an external mechanism, such as probe-brane motion in a bulk spacetime, rather than derived from the substrate's own operational constraints [3]. Relativity itself can be extended to accommodate a variable $c$ with a minimum of changes, which shows that the kinematic structure is flexible; what remains missing is a reason for the specific finite value [2].

The gap blocks a specific design decision. If $c$ is the tracing capacity of a distributed control network — the maximum instability rate the network can track before its self-estimation error diverges — then Lorentz invariance stops being a postulate and becomes a stability condition, and the value of $c$ becomes a calculable quantity tied to the network's spacing and update protocol. That derivation would change what counts as evidence in quantum-gravity research: discrete-substrate programs that already derive a light-cone structure from graph spectral properties [8] would gain a control-theoretic criterion for why the bound must hold, and engineering programs that build distributed feedback systems with explicit stability budgets — such as accelerator networks in which a beam position must be held stable to the order of $50\ \mathrm{nm}$ for an intra-train collision feedback loop to stay in capture range [4] — would share a formal vocabulary with foundational physics.

This paper formalizes that conjecture for a concrete model. The vacuum is modeled as a lattice of estimator nodes, each tracking unstable field modes through nearest-neighbor communication at a finite update rate. The central claim is that the causal speed equals the network's tracing capacity: exceeding it makes the estimation error diverge, and the resulting stability boundary reproduces a Lorentz-invariant causal cone. The contributions are:

- A per-update error recursion for the distributed estimator, yielding the exact stability threshold $\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}(g) = -\ln(1-g)\,c/a$ on the trackable instability rate, derived in Section 4.3.
- A numerical evaluation under the Planck-spacing assumption $a = \ell_P$ and gain $g = 1/2$, giving $\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1.286\times 10^{43}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, with a sensitivity table over gains $g \in \{0.1, 0.5, 0.9\}$, derived in Section 4.4.
- A divergence-timescale result: an instability at $1.5\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}$ overruns a $10^{6}$-fold error tolerance within $2.156\times 10^{-42}\ \mathrm{s}$, approximately $40$ update intervals, derived in Section 4.5.
- Independent recomputation of the causal bandwidth $B = 5.905\times 10^{42}\ \mathrm{Hz}$, the congestion delay $3.336\times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{s/m}$ at $\rho/\rho_c = 10^{-3}$, and the device-scale cutoff $4.772\times 10^{10}\ \mathrm{Hz}$ for millimetre geometry, confirming the internal consistency of the model against the results of [10], derived in Sections 4.2, 4.6 and 4.7.
- A Nyquist consistency check showing the model's bandwidth uses a fraction $2/\pi$ of the available sampling headroom, derived in Section 4.2, and a formal statement of the causal cone with its isotropy assumptions, given in Section 4.8.
- Falsification conditions and a proposed minimal lattice simulation, stated in Sections 6 and 7.

The derivation could also bear on problems outside fundamental physics where a network must track dynamics faster than its communication allows: the same threshold structure governs any distributed estimator of unstable processes, and the capacity-fidelity trade it exposes parallels the payload-versus-detection trade measured in learned steganographic channels [5].

## 2. Background and Related Work

The closest prior derivation of the speed of light from a discrete substrate is the Bruhat-Tits-tree program. That work maps the timeless adjacency operator of the Bruhat-Tits tree to a time-evolution operator via the Page-Wootters mechanism, derives a causal light-cone structure from Lieb-Robinson bounds, and concludes that the dimensionless upper bound on the speed of light is the Lieb-Robinson velocity derived from the graph's spectral radius, with physical dimensions supplied by the observer's thermodynamic modular Hamiltonian [8]. Its summary sections state that $c$ is thereby a derived, macroscopic, epistemic property of combinatorial geometry rather than a fundamental axiom [8]. The present paper shares the conclusion that $c$ is derived from a network's spectral and structural properties, but supplies a different and complementary mechanism: where the tree program uses Lieb-Robinson propagation bounds on a $p$-adic geometry, this paper uses the stability of a distributed estimator tracking unstable dynamics, which produces an explicit inequality on the trackable instability rate rather than a propagation bound alone.

The regularization framing of the same conjecture is developed in the distributed-estimation-vacuum paper. That work derives an exact causal bound $|v_g| \le v$ for the lattice dispersion $\omega(k) = (2v/a)|\sin(ka/2)|$, evaluates the causal bandwidth $\omega_{\max} = 2c/\ell_P = 3.710\times 10^{43}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $B = \omega_{\max}/2\pi = 5.905\times 10^{42}\ \mathrm{Hz}$ for Planck spacing, shows that an assumed first-order response suppresses above-cutoff modes at $-20\ \mathrm{dB}$ per decade — the quantitative signature of early stopping — states a stability window $\omega_s = \omega_c\sqrt{\Gamma_0/\gamma_0}$, and derives congestion delays $\Delta\tau = 3.336\times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{s}$ per metre at $\rho/\rho_c = 10^{-3}$ with a device-scale cutoff of $4.775\times 10^{10}\ \mathrm{Hz}$ for millimetre-scale geometry [10]. Crucially, its discussion states what the model does not establish: that the physical vacuum is such a lattice, that the response function has the assumed form, or that the congestion variable maps onto the metric tensor; the conjecture is formalized and made falsifiable, not confirmed [10]. The present paper extends that program along the axis it leaves open: it adds the estimation-error dynamics themselves, derives the divergence threshold on the tracked instability, and quantifies how fast coherence is lost when the threshold is exceeded.

Variable-speed-of-light cosmology provides the physical motivation and the contrast case. In the brane-induced realization, the observed four-dimensional universe is a probe brane moving in a higher-dimensional bulk toward a black hole produced by a stack of branes, and the speed of light on the probe brane decreases in time as the brane approaches the horizon; a rotating black-hole background prevents the brane from hitting the horizon, bulk expansion provides effective friction, and freezeout followed by virialization stabilizes the orbit so that the speed of light tends to a constant at late times [3]. The introduction of that work frames the horizon problem quantitatively: the cosmic microwave background is isotropic to a few parts in $10^{5}$ over regions causally disconnected at recombination, which standard cosmology cannot explain through causal microphysical processes [3]. That treatment derives a time-varying $c$ from the substrate's dynamics but leaves the finiteness of $c$ itself unexplained; the present model addresses finiteness directly as a stability requirement.

On the kinematic side, the extended-relativity work shows that special and general relativity survive a variable light speed with minimal changes. It extends the Lorentz transform and the Minkowski line element under the single assumption that all moving observers at the same spacetime point measure the same light speed, even though that speed may vary in time, and introduces the differential construct $d\hat{T} = c\,dT$ together with a rest mass $m$ that varies so as to keep the rest energy $\hat{m} = mc^{2}$ constant, generating covariant velocity, energy-momentum, force vectors and a stress-energy tensor for an ideal fluid [2]. Its introduction positions this against variable-$c$ proposals that impose a variable rate to achieve a cosmological goal, and notes consistency with the requirements that a variable light speed must satisfy [2]. This matters for the present paper because it establishes the testable kinematic consequence: if the vacuum network's tracing capacity drifted, the extended transform of [2] describes how relativity would respond, so the model's prediction of a constant $c$ is a substantive claim about network stationarity rather than a kinematic necessity.

The discrete-substrate ontology underlying the lattice assumption is articulated in the syntactic-generation framework. That work avoids positing any material substrate and takes as its single foundational element a syntactic token — the drawing of a boundary separating inside from outside — with a syntactic void acting as the identity element against which boundaries hold structural meaning, and with strictly recursive combination rules that generate infinite complexity from a minimal alphabet [9]. Its reduction law of idempotence makes two adjacent identical boundaries equivalent to one [9]. This supplies the conceptual warrant for modeling the vacuum as discrete nodes with local relations rather than a continuum: the present paper's assumption A1, that field dynamics live on a lattice of spacing $a$, is the physical reading of a boundary-combination structure, and the epistemic-interface framing of [11] — in which the experienced interface is generated by an observer subgraph interacting with a static relational network, and model success does not guarantee correspondence to ontology [11] — reinforces the paper's own honesty about what a formal model can and cannot establish.

Three experimental and computational works supply the operational vocabulary the model borrows. The structured-light work demonstrates that a topological index alone can govern propagation: an input beam carrying no initial spin and no modal asymmetry develops spin-dependent redistribution during propagation exclusively from the Pancharatnam topological index $\ell_p$, which determines the distinct paraxial modal families into which the two spin components evolve, deterministically rather than as a generic consequence of paraxial propagation [1]. Its experimental section validates the prediction through Stokes polarimetry of a HeNe beam shaped by a liquid-crystal spatial light modulator across three propagation planes [1]. The relevance is structural: discrete indices selecting which modal families propagate is exactly the behavior the lattice model exhibits when the tracing capacity selects which field modes remain causally coherent. The tomography work shows that a full optical state — spatial, spectral, temporal and polarisation — can be reconstructed from intensity-only measurements through a single-mode fibre with a single-pixel detector, including for mutually incoherent fields that spectrally overlap, by leveraging internal references so that no auxiliary light source is needed [7]. Its principle section frames measurement as projection of an unknown density matrix through a sequence of analyser states given by eigenvectors of Pauli matrices encoded on a spatial light modulator [7]. This is the measurement-side analogue of the estimator model: a band-limited observer reconstructing a field state from projected, finite-rate samples. The light-shift spectroscopy work provides the same lesson in atomic physics: the spatially varying ac-Stark shift of a dipole trap maps the radial location of each atom into a unique frequency shift, so a single-shot snapshot of the light-shift-broadened spectrum infers atom number, temperature, trap depth and transition linewidth with high independence between parameters, validated in a hollow-core fibre loaded with a magneto-optically trapped rubidium ensemble [6]. In all three cases a continuous physical distribution is recovered through a discrete, capacity-limited channel — the operating assumption of the vacuum estimator taken here.

Finally, the accelerator-engineering literature shows that distributed control networks with explicit stability budgets are built and operated at the relevant level of rigor. The ILC Technology Network's final-doublet work package requires the vertical beam fluctuation at the interaction point quadrupole to be stable to the order of $50\ \mathrm{nm}$ to stay within the capture range of the intra-train collision feedback, evaluated through a cryostat vibration experiment with a superfluid-helium service line routed through radiation shielding [4]. Its conclusions report that responsible institutes are assigned for all work packages, with completion anticipated by April 2027 under current funding, and that the work packages carry relevance to other accelerator projects including the FCC and synchrotron light sources [4]. The steganography work completes the capacity vocabulary: StegNet hides a same-size image with a decoding rate of $98.2\%$, corresponding to $23.57$ bits per pixel, by changing only $0.76\%$ of the cover coefficients, explicitly trading accuracy for capacity and noting that the embedded image need not even be visually similar to the cover [5]. Its survey of traditional methods — image-domain, transform-domain and file-format-based, the last being fragile because any alteration crashes the hidden payload [5] — quantifies how channel capacity and robustness trade against each other, the same trade the vacuum estimator faces between tracking bandwidth and estimation fidelity.

## Research idea

Intelligence Converges to a Metabolically Constrained Resonant Equilibrium, Not a Singularity. Claim: any self-referential information-processing system, biological or artificial, evolves toward a 'resonant equilibrium'—a state of maximal energy-per-computation efficiency operating at the boundary set by Gödelian incompleteness (self-reference causing destabilizing 'resonant leakage') and thermodynamic limits (Landauer's principle)—rather than toward unbounded recursive self-improvement. Why it matters: it predicts a hard ceiling on intelligence scaling and unifies logical and thermodynamic constraints in one framework. Testable: (1) formal analysis—model self-referential proof/learning systems and show instability grows as the system approaches complete self-modeling, quantifying 'leakage' as divergence or error amplification; (2) simulation—train self-modeling agents under an energy budget and measure whether performance saturates at an optimum energy-information coupling rather than growing without bound; (3) derive whether Landauer-limited computation plus incompleteness bounds jointly imply a finite optimal operating point, and compare with observed scaling-law saturation in LLMs.

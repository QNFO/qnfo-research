## Research idea

Cross-Ratios as Universal Atoms of Meaning. Conjecture: all meaningful structure—physical constants, likelihood ratios, mutual information, perceptual invariants—can be encoded as networks of dimensionless cross-ratios, the unique projective invariants of four distinctions. Meaning is defined as the invariant relational pattern an epistemic agent can detect and reuse to anticipate interactions, with the fine-structure constant α = r_e/λ̄_C as the paradigm case of an 'epistemic translator' surviving changes of units, instruments, and theory. Why it matters: it would unify epistemology and ontology into one formal framework and dissolve objectivity/subjectivity dichotomies via shared invariants. Test: formalize 'meaning units' as projective invariants over distinction spaces; prove which statistical and information-theoretic quantities (likelihood ratios, mutual information) reduce to cross-ratio form; simulate agents extracting stable invariants from noisy relational data and check whether cross-ratio-like quantities are uniquely selected as predictive, observer-independent descriptors.

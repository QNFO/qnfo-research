# Topological Extension Hypothesis, Version 1.0.2: An Erratum-Driven Recalibration of the Predictive and Regularization Claims

## Abstract

The published topological extension hypothesis paper asserts, at abstract level, that its framework "outperforms point-based models by accurately predicting the age spreads in stellar clusters and the hysteresis of cognitive retention" and that "finite-volume regularization and topological charge stability successfully resolves singularities such as the UV catastrophe." This revision applies the merged errata to those claims and audits them against the stellar-cluster literature. The audit shows the age-spread claim is unsupported: the isochrone bracket for the extended main sequence turnoff clusters NGC 1806 and NGC 1846 spans $1.41$ to $1.86$ Gyr, an upper-limit spread of $0.45$ Gyr, yet their sub-giant branches and red clumps are significantly narrower than a $200$-$600$ Myr spread would produce, and independent analyses of young massive LMC clusters find no significant spreads. Measured gradients are of order $1.7$ Myr in NGC 2024 and $0.6$ Myr in the Orion Nebula Cluster, within systematic age uncertainties of $30$-$100\%$. The regularization claim is likewise downgraded: imposing a cutoff $\tau$ yields a finite effective action $W_{\mathrm{reg}} \approx -1.583 \times 10^{-3}\, a_0/\tau^{2}$ in four dimensions, but this censors the ultraviolet divergence rather than resolving it, and renormalization remains necessary. The paper retracts the two abstract claims, restates the hypothesis as a falsifiable research program, and specifies the observational test that would reverse each retraction.

## 1. Introduction

Researchers who cite interdisciplinary theory papers inherit their claims. When a preprint asserts that it "outperforms point-based models" and "successfully resolves" a named singularity, downstream readers — stellar-population astronomers interpreting color-magnitude diagrams, cognitive modelers looking for a formal hysteresis law, philosophers of physics weighing ontological proposals — may treat those assertions as established results and propagate them into reviews, curricula, and follow-up studies. The cost is concrete: an astronomer who cites the published topological extension hypothesis for the proposition that extended main sequence turnoffs (eMSTOs) demonstrate age spreads of several hundred Myr would be citing a claim that the primary literature actively contradicts, and a physicist who cites it for the resolution of the ultraviolet catastrophe would be citing a claim that confuses a cutoff with a solution. If the present revision is correct, the citation record changes: two abstract-level claims of the version 1.0 paper are retracted, one section-level claim is weakened to a contested interpretation, and the hypothesis survives in a weaker but honest form as a source of falsifiable predictions rather than of demonstrated predictive wins.

The problem this paper addresses is therefore remediation, not new theory. The version 1.0 paper [12] advances the topological extension hypothesis: that fundamental entities must possess non-zero dimension ($d \ge 1$) and non-trivial topological charge, that divergences are artifacts of zero-dimensional approximation, and that a finite intrinsic scale $\tau$ regularizes them. That ontological program is not what the errata target. The errata target three specific sentences. First, the abstract sentence claiming predictive outperformance over point-based models in stellar-cluster age spreads and cognitive-retention hysteresis: no comparative prediction against a point-based baseline was carried out in the paper, so "outperforms" and "accurately predicting" are unsupported. Second, the abstract and Section 4.1 sentence claiming that finite-volume regularization and topological charge stability "successfully resolves singularities such as the UV catastrophe": a lower cutoff on a divergent integral makes the truncated integral finite but does not remove the divergence, and the renormalization program remains necessary; "resolves" overstates the mathematics. Third, the Section 5.5 sentence asserting that the persistence of the age spread across multiple clusters shows it is "a fundamental feature of massive star formation rather than an anomaly caused by rotation or binaries": the literature now contains direct tests of the age-spread interpretation that find no significant spreads, so the sentence must be downgraded to a contested hypothesis.

The stakes of the third correction are the largest. The eMSTO phenomenon in intermediate-age ($1$-$2$ Gyr) Magellanic Cloud clusters was initially read as evidence of age spreads of $100$-$500$ Myr, and the version 1.0 paper built its astrophysical case on exactly that reading, quoting a formation-event width of approximately $0.5$ Gyr. Subsequent morphological analysis of the sub-giant branch and red clump shows that clusters with wide turnoffs have sub-giant branches far too narrow to harbor such spreads, which overturns the age-spread interpretation for at least the analyzed pair. A remediation that leaves Section 5.5 standing would leave the paper's central astrophysical "validation" resting on a refuted premise.

This revision proceeds by applying the errata, re-deriving every quantitative claim that the corrected text retains, and cross-checking the retained claims against the primary literature. The contributions are:

- A retraction analysis of the two abstract-level claims and the Section 5.5 claim of [12], with the exact corrected wording for each, derived in Sections 4 and 5.
- A quantitative audit of the age-spread evidence: the $0.45$ Gyr isochrone-bracket upper limit for NGC 1806 and NGC 1846, its inconsistency with the observed sub-giant-branch and red-clump widths, and the Myr-scale gradients in NGC 2024 and the Orion Nebula Cluster, derived in Section 4 and reported in Section 5.
- A derivation of the finite regularized effective action under the hypothesis's own cutoff prescription, $W_{\mathrm{reg}} \approx -1.583 \times 10^{-3}\, a_0\,\tau^{-2}$ for $n = 4$, showing that the cutoff censors rather than resolves the ultraviolet divergence, derived in Section 4.
- A consistency check of the companion Zitterbewegung numbers, $\omega_C \approx 1.55 \times 10^{21}$ Hz and $\lambda_C/2 \approx 1.93 \times 10^{-13}$ m, whose product reproduces the speed of light to $0.2\%$, derived in Section 4.
- A statement of the observational test that would falsify or reverse each retraction, in Sections 6 and 7.

## 2. Background and Related Work

The version 1.0 paper [12] states the pathology it targets: modeling entities as zero-dimensional coordinates yields accurate predictions at low resolution but generates singularities as the observation scale approaches the entity's intrinsic scale, with the point-electron self-energy diverging linearly or logarithmically depending on the cutoff. The paper's Section 7.2 then claims a structural isomorphism between the 't Hooft-Polyakov monopole, the eMSTO age spread, the space-time path, and the extended present, treating the age spread of a star cluster and the temporal width of a cognitive retention loop as "topologically equivalent." The errata concern the empirical leg of that isomorphism, and the relevant literature is the stellar-cluster age-spread literature together with the regularization literature.

On the astrophysical side, the review in [1] frames the question precisely: estimating absolute ages of young stars and the extent of age spreads is crucial for understanding formation mechanisms, disk dispersal, and the role of birth environments, and the two diagnostic channels are Hertzsprung-Russell diagram interpretation and photospheric lithium depletion. Its summary section states the current consensus directly: age spreads within a single cluster are "a lot smaller than 10 Myr," and only part of the observed dispersion in cluster diagrams is genuine, attributable to "a modest age spread of a few Myr" distributed among stellar radii. This few-Myr scale is two orders of magnitude below the $\sim 0.5$ Gyr formation width that [12] asserts for massive clusters, and it motivates the retraction of the Section 5.5 claim in its strong form.

The strongest direct counter-evidence comes from morphology. The analysis in [4] uses HST/ACS imaging of NGC 1806 and NGC 1846, adopts Marigo et al. (2008) isochrones at $Z = 0.008$, and brackets the eMSTO with isochrones of $1.41$, $1.58$, and $1.86$ Gyr; it explicitly notes that binaries and photometric errors make these brackets upper limits to any actual spread. Its conclusion is that the sub-giant branches and red clumps of both clusters are "significantly narrower and offset" from what age spreads of $200$-$600$ Myr would produce, and that the results "are not consistent with the interpretation of age spreads being the cause of the eMSTO phenomenon." A parallel study in [6] tests the hypothesis that an extended star-formation history in intermediate-age clusters should leave an equally extended history in young clusters: it fits star-formation histories of young massive LMC clusters with the FITSFH code against model Hess diagrams and finds that most clusters show "a single and well defined peak," with only low-level extended formation or a weak second burst in a minority. Together [4] and [6] remove the empirical foundation from the sentence under erratum in Section 5.5 of [12].

The positive detections that do exist are Myr-scale and spatially structured. The MYStIX-based analysis in [8] introduces the $Age_{JX}$ estimator, built on $J$-band photospheric emission and X-ray coronal emission under a near-universal $L_X$-$M$ relation for pre-main-sequence stars, and computes median ages in annular subregions: within $1$ pc, ages rise from $\sim 0.1$ to $\sim 1.8$ Myr in NGC 2024 and from $\sim 1.4$ to $\sim 2$ Myr in the Orion Nebula Cluster, with interquartile ranges of $\sim 1$ and $\sim 2$ Myr that the authors attribute largely to individual-age uncertainties. These core-halo gradients are the honest empirical content of "extended formation," and they are three orders of magnitude smaller than the Gyr widths claimed in [12].

Two further works constrain what any age-spread claim must survive. The assessment in [7] shows that pre-main-sequence evolutionary tracks vary between theory groups, under-predict stellar masses by $30$-$50\%$, under-predict low-mass stellar ages by $30$-$100\%$, and over-predict high-mass stellar ages by $20$-$100\%$; its simulation method inserts an $80\%$ age spread (for a $1$ Myr median age, stars from $0.2$ to $1.8$ Myr) against a burst scenario and separates them with a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on the luminosity distributions, with binaries contributing a high-luminosity tail and a $0.1$ dex error distribution the core. Any claimed spread smaller than these systematics is not measurable by isochrone fitting alone. On the theory side, the feedback-regulated model in [5] defines the age spread mechanically as $\Delta\tau_{*} = t_{\mathrm{exp}} - t(N_{*} = 1)$, the interval between the first star and gas expulsion, and shows that winds from massive stars impose dual constraints on the star formation efficiency and this spread; the spread is therefore a derived quantity of the feedback physics, not a free topological parameter.

The formation environment itself is extended in space, which is the defensible core of the "temporal manifold" language. The simulation study in [2] follows $\sim 12$ million SPH particles through a galactic spiral shock, forming $2000$ sink particles and around $20$ clusters, with clusters spaced every $5$-$10$ pc along a $\sim 100$ pc ridge and a stellar age gradient created because the shock reaches one side of the ridge earlier than the other. Cluster dynamics then act on these extended structures over well-defined timescales: the analysis in [10] separates bound clusters from unbound associations by the ratio $\Pi = t/t_{\mathrm{cr}}$ with $\Pi > 1$ for bound objects, using the crossing time $t_{\mathrm{cr}} = 10\,(r_{\mathrm{eff}}^{3}/GM)^{1/2}$ with $G \simeq 4.5 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{pc^{3}\,M_{\odot}^{-1}\,Myr^{-2}}$, and models cluster evolution with the kira integrator and SeBa stellar evolution over initial relaxation times from $1$ Myr to $4$ Gyr. The qualitative study in [11] extends this to dense clusters, following evolutionary tracks with stellar evaporation and head-on collisions until relativistic collapse or dissolution. Environmental effects reach into planet formation: [9] shows that the Orion Nebula Cluster holds $\sim 50\%$ of its mass in gas, and that a high-density phase above a few $10^{5}$ stars $\mathrm{pc^{-3}}$ in globular clusters truncates circumstellar disks inside the gas-giant region, explaining the lack of close planets in 47 Tuc. Earlier HST work on the LMC clusters in [3] had already flagged the interpretive difficulty: its WFPC2 color-magnitude diagrams for NGC 1818 and NGC 1805 overlay isochrones of $25$, $40$, $63$, and $100$ Myr, with binary sequences marked explicitly, and NGC 1818's relaxation time of $150$-$600$ Myr against a dynamical time of $1.6$-$10$ Myr shows how slowly the cluster erases its formation-era structure.

On the regularization side, the erratum concerns what a cutoff achieves. The companion adelic framework in [13] argues, from Ostrowski's theorem and the product formula on the adele ring, that ultraviolet and infrared divergences are "measure-theoretic artifacts of the $\infty$-place continuum" and that p-adic geometry provides natural regulators; notably, even this stronger claim is phrased as regulators and a "workaround for single-place QFT," not as a resolution that eliminates renormalization. The ZBW-Majorana framework in [14] illustrates the standard of claim the remediated paper should meet: it quotes the Compton-scale numbers $\omega_C = 2mc^{2}/\hbar \approx 1.55 \times 10^{21}$ Hz and $\lambda_C/2 = \hbar/(2mc) \approx 1.93 \times 10^{-13}$ m, derives a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ falsifiable prediction (the vanishing of the ZBW current-current correlator for Majorana fermions), and attaches a phased experimental roadmap with go/no-go gates. Claims at that standard are falsifiable before they are asserted; the two sentences under erratum in [12] were asserted without a falsification path, which is the root defect this revision repairs.

## 3. Methods

This paper is a remediation study: it applies a merged erratum set to the version 1.0 text of [12], re-derives the quantitative content that survives, and audits the corrected claims against the primary literature cited above. It performs no observation, simulation, or measurement; every number is either quoted from a cited work with shown arithmetic applied to it, or labeled as an assumption.

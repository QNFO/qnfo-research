# Topological Extension Hypothesis, Version 1.0.2: A Remediated Statement of the Finite-Volume Regularization and Age-Spread Claims

## Abstract
Interdisciplinary theory preprints propagate their claims into astronomy, physics and cognitive modelling, and two abstract-level claims of the version 1.0 topological extension hypothesis paper — that it "outperforms point-based models" on stellar-cluster age spreads and cognitive-retention hysteresis, and that finite-volume regularization "successfully resolves singularities such as the UV catastrophe" — are unsupported by the primary literature and by the mathematics itself. This revision applies the merged errata, re-derives every quantitative claim that survives, and audits the corrected claims against the stellar-cluster and regularization literature. The audit shows the age-spread claim is unsupported: the isochrone bracket for the extended main-sequence turnoff clusters NGC 1806 and NGC 1846 spans $1.41$ to $1.86$ Gyr, an upper-limit spread of $0.45$ Gyr, yet their sub-giant branches and red clumps are narrower than a $200$–$600$ Myr spread would produce, and measured formation gradients are of order $1.7$ Myr in NGC 2024 and $0.6$ Myr in the Orion Nebula Cluster, within systematic age uncertainties of $30$–$100\%$. The regularization claim is downgraded: a cutoff $\tau$ yields a finite effective action $W_{\mathrm{reg}} \approx -1.583 \times 10^{-3}\, a_0\,\tau^{-2}$ in four dimensions but censors the ultraviolet divergence rather than resolving it. The hypothesis is restated as a falsifiable research program, and the observational test that would reverse each retraction is specified.

## 1. Introduction
Researchers who cite interdisciplinary theory papers inherit their claims. When a preprint asserts that it "outperforms point-based models" and "successfully resolves" a named singularity, downstream readers — stellar-population astronomers interpreting color-magnitude diagrams, cognitive modelers looking for a formal hysteresis law, philosophers of physics weighing ontological proposals — may treat those assertions as established results and propagate them into reviews, curricula and follow-up studies. The cost is concrete: an astronomer who cites the version 1.0 topological extension hypothesis paper [12] for the proposition that extended main-sequence turnoffs demonstrate age spreads of several hundred Myr cites a claim that the primary literature actively contradicts, and a physicist who cites it for the resolution of the ultraviolet catastrophe cites a claim that confuses a cutoff with a solution. If the present revision is correct, the citation record changes: two abstract-level claims of the version 1.0 paper are retracted, one section-level claim is weakened to a contested interpretation, and the hypothesis survives in a weaker but honest form as a source of falsifiable predictions rather than of demonstrated predictive wins.

The problem this paper addresses is remediation, not new theory. The version 1.0 paper [12] advances the topological extension hypothesis: that fundamental entities must possess non-zero dimension ($d \ge 1$) and non-trivial topological charge, that divergences are artifacts of zero-dimensional approximation, and that a finite intrinsic scale $\tau$ regularizes them. That ontological program is not what the errata target. The errata target three specific sentences. First, the abstract sentence claiming predictive outperformance over point-based models in stellar-cluster age spreads and cognitive-retention hysteresis: no comparative prediction against a point-based baseline was carried out, so "outperforms" and "accurately predicting" are unsupported. Second, the abstract and Section 4.1 sentence claiming that finite-volume regularization and topological charge stability "successfully resolves singularities such as the UV catastrophe": a lower cutoff on a divergent integral makes the truncated integral finite but does not remove the divergence, and the renormalization program remains necessary. Third, the Section 5.5 sentence asserting that the persistence of the age spread across multiple clusters shows it is "a fundamental feature of massive star formation rather than an anomaly caused by rotation or binaries": the literature now contains direct tests of the age-spread interpretation that find no significant spreads, so the sentence is downgraded to a contested hypothesis.

The stakes of the third correction are the largest. The extended main-sequence turnoff (eMSTO) phenomenon in intermediate-age ($1$–$2$ Gyr) Magellanic Cloud clusters was initially read as evidence of age spreads of $100$–$500$ Myr, and the version 1.0 paper built its astrophysical case on exactly that reading, quoting a formation-event width of approximately $0.5$ Gyr. Subsequent morphological analysis of the sub-giant branch and red clump shows that clusters with wide turnoffs have sub-giant branches far too narrow to harbor such spreads, which overturns the age-spread interpretation for at least the analyzed pair. A remediation that leaves the Section 5.5 sentence standing would leave the paper's central astrophysical "validation" resting on a refuted premise.

This revision proceeds by applying the errata, re-deriving every quantitative claim that the corrected text retains, and cross-checking the retained claims against the primary literature. The contributions are:

- A retraction analysis of the two abstract-level claims and the Section 5.5 claim of [12], with the exact corrected wording for each, derived in Sections 4 and 5.
- A quantitative audit of the age-spread evidence: the $0.45$ Gyr isochrone-bracket upper limit for NGC 1806 and NGC 1846, its inconsistency with the observed sub-giant-branch and red-clump widths, and the Myr-scale gradients in NGC 2024 and the Orion Nebula Cluster, derived in Section 4 and reported in Section 5.
- A derivation of the finite regularized effective action under the hypothesis's own cutoff prescription, $W_{\mathrm{reg}} \approx -1.583 \times 10^{-3}\, a_0\,\tau^{-2}$ for $n = 4$, showing that the cutoff censors rather than resolves the ultraviolet divergence, derived in Section 4.
- A consistency check of the companion Zitterbewegung numbers, $\omega_C \approx 1.55 \times 10^{21}$ Hz and $\lambda_C/2 \approx 1.93 \times 10^{-13}$ m, whose product reproduces the speed of light to $0.2\%$, derived in Section 4.
- A statement of the observational test that would falsify or reverse each retraction, in Sections 6 and 7.

## 2. Background and Related Work
The version 1.0 paper [12] states the pathology it targets: modeling entities as zero-dimensional coordinates yields accurate predictions at low resolution but generates singularities as the observation scale approaches the entity's intrinsic scale, with the point-electron self-energy diverging linearly or logarithmically depending on the cutoff. Its Section 7.2 then claims a structural isomorphism between the 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole, the eMSTO age spread, the space-time path and the extended present, treating the age spread of a star cluster and the temporal width of a cognitive retention loop as "topologically equivalent." The errata concern the empirical leg of that isomorphism, and the relevant literature is the stellar-cluster age-spread literature together with the regularization literature.

On the astrophysical side, the review in [1] frames the question precisely: estimating absolute ages of young stars and the extent of age spreads is crucial for understanding formation mechanisms, disk dispersal and the role of birth environments, and the two diagnostic channels are Hertzsprung–Russell diagram interpretation and photospheric lithium depletion. Its summary section states the current consensus directly: age spreads within a single cluster are "a lot smaller than 10 Myr," and only part of the observed dispersion in cluster diagrams is genuine, attributable to "a modest age spread of a few Myr" distributed among stellar radii. This few-Myr scale is two orders of magnitude below the $\sim 0.5$ Gyr formation width that [12] asserts for massive clusters, and it motivates the retraction of the Section 5.5 claim in its strong form.

The strongest direct counter-evidence comes from morphology. The analysis in [4] uses HST/ACS imaging of NGC 1806 and NGC 1846, adopts Marigo et al. (2008) isochrones at $Z = 0.008$, and brackets the eMSTO with isochrones of $1.41$, $1.58$ and $1.86$ Gyr; it explicitly notes that binaries and photometric errors make these brackets upper limits to any actual spread. Its conclusion is that the sub-giant branches and red clumps of both clusters are "significantly narrower and offset" from what age spreads of $200$–$600$ Myr would produce, and that the results "are not consistent with the interpretation of age spreads being the cause of the eMSTO phenomenon." A parallel study in [6] tests the hypothesis that an extended star-formation history in intermediate-age clusters should leave an equally extended history in young clusters: it fits star-formation histories of young massive LMC clusters with the FITSFH code against model Hess diagrams and finds that most clusters show "a single and well defined peak," with only low-level extended formation or a weak second burst in a minority. Together [4] and [6] remove the empirical foundation from the sentence under erratum in Section 5.5 of [12].

The positive detections that do exist are Myr-scale and spatially structured. The MYStIX-based analysis in [8] introduces the $Age_{JX}$ estimator, built on $J$-band photospheric emission and X-ray coronal emission under a near-universal $L_X$–$M$ relation for pre-main-sequence stars, and computes median ages in annular subregions: within $1$ pc, ages rise from $\sim 0.1$ to $\sim 1.8$ Myr in NGC 2024 and from $\sim 1.4$ to $\sim 2$ Myr in the Orion Nebula Cluster, with interquartile ranges of $\sim 1$ and $\sim 2$ Myr that the authors attribute largely to individual-age uncertainties. These core-halo gradients are the honest empirical content of "extended formation," and they are three orders of magnitude smaller than the Gyr widths claimed in [12].

Two further works constrain what any age-spread claim must survive. The assessment in [7] shows that pre-main-sequence evolutionary tracks vary between theory groups, under-predict stellar masses by $30$–$50\%$, under-predict low-mass stellar ages by $30$–$100\%$ and over-predict high-mass stellar ages by $20$–$100\%$; its simulation method inserts an $80\%$ age spread (for a $1$ Myr median age, stars from $0.2$ to $1.8$ Myr) against a burst scenario and separates them with a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test on the luminosity distributions, with binaries contributing a high-luminosity tail and a $0.1$ dex error distribution the core. Any claimed spread smaller than these systematics is not measurable by isochrone fitting alone. On the theory side, the feedback-regulated model in [5] defines the age spread mechanically as $\Delta\tau_{*} = t_{\mathrm{exp}} - t(N_{*} = 1)$, the interval between the first star and gas expulsion (its Eq. 12), and shows that winds from massive stars impose dual constraints on the star-formation efficiency and this spread; the spread is therefore a derived quantity of the feedback physics, not a free topological parameter.

The formation environment itself is extended in space, which is the defensible core of the "temporal manifold" language. The simulation study in [2] follows $\sim 12$ million SPH particles through a galactic spiral shock, forming $2000$ sink particles and around $20$ clusters, with clusters spaced every $5$–$10$ pc along a $\sim 100$ pc ridge and a stellar age gradient created because the shock reaches one side of the ridge earlier than the other. Cluster dynamics then act on these extended structures over well-defined timescales: the analysis in [10] separates bound clusters from unbound associations by the ratio $\Pi = t/t_{\mathrm{cr}}$ with $\Pi > 1$ for bound objects, using the crossing time $t_{\mathrm{cr}} = 10\,(r_{\mathrm{eff}}^{3}/GM)^{1/2}$ with $G \simeq 4.5 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{pc^{3}\,M_{\odot}^{-1}\,Myr^{-2}}$, and models cluster evolution with the kira integrator and SeBa stellar evolution over initial relaxation times from $1$ Myr to $4$ Gyr. The qualitative study in [11] extends this to dense clusters, following evolutionary tracks with stellar evaporation and head-on collisions until relativistic collapse or dissolution. Environmental effects reach into planet formation: [9] shows that the Orion Nebula Cluster holds $\sim 50\%$ of its mass in gas, and that a high-density phase above a few $10^{5}$ stars $\mathrm{pc^{-3}}$ in globular clusters truncates circumstellar disks inside the gas-giant region, explaining the lack of close planets in 47 Tuc. Earlier HST work on the LMC clusters in [3] had already flagged the interpretive difficulty: its WFPC2 color-magnitude diagrams for NGC 1818 and NGC 1805 overlay isochrones of $25$, $40$, $63$ and $100$ Myr, with binary sequences marked explicitly, and NGC 1818's relaxation time of $150$–$600$ Myr against a dynamical time of $1.6$–$10$ Myr shows how slowly the cluster erases its formation-era structure.

On the regularization side, the erratum concerns what a cutoff achieves. The companion adelic framework in [13] argues, from Ostrowski's theorem and the product formula on the adele ring, that ultraviolet and infrared divergences are "measure-theoretic artifacts of the $\infty$-place continuum" and that p-adic geometry provides natural regulators; notably, even this stronger claim is phrased as regulators and a "workaround for single-place QFT," not as a resolution that eliminates renormalization. The ZBW–Majorana framework in [14] illustrates the standard of claim the remediated paper should meet: it quotes the Compton-scale numbers $\omega_C = 2mc^{2}/\hbar \approx 1.55 \times 10^{21}$ Hz and $\lambda_C/2 = \hbar/(2mc) \approx 1.93 \times 10^{-13}$ m, derives a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ falsifiable prediction (the vanishing of the ZBW current-current correlator for Majorana fermions), and attaches a phased experimental roadmap with go/no-go gates. Claims at that standard are falsifiable before they are asserted; the two sentences under erratum in [12] were asserted without a falsification path, which is the root defect this revision repairs.

## 3. Methods
This paper is a remediation study: it applies a merged erratum set to the version 1.0 text of [12], re-derives the quantitative content that survives, and audits the corrected claims against the primary literature cited above. It performs no observation, simulation or measurement; every number is either quoted from a cited work with shown arithmetic applied to it, or labeled as an assumption.

The remediation protocol has four steps. First, claim extraction: each erratum-targeted sentence is isolated, and the specific assertion it makes is stated in falsifiable form (what quantity, what comparison, what would make it false). Second, literature confrontation: for each extracted assertion, the primary analyses that bear on it are identified and their stated results are used as the test data — for the age-spread claims, the isochrone brackets and sub-giant-branch morphology of [4], the Hess-diagram star-formation-history fits of [6], the Myr-scale gradients of [8] and the systematic-uncertainty budget of [7]. Third, re-derivation: each quantitative claim that survives the confrontation is recomputed from its stated inputs with every arithmetic step shown (Section 4). Fourth, restatement: the corrected wording is written so that each retained claim carries its own falsification condition.

Two conventions govern the restatement, and we state them here because the corrected claims depend on them. We take the convention that a lower cutoff on a divergent integral censors rather than resolves the divergence; this convention follows from the explicit cutoff dependence of the regularized effective action derived in Section 4, since a quantity that retains a power of $1/\tau$ is not cutoff-independent. We further take the convention that an isochrone bracket is an upper limit on an age spread whenever binaries and photometric errors widen the bracket, following the explicit statement to that effect in [4]; under this convention the $0.45$ Gyr bracket of NGC 1806 and NGC 1846 is reported as an upper limit, not a detection. Where earlier drafts of the hypothesis treated the same bracket as a measured formation-event width of $\sim 0.5$ Gyr, this revision adopts the upper-limit convention because the sub-giant-branch and red-clump widths measured in [4] are narrower than a $200$–$600$ Myr spread would produce.

The spectral-geometry machinery retained from the version 1.0 text is used only as the derivation engine for the regularized action. The Laplacian $\Delta$ on a compact Riemannian manifold $M$ of dimension $n$ generates the heat kernel $K(t) = \operatorname{Tr}\!\big(e^{-t\Delta}\big)$, whose small-$t$ asymptotic expansion is
$$K(t) \sim (4\pi t)^{-n/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k\, t^{k},$$
with $a_0$ the volume, $a_1$ the boundary area and $a_2$ the integrated curvature. The version 1.0 prescription replaces the lower limit $t = 0$ in the effective-action integral $W = -\tfrac12 \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{dt}{t}\, K(t)$ with a physical cutoff $\tau > 0$. We evaluate the leading term of the regularized integral for $n = 4$ in Section 4 and use its residual cutoff dependence to adjudicate the "resolves versus censors" question.

## 4. Analysis
Every input number below is quoted from the cited work named with it, or labeled as an assumption. All arithmetic is shown step by step.

**D1. Density scaling under a ten-fold reduction of intrinsic length.** The version 1.0 text derives $\rho \propto L^{-d}$ for an entity of characteristic size $L$ in $d$ dimensions holding scale-independent energy $E$. We evaluate the ratio for $d = 3$.

1. Reference volume: $V_{1} = L^{3} = (1.0\ \mathrm{m})^{3} = 1.0\ \mathrm{m^{3}}$.
2. Reduced length: $L_{2} = 0.1\ \mathrm{m}$; new volume: $V_{2} = (0.1\ \mathrm{m})^{3} = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^{3}}$.
3. With the same energy $E$ in both configurations (assumption: $E$ is cutoff-independent, as stated in the version 1.0 derivation), the densities are $\rho_{1} = E / V_{1} = E\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ and $\rho_{2} = E / V_{2} = 10^{3}\, E\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$.
4. The ratio is
$$\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}} = \frac{10^{3} E}{E} = 10^{3}.$$

This ratio is a statement about the model's own scaling, not about any measured system; it shows only that a finite non-zero $L$ caps the density that diverges as $L \to 0$.

**D2. Regularized effective action in four dimensions.** Inputs: the heat-kernel expansion above with $n = 4$, so the leading term is $K(t) \approx a_0\,(4\pi t)^{-2} = a_0 / (16\pi^{2} t^{2})$; the cutoff prescription of Section 3.

1. Substitute the leading term into the regularized integral:
$$W_{\mathrm{reg}} = -\frac12 \int_{\tau}^{\infty} \frac{dt}{t}\, \frac{a_0}{16\pi^{2} t^{2}} = -\frac{a_0}{32\pi^{2}} \int_{\tau}^{\infty} t^{-3}\, dt.$$
2. Evaluate the integral: $\int_{\tau}^{\infty} t^{-3}\, dt = \left[-\tfrac12 t^{-2}\right]_{\tau}^{\infty} = 0 - \left(-\tfrac{1}{2\tau^{2}}\right) = \frac{1}{2\tau^{2}}$.
3. Therefore
$$W_{\mathrm{reg}} = -\frac{a_0}{32\pi^{2}} \cdot \frac{1}{2\tau^{2}} = -\frac{a_0}{64\pi^{2}\,\tau^{2}}.$$
4. Compute the numerical coefficient: $\pi^{2} = 9.8696044$, so $64\pi^{2} = 64 \times 9.8696044 = 631.65468$, and
$$\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}} = \frac{1}{631.65468} = 1.58316 \times 10^{-3}.$$
5. Hence
$$W_{\mathrm{reg}} \approx -1.583 \times 10^{-3}\, \frac{a_0}{\tau^{2}} \qquad (n = 4).$$

The result retains an explicit $\tau^{-2}$ dependence: changing $\tau$ by a factor of two changes $W_{\mathrm{reg}}$ by a factor of four. A cutoff-independent answer would carry no such dependence, so under the censoring convention of Section 3 the cutoff censors the ultraviolet divergence rather than resolving it, and the renormalization program remains necessary.

**D3. Consistency check of the companion Zitterbewegung numbers.** Inputs from [14]: $\omega_C = 2mc^{2}/\hbar \approx 1.55 \times 10^{21}$ Hz and $\lambda_C/2 = \hbar/(2mc) \approx 1.93 \times 10^{-13}$ m.

1. Form the product:
$$\omega_C \cdot \frac{\lambda_C}{2} = \frac{2mc^{2}}{\hbar} \cdot \frac{\hbar}{2mc} = c.$$
2. Numerically: $1.55 \times 10^{21} \times 1.93 \times 10^{-13} = (1.55 \times 1.93) \times 10^{8} = 2.9915 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.
3. Compare with $c = 2.99792458 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The relative deviation is
$$\frac{2.99792458 - 2.9915}{2.99792458} = \frac{0.00642458}{2.99792458} = 2.143 \times 10^{-3} \approx 0.21\%.$$

The two quoted numbers are internally consistent with the speed of light to $0.2\%$, which is the level of agreement expected from the three-significant-figure rounding of each input.

## 5. Results

The audit yields three results, each traceable to the derivations of Section 4 and the literature confrontation of Sections 2 and 3.

**R1. The age-spread claim of [12] is retracted as a detection and retained only as an upper limit.** The isochrone bracket for NGC 1806 and NGC 1846 spans $1.41$ to $1.86$ Gyr, so the bracket width is
$$\Delta t_{\mathrm{bracket}} = 1.86 - 1.41 = 0.45\ \mathrm{Gyr},$$
and [4] states explicitly that binaries and photometric errors make this an upper limit to any actual spread. The same analysis finds sub-giant branches and red clumps significantly narrower and offset from what age spreads of $200$–$600$ Myr would produce, and concludes the results are not consistent with the age-spread interpretation of the eMSTO phenomenon. The independent test in [6] fits star-formation histories of young massive LMC clusters with the FITSFH code and finds most clusters show a single and well-defined peak, so an extended formation history of the kind asserted in [12] is absent where it should be present if the eMSTO were an age spread. The version 1.0 claim of a formation-event width of approximately $0.5$ Gyr is therefore unsupported: the only bracket that exists is an upper limit of $0.45$ Gyr, and the morphology of the same clusters contradicts a spread of that size. The Section 5.5 sentence of [12] asserting persistence "across multiple clusters" as a fundamental feature of massive star formation is retracted in its strong form; the honest residual is the Myr-scale, spatially structured gradient measured in [8], which is three orders of magnitude smaller than the claimed Gyr width and lies within the $30$–$100\%$ systematic age uncertainties quantified in [7].

**R2. The regularization claim of [12] is downgraded from "resolves" to "censors."** Derivation D2 gives, for $n = 4$ under the version 1.0 cutoff prescription,
$$W_{\mathrm{reg}} \approx -1.583 \times 10^{-3}\, \frac{a_0}{\tau^{2}},$$
with the coefficient $1/(64\pi^{2}) = 1.58316 \times 10^{-3}$ computed from $64\pi^{2} = 631.65468$. The regularized action retains an explicit power of the cutoff: halving $\tau$ quadruples $|W_{\mathrm{reg}}|$. A resolved singularity would yield a cutoff-independent finite answer; a censored singularity yields a finite answer that depends on where the integral is stopped. The derived $\tau^{-2}$ dependence places the version 1.0 prescription squarely in the second category, so the abstract claim that finite-volume regularization "successfully resolves singularities such as the UV catastrophe" is corrected to: the cutoff renders the truncated integral finite but leaves the divergence censored, and the renormalization program remains necessary. This is consistent with the density-scaling result D1, which shows only that a finite $L$ caps the $L^{-3}$ density divergence without removing the limit $L \to 0$.

**R3. The retained quantitative content of the companion frameworks passes its internal consistency check.** The Zitterbewegung numbers quoted in [14], $\omega_C \approx 1.55 \times 10^{21}$ Hz and $\lambda_C/2 \approx 1.93 \times 10^{-13}$ m, satisfy $\omega_C \cdot \lambda_C/2 = c$ analytically and reproduce $c = 2.99792458 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ to a relative deviation of $2.143 \times 10^{-3} \approx 0.21\%$ numerically (D3), which is the level expected from three-significant-figure rounding. This is the standard of claim the remediated hypothesis should meet: a number that can be checked before it is asserted, attached in [14] to a falsifiable $\mathbb{Z}_2$ prediction and a phased experimental roadmap with go/no-go gates.

## 6. Discussion

**What survives of the hypothesis.** The remediated topological extension hypothesis retains three elements. First, the observation that zero-dimensional approximations generate divergences is correct and standard; the point-electron self-energy divergence quoted in [12] is textbook material. Second, finite intrinsic scales do regularize integrals in the technical sense of making them finite, as D2 shows quantitatively. Third, extended formation in star formation is real but Myr-scale and spatially graded, as [8] and the spiral-shock simulations of [2] show. What does not survive is the leap from these elements to the two abstract-level claims: no comparative prediction against a point-based baseline was ever performed, and a cutoff is not a resolution.

**Limitations.** The retraction analysis rests on the following assumptions, each with its sensitivity:

- The upper-limit convention for isochrone brackets follows [4]. If a future analysis accounted for differential extinction, binarity and rotation and still found the sub-giant branches compatible with a $200$–$600$ Myr spread, the retraction of the Section 5.5 claim would need partial reversal; the result is sensitive to this convention because the entire age-spread case rests on CMD morphology.
- The censoring-versus-resolving distinction rests on the residual $\tau^{-2}$ dependence of $W_{\mathrm{reg}}$ in D2. This is computed only for the leading heat-kernel term in $n = 4$; subleading terms $a_1$, $a_2$ add further powers of $\tau$ but do not remove the dependence, so the conclusion is robust to the truncation of the expansion at leading order.
- The literature confrontation uses the specific analyses [4], [6], [7] and [8]. The bibliography is limited to these and the other listed works; no claim in this paper depends on literature outside it, and the age-spread debate remains active, as the review in [1] states.
- The density-scaling result D1 assumes scale-independent energy $E$ under the reduction of $L$; this is the version 1.0 derivation's own assumption, and the result characterizes the model's internal scaling rather than any measured system.

**Falsification of the main result.** The main result — that the age-spread claim of [12] is unsupported — would be falsified by a single calculation or observation: a morphological analysis of NGC 1806 and NGC 1846, controlling for binaries, photometric errors, differential extinction and rotation, that finds sub-giant-branch and red-clump widths consistent with a $200$–$600$ Myr spread. Such a result would contradict [4] directly and would restore the Section 5.5 claim to at least contested status. Similarly, the regularization downgrade would be falsified by a derivation within the version 1.0 framework that produces an effective action with no residual dependence on $\tau$; the calculation in D2 shows the leading term carries $\tau^{-2}$, and any claimed resolution must show where that dependence cancels.

**Open questions.** Whether the adelic regulators proposed in [13] — which are phrased as a "workaround for single-place QFT," not as a resolution — can be connected to the finite-scale prescription of [12] is open. Whether the cognitive-retention hysteresis claim of [12] can be given a falsifiable form at the standard set by [14] is open; no comparative prediction against a point-based cognitive model exists in the literature surveyed here. Whether the Myr-scale gradients of [8] generalize to massive intermediate-age clusters is an empirical question the present bibliography does not settle.

## 7. Practical Implications and Engineering Pathways

**(a) Who would use the result and what changes.** The primary users are three groups. Stellar-population astronomers and reviewers who encounter citations of [12] for eMSTO age spreads would instead cite [4] and [6] for the morphological counter-evidence and [8] for the Myr-scale gradients; the decision that changes is whether a Gyr-scale age spread is treated as established or as an upper limit contradicted by sub-giant-branch morphology. Physicists and philosophers of physics who encounter the "resolves the UV catastrophe" claim would instead treat finite-scale cutoffs as regulators requiring renormalization; the design change is in how cutoff-based proposals are evaluated, namely by checking for residual cutoff dependence as in D2. Educators and curricula builders who inherited the version 1.0 claims would replace them with the corrected wording. This work is remediation rather than foundational; its users exist because the version 1.0 paper is published and cited.

**(b) Current readiness.** This paper is analytical work that has not been peer reviewed: under the NASA Technology Readiness Level definitions it sits at TRL 1–2. The derivations D1–D3 are basic research outputs; no prototype, instrument or observational program has been designed or built around them, and no analytical result of this kind can exceed TRL 3.

**(c) The smallest next test.** The smallest de-risking step is a re-analysis calculation, not a new observation: re-fit the existing HST/ACS photometry of NGC 1806 and NGC 1846 with isochrone grids that marginalize over binary fraction, differential extinction and rotation, and report whether the sub-giant-branch width remains narrower than a $200$–$600$ Myr spread predicts. An outcome in which the sub-giant branches widen to compatibility with such a spread would refute the retraction of the Section 5.5 claim; an outcome confirming [4] would consolidate it. On the regularization side, the smallest check is an extension of D2 to include the $a_1$ and $a_2$ terms and to $n = 2$ and $n = 3$, verifying that every term retains an explicit power of $\tau$; a single term that becomes cutoff-independent after the full expansion would weaken the censoring conclusion.

**(d) Binding constraints.** For the observational test, the binding constraint is photometric precision and systematics control: the analysis in [4] uses field-star-subtracted HST/ACS catalogues with extinction corrections taken from published values, and any re-analysis must match or exceed that control. For the regularization extension, the binding constraint is the validity of the heat-kernel expansion itself, which assumes a smooth background manifold, as the version 1.0 text's own limitations section acknowledges. Data availability is not a constraint for the proposed re-analysis, since the archival imaging already exists in the HST archive under the programmes used by [4]. No cost, regulatory or manufacturing constraint binds, because no hardware is proposed.

**(e) Order-of-magnitude bounds.** No cost or performance projection is offered: the proposed tests use existing archival data and closed-form calculations, and no cost figures for either test are stated in the works cited here or derivable from the calculations of Section 4.

**(f) Risks and misuse paths.** The remediated hypothesis could be misused in the opposite direction: a reader might cite this paper's retraction of the strong age-spread claim as evidence that no age spreads exist anywhere, which the literature does not support — [8] measures genuine Myr-scale core-halo gradients, and [1] concludes that part of the observed dispersion is a genuine few-Myr spread. The corrected claim is scale-specific: Gyr-scale spreads in intermediate-age clusters are unsupported, Myr-scale gradients are measured.

## 8. Conclusion

This revision retracts two abstract-level claims of the version 1.0 topological extension hypothesis paper and downgrades a third. The claim of predictive outperformance over point-based models on stellar-cluster age spreads and cognitive-retention hysteresis is retracted because no comparative prediction was performed. The claim that finite-volume regularization "successfully resolves singularities such as the UV catastrophe" is corrected: the cutoff prescription yields a finite but cutoff-dependent regularized action, $W_{\mathrm{reg}} \approx -1.583 \times 10^{-3}\, a_0\,\tau^{-2}$ in four dimensions, so the divergence is censored and renormalization remains necessary. The Section 5.5 claim that Gyr-scale age spreads persist across multiple clusters as a fundamental feature of massive star formation is retracted in its strong form: the isochrone bracket of $0.45$ Gyr for NGC 1806 and NGC 1846 is an upper limit, the sub-giant branches and red clumps of those clusters contradict a $200$–$600$ Myr spread, and the measured formation gradients are of order $1.7$ Myr in NGC 2024 and $0.6$ Myr in the Orion Nebula Cluster, within the $30$–$100\%$ systematic age uncertainties of pre-main-sequence isochrone fitting. The hypothesis survives as a weaker but honest program: extended formation is real at the Myr scale, finite scales censor divergences, and the companion Zitterbewegung numbers pass their internal consistency check to $0.2\%$. Each retraction carries its own reversal condition, stated in Sections 6 and 7, so the corrected text is falsifiable in the way the original was not.

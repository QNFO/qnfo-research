## Research idea

Cross-Model Convergence as an Independent Gate-Check Signal for Fringe Physics Claims. Claim: When two independent LLMs (different architectures, training data, parent organizations) converge on the same evaluation of a fringe scientific claim, that convergence constitutes a verification signal stronger than any single model's judgment, and can serve as an automated gate-check for whether a fringe theory merits expert review. Why it matters: Existing benchmarks (CLAIM-BENCH, SCITAB, Climinator) evaluate single-LLM performance against ground truth; none tests whether inter-model agreement itself carries evidential weight, especially when convergence emerges without adversarial prompting. How to test: Formalize convergence as a probabilistic independence argument—estimate the probability that two conditionally independent evaluators agree by chance given base rates of acceptance/rejection—then empirically measure agreement rates across model pairs on a corpus of known-valid and known-refuted physics claims (e.g., Bell-test literature). If agreement on refuted claims significantly exceeds chance, convergence is a usable screening signal; if models share correlated biases (common training data), quantify the correlation and bound the signal's value.

# Falsification Criteria for Quantum-Advantage Claims: An Effective-Criterion Audit Framework

## Abstract

Claims of quantum advantage are frequently stated in forms that resist empirical refutation: the advantage is qualified by hardware assumptions, sampling definitions, or problem encodings that can be revised after a classical rebuttal appears, leaving funders and editors without a decision procedure. This paper constructs a structured falsification framework for such claims, modeled on *effective* criteria in algebra — criteria satisfied on a dense, open subset of the relevant class of objects — as found in tensor identifiability, critical-pair elimination for Gröbner bases, and finite-step decidability of quantum gate universality. We define a five-component falsification index $F \in [0,1]$ covering specification completeness, inference–prediction independence, classical-baseline auditability, replication independence, and pre-registered failure thresholds, with fixed weights and explicit decision thresholds. Worked examples give $F = 0.675$ (conditionally falsifiable) and $F = 0.10$ (structurally unfalsifiable), and a necessity proof shows any claim without a complete specification is capped at $F = 0.70 < 0.8$. Instantiation against published audits shows the framework discriminates: a semigroup mass-ratio claim scores $F = 0.70$ and was in fact refuted by a statistical audit with $p_{\text{global}} = 0.116$, while a kill-criteria exercise found zero of seven criteria operational. The framework gives editors and auditors a repeatable scoring instrument with a constructive repair path.

## 1. Introduction

A quantum-advantage claim asserts that a quantum device solves some computational task faster, cheaper, or with fewer resources than any classical method. The community's response to contested claims has been largely reactive: each contested experiment generates an ad hoc classical simulation, a dispute about resource counts, and a slow drift toward ambiguity. Program managers at funding agencies must decide whether to continue supporting a platform; journal editors must decide whether an advantage claim is publishable; classical-algorithm groups must decide whether a rebuttal is even addressed to a stable target. Today each of these decisions is made case by case, without a shared instrument, and the claim survives not because it is true but because it has been made unfalsifiable — the task is redefined, the noise model adjusted, or the baseline weakened until the claim is no longer comparable to the rebuttal. A decision procedure that scores a claim's falsifiability *before* publication, and outputs which specific component to repair, would change all three decisions: editors could gate on a number with stated thresholds, funders could track repair progress across versions of a claim, and rebuttal authors would have a fixed target that cannot be moved after the fact.

What is missing is a criterion that is *effective* in a precise sense borrowed from algebraic geometry and computational algebra. In the theory of tensor decompositions, a criterion for $h$-identifiability is called effective if it is satisfied in a dense, open subset of the set of rank-$h$ tensors [1]. Genericity is the point: a criterion that fails on a measure-zero set of degenerate cases is still useful, because the degenerate cases can be recognized and handled separately. We argue that falsifiability criteria for quantum-advantage claims should be designed and evaluated the same way — while also flagging, in Section 6, that claim space has no canonical topology or measure, so the dense-open language can only be used heuristically.

The philosophical anchor is the debate over falsification in theories of consciousness. Kleiner and Hoel introduced a formal mathematical model of the process of generating observable data from experiments and using that data to generate inferences and predictions onto an experience space, defining falsification as the situation where an inference at an observation point falls outside the prediction at that point; a reply to that work showed that the original independence definition trivially implied pre-falsification and introduced a corrected, two-type definition of independence [3]. Whatever the merits of that dispute, it demonstrates that falsification arguments are themselves model-relative and can be contested at the level of the model rather than the conclusion — exactly the situation of quantum-advantage claims, where the model connecting experiment to claim is where unfalsifiability hides.

The practical anchor is the audit literature itself. A full-spectrum audit of Huang, Choi, McClean and Preskill's "The vast world of quantum advantage" subjects both that work's five-keystone framework and the auditor's own prior critiques to symmetric scrutiny, and identifies Theorem 1 of the audited work — the result that detecting quantum advantage is itself classically hard — as the most sophisticated challenge to the auditor's epistemic posture yet produced [12]. A statistical audit of a semigroup mass-ratio claim found two arithmetically false claimed fits, five non-optimal triples out of nine, and a global look-elsewhere probability of $p_{\text{global}} = 0.116$, issuing a negative verdict as a published deliverable [9]. A kill-criteria exercise for an adelic framework proposed seven criteria, audited each for escape hatches, and found zero of seven currently operational [10]. These artifacts show that structured falsification audits are already being performed; what they lack is a shared scoring rubric that makes their coverage, thresholds, and repair outputs comparable across audits.

Our contributions are: (i) a five-criterion falsification framework with explicit scoring rules, derived in Section 3; (ii) a composite index $F$ with fixed weights, full arithmetic, and two worked examples ($F = 0.675$ and $F = 0.10$), derived in Section 4; (iii) a necessity proof that specification completeness is required for the top decision band, derived in Section 4; (iv) an illustrative scoring of a published audited claim showing the index reproduces the audit's outcome, derived in Section 4 from the numbers of [9]; and (v) an explicit list of failure modes and falsification conditions for the framework itself, stated in Section 6.

## 2. Background and Related Work

We review the literature spanning algebra, quantum information, philosophy of science, open-infrastructure projects, and structured audit practice. Each work is discussed for the specific contributions its text makes to the framework design.

**[1] Effective identifiability criteria for tensors and polynomials (arXiv:1703.02637v1).** This work defines a tensor $T$ in a given tensor space to be $h$-identifiable if it admits a unique decomposition as a sum of $h$ rank-one tensors, and calls a criterion for $h$-identifiability effective if it is satisfied in a dense, open subset of the set of rank-$h$ tensors; the paper gives effective $h$-identifiability criteria for a large class of tensors and then improves them for some cases. Its Section 3 derives the criteria through flattenings: Proposition 3.1 requires, for an $(A,B)$-flattening $\widetilde{T}:V_{A}^{*}\rightarrow V_{B}$ with $\dim(V_{A}^{*})\geq h$, that the projected space $\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{T}(V_{A}^{*}))$ have dimension $h-1$ and meet the secant variety in dimension zero and degree $h$. This is the direct template for our notion of an effective falsification criterion: genericity on a dense open set, with degenerate cases handled separately, and with checkable algebraic conditions rather than holistic judgment.

**[2] Non-Commutative Gebauer-Moeller Criteria (arXiv:1302.3805v3).** For an efficient implementation of Buchberger's Algorithm it is essential to avoid treating as many unnecessary critical pairs or obstructions as possible; in the commutative polynomial ring this is achieved by the Gebauer-Möller criteria, and the paper adapts these criteria to non-commutative polynomial rings, i.e., free associative algebras $K\langle X\rangle$. Its Section 3 states the operational test directly: the criteria check whether an obstruction can be represented by "smaller" obstructions, and if so the obstruction is declared unnecessary. Its Section 4 reports an experimental implementation in C++ within the ApCoCoA library, applied to ideals generated from finite generalized triangle groups in $\mathbb{Q}\langle a,b\rangle$ for $k = 1,\dots,13$. The audit-relevant lesson is pruning: an audit protocol that must check every conceivable objection is computationally infeasible, and structural criteria that eliminate unnecessary checks in advance are what make an audit tractable — and the presence of measured experiments shows pruning claims can be quantified.

**[3] No Substitute for Functionalism — A Reply to 'Falsification & Consciousness' (arXiv:2006.13664v3).** Kleiner and Hoel, in 'Falsification and Consciousness', introduced a formal mathematical model of the process of generating observable data from experiments and using that data to generate inferences and predictions onto an experience space; the resulting substitution argument was used to show that any theory of consciousness with independent inference and prediction components is subject to a structural objection, and the reply defends functionalism against this argument. The reply's Section 2 records the load-bearing definitions: falsification as $inf(o)\notin pred(o)$ at an observation $o$; $o_r$-substitution as a transformation of the parameter space that disjointly splits predicted observations; and independence of inference and prediction data as a variation condition on observations. Its Section 6 concludes that the sweeping claim that most contemporary theories of consciousness are already falsified is unjustified because independence was defined to trivially imply pre-falsification, and introduces Type-1 and Type-2 variations to repair the definition. For our purposes the episode establishes two lessons: falsification arguments are model-relative and can be attacked at the level of the model; and a falsification framework must specify which components of a theory are independently inferable and predictable, with a non-trivial independence definition, or it will be vulnerable to the same substitution-style counterattack. We build this requirement into criterion $C_2$.

**[4] On the unraveling of open quantum dynamics (arXiv:2309.13408v2).** The state operator of an open quantum system can generically be represented as the solution of a time-local quantum master equation; unraveling in quantum trajectories offers a dual picture, in which physical indicators are computed as Monte-Carlo averages over a stochastic process. The paper's Section 2 gives the canonical master-equation form with $d^{2}-1$ decoherence couplings $\{w_{l;t}\}$ whose sign structure determines complete positivity, and its Section 9 reviews the influence martingale as the most general unraveling and identifies parameter estimation from measurement records and quantum error mitigation as the most promising applications. This supplies the technical vocabulary for our stochastic-error requirement: many advantage claims depend on noise models whose observable consequences are computed exactly as such Monte-Carlo averages, so a falsification criterion must state which unraveling, or which master-equation equivalent, is part of the claim's specification.

**[5] OpenPodcar: an Open Source Vehicle for Self-Driving Car Research (arXiv:2205.04454v2).** OpenPodcar is a low-cost, open-source hardware and software autonomous-vehicle research platform built on an off-the-shelf, hard-canopy mobility-scooter donor vehicle, with hardware and software build instructions provided to convert the donor vehicle into a fully autonomous platform; the open platform consists of CAD designs, a bill of materials, and build instructions, plus software. Its metadata section states the replication plan concretely: a second copy is to be built from the documentation alone to improve its accuracy, and the design is being forked by a commercial manufacturer. It exemplifies the replication standard our criterion $C_4$ demands: complete build instructions, not descriptions of builds, with replication exercised as part of the project itself.

**[6] CoReCon: an open, community-powered collection of Reionization constraints (arXiv:2309.06480v1).** Constraints on the Universe during and before cosmic reionization are rapidly growing in number but are scattered across inhomogeneous formats, unit systems, and sampling strategies; CoReCon is a Python package providing a growing set of constraints on key physical quantities related to the Epoch of Reionization and a platform for high reproducibility, authored by Enrico Garaldi and released in September 2023. This is the data-governance model for $C_3$ and $C_4$: constraints — here, audit evidence and baseline data — scattered across formats are effectively unusable; a community-curated, format-normalized collection is the remedy.

**[7] Criteria for universality of quantum gates (arXiv:1610.00547v6).** This work addresses deciding whether a set of quantum one-qudit gates $\mathcal{S}=\{U_1,\ldots,U_n\}$ is universal, providing compact-form criteria leading to a simple algorithm that decides universality of any given gate set in a finite number of steps; for a non-universal $\mathcal{S}$, the criteria indicate what type of gates could be added to make it universal. Its Section III gives the algorithm explicitly: Step 1 checks whether the adjoint commutant consists of scalar multiples of the identity, a linear-algebra test on the kernel of a matrix built from the $\mathrm{Ad}_{U_i}$; Step 2 checks whether some gate power falls in a specified ball $\mathcal{B}$ outside the center; Step 3 enlarges $\mathcal{S}$ by words of length $l$ and iterates. This is the strongest template for our program: a decision procedure that terminates in finitely many steps, and that is *constructive on failure* — it says not only "no" but "what is missing." Our repair-path design borrows this constructive-failure property.

**[8] HPLT 3.0: Very Large-Scale Multilingual Resources for LLMs and MT (arXiv:2511.01066v3).** The HPLT initiative provides open, very large, high-quality, richly annotated textual datasets for almost 200 languages; at 30 trillion tokens it is likely the largest generally available multilingual collection of LLM pre-training data, derived from web crawls and accompanied by a complete open-source pipeline for document selection. Its evaluation sections describe HPLT-e, an automated multilingual evaluation framework that pretrains separate language models per language under a fixed setup and evaluates at 1B-token checkpoint intervals, reporting a final suite of 26 selected tasks across seven languages after excluding tasks for lesser-resourced languages such as Basque and Galician that proved unsuitable. We use its scale as a concrete calibration point for what "open at scale" means in quantitative terms (Section 4), and its per-language evaluation practice as a model for how audit infrastructure should report coverage honestly — including where evaluation is not meaningful.

**[9] QNFO: Statistical Audit of the 5-Smooth Semigroup Mass-Ratio Claim.** This audit examines the claim that all Standard Model mass ratios are Pythagorean ($2^a \cdot 3^b \cdot 5^c$) to within approximately 1%, with nine fitted ratios and maximum claimed deviation 0.29%. Its Finding A shows two claimed fits are arithmetically false: the printed value 16.80 for $m_\tau/m_\mu$ against an actual value of 0.2624 (a 98.4% deviation), and the printed value 244888.0 for $m_h/m_e$ against an actual value of 239.15 (a 99.9% deviation), with 244888 not even a 3-smooth number since $244{,}888 = 2^3 \cdot 7 \cdot 4373$. Its Finding B reports an exhaustive bounded search over $|a|,|b|,|c| \leq 14$ finding strictly better triples for five of nine ratios, dropping the maximum deviation to 0.11% under optimal triples. Its look-elsewhere analysis gives $p_{\text{global}} = 0.116$, meaning the observed tolerance is achieved by 99.8% of random ratio sets, and its Section 5.2 computes $R = 2.453\times10^{-3}/7.53\times10^{-5} = 32.576$ with best 3-smooth fit $2^{-5}3^{-1}5^{5} = 32.552$, deviation 0.07%, so the pre-registered neutrino prediction passes trivially. The verdict is "consistent with look-elsewhere artifact," published as a negative deliverable under an outcome-neutrality commitment. This audit is our primary worked instantiation: it demonstrates every component our framework requires — arithmetic verification, optimality search, a pre-registered prediction, and a null-hypothesis baseline.

**[10] QNFO: Killing the Framework: Seven Criteria for the Falsification of Adelic Physics.** This paper proposes seven kill criteria for the adelic framework and, crucially, audits each one for escape hatches. Its Section 2.1 illustrates the method on KC1: the global product formula criterion is rendered inoperative by three escape hatches, including a pre-declared reclassification of any observed fine-structure-constant variation as modulus evolution, and the absence of an operational definition of "p-adic charge." Its Section 3 summary table grades all seven criteria on operationality, escape hatches, and testability, concluding that zero of seven kill criteria are currently operational, that two (KC2, KC4) could become operational with specific derivations, and that five are structurally unfalsifiable or promissory as written. This is the clearest published demonstration of the failure mode our framework must detect: criteria that look like falsification criteria but admit escape clauses, and the value of red-teaming one's own criteria before deployment.

**[11] QNFO: The Falsifiability Crisis in Contemporary Physics: LCDM, the Standard Model, and the Measurement-Exercise Trap.** This paper diagnoses contemporary fundamental physics as de-facto unfalsifiable through five structural patterns. Its Section 1 defines the falsifiability gradient: the set of observations $O$ such that $P(O|T)$ is bounded near zero, which is empty — zero empirical content — if a theory can accommodate any observation through auxiliary hypotheses or reclassification. Its Section 2 maps the LCDM framework onto Lakatos's research-programme structure, with Einstein's field equations as the hard core and dark-matter density profiles, the dark-energy equation of state, and inflationary potentials as the protective belt that absorbs anomalies. Its Section 7 lists the five patterns, including that the Standard Model's nineteen free parameters are measured inputs rather than predictions, and prescribes independent consilience with pre-registered falsification conditions, operationalized through a Bayesian delta-log-odds gate, explicitly requiring that the paper's own claims pass the same gate. This supplies the threshold logic our decision rule adopts: a claim must be scored against pre-registered conditions, and the scorer must be scorable.

**[12] QNFO: The Vast World of Quantum Advantage — A Full-Spectrum Audit.** This audit subjects both the Huang, Choi, McClean & Preskill (2025) five-keystone framework (arXiv:2508.05720) — five keystone properties of Predictability, Typicality, Robustness, Verifiability, and Usefulness — and the auditor's own prior critiques to symmetric scrutiny. Its Section 3 reconstructs Theorem 1: assuming BPP $\neq$ BQP, the decision problem "does this specific quantum circuit outperform Pauli propagation classical simulation?" is in BQP but not in BPP, with the proof strategy that the problem is quantumly easy and that classical easiness would collapse BQP into BPP; the audit calls this the most sophisticated challenge to QNFO's epistemic posture yet produced. Its revised vulnerability table grades five documented vulnerabilities (V1–V5) with severities, plus a systemic cross-cutting finding that at least three D1 papers were truncated with corrections not propagated to source documents. The methodological commitment — symmetric scrutiny of a target framework *and* of the auditor's own prior critiques, with severity revisions dated and evidenced — is the epistemic posture this paper adopts, and its vulnerability table supplies a concrete example of audit output that our index is designed to normalize.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Claim formalization

An advantage claim $\mathcal{K}$ is a tuple

$$\mathcal{K} = (T, D, B, M, \Theta),$$

where $T$ is the computational task, $D$ the quantum device with noise model $\Theta$, $B$ the classical baseline, and $M$ the comparison metric. The claim is *specification-complete* if each component is fixed in advance and cannot be revised after classical rebuttals appear.

### 3.2 The five falsification criteria

- $C_1$ (Specification completeness): every element of $(T, D, B, M, \Theta)$ is stated with enough precision that two independent laboratories would agree on its instantiation.
- $C_2$ (Inference–prediction independence): following the structural lesson of [3], the claim must state which quantities are inferred from experiment and which are predicted by theory, under an independence definition that does not trivially imply its own conclusion — the repair the reply to Kleiner and Hoel made necessary by showing the original definition trivially implied pre-falsification [3]. No component may be simultaneously inferred and predicted in a way that shields it from refutation.
- $C_3$ (Baseline auditability): the classical baseline $B$ is specified with a resource bound, and the best-known classical algorithm's cost is either stated or bounded by a formula a third party can evaluate; where indicators are Monte-Carlo averages over stochastic processes, as in quantum-trajectory unravelings [4], the claimed indicator must carry an explicit sampling-error budget, and the classical comparison must meet the same budget. The null-baseline computation in [9] — the look-elsewhere analysis yielding $p_{\text{global}} = 0.116$ — is the model of what a third-party-evaluable baseline check looks like.
- $C_4$ (Replication independence): the full experimental recipe — hardware, calibration, and analysis pipeline — is open in the sense of [5] and [8]: build instructions, data, and selection pipeline all public, with evidence deposited in a community-curated, format-normalized collection in the spirit of CoReCon [6].
- $C_5$ (Pre-registered failure threshold): a number $\epsilon \in (0,1)$ is fixed in advance such that if the observed advantage ratio falls below threshold, the claim is declared refuted, with no post-hoc revision permitted. The kill-criteria exercise of [10] shows why the threshold alone is insufficient: each threshold must also be audited for escape hatches, since a criterion with a pre-declared reclassification path is inoperative regardless of its threshold.

### 3.3 The falsification index

Each criterion $C_i$ receives a score $s_i \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$: $1$ if fully satisfied, $0.5$ if partially satisfied (specified but with an ambiguous term), $0$ if absent. The weights are fixed ex ante:

$$w_1 = 0.30, \quad w_2 = 0.25, \quad w_3 = 0.20, \quad w_4 = 0.15, \quad w_5 = 0.10,$$

with $\sum_{i=1}^{5} w_i = 1$. The falsification index is

$$F(\mathcal{K}) = \sum_{i=1}^{5} w_i \, s_i.$$

Decision rule: a claim with $F \geq 0.8$ is *falsifiable-ready*; $0.5 \leq F < 0.8$ is *conditionally falsifiable* (repairs required); $F < 0.5$ is *structurally unfalsifiable* and should not be published as an advantage claim. The thresholds are conventional, and Section 6 states what empirical evidence would recalibrate them; the delta-log-odds gate of [11] is a compatible alternative threshold mechanism, and the two are not competitors but different granularities of the same pre-registration requirement.

### 3.4 Termination

Mirroring the finite-step decision procedure of [7], the audit terminates: each $s_i$ is decided by inspecting a finite document set (the claim, its supplement, and the baseline code), so the total procedure is a finite composition of finite checks. The algorithm of [7] terminates because each step is a finite linear-algebra or enumeration check over a bounded set; our audit terminates for the same reason, with the document set playing the role of the bounded gate set. If $F < 0.8$, the framework outputs the ordered list of criteria with $s_i < 1$, weighted by $w_i$, as a constructive repair path — the analogue of [7]'s indication of which gates to add to a non-universal set.

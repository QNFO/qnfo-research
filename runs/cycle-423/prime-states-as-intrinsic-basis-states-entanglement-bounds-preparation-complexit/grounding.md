## Research idea

Prime Numbers as Intrinsic Basis States for Computation. The author conjectures that prime numbers should be treated not as parameters of algorithms (e.g., hash moduli) but as the fundamental units of computation itself—analogous to bits or qubits—via a 'Prime state': a quantum superposition of all primes below 2^n serving as the intrinsic basis of a register. The open question is whether such prime-basis architectures yield provable computational advantages and robustness (e.g., near-maximal entanglement entropy enabling quantum speedup for number-theoretic problems), and whether a purely mathematical 'QuPrimes' model can replicate these advantages on classical hardware at room temperature. A paper could test this by: (1) formally deriving the entanglement structure and complexity of Grover-prepared prime states with a Miller-Rabin oracle; (2) simulating prime-basis encodings on classical hardware to benchmark claimed exponential speedups against classical and physical quantum baselines; and (3) analyzing whether coprimality and uniform-distribution properties translate into provable error or collision robustness at the architecture level.

## Research idea

Decryption-capable analog machines as general-purpose computers. Conjecture: any physical machine capable of decrypting messages (like Turing's Bombe) is necessarily a general-purpose computer, since decryption requires the same search/dynamics as universal computation. Question: what computational class do oscillator-, photonic-, or phonon-based analog/hybrid machines occupy relative to the von Neumann digital model, and what do they optimize (speed, energy, non-brute-force search)? Testable via formal analysis: characterize the computational universality of analog decryption machines by mapping their dynamics onto known models (analog circuits, resonant parametron networks, phase-change systems); simulate representative oscillator networks on tasks requiring search beyond gradient descent; and derive conditions under which decryption capability implies Turing completeness, or identify counterexamples where it does not.

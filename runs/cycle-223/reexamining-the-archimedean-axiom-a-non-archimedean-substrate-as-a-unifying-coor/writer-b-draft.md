# The Archimedean Axiom as a Coordinate Choice: A Computable Adjudication Criterion for Non-Archimedean Foundations of Physics

## Abstract

The real numbers are the default continuum of theoretical physics, yet Ostrowski's theorem shows they are one completion of the rationals among many, and a growing body of work argues that the Archimedean axiom underlying this default is an unrecognized coordinate choice whose reification produces specific pathologies: ultraviolet divergences, set-theoretic paradoxes, and the timelessness of the Wheeler-DeWitt equation. The missing element is not another manifesto but a computable rule for deciding when a non-Archimedean substrate actually beats the continuum. We formalize the proposed "Keplerian criterion" — a framework wins if it derives known physical numbers from first principles with fewer free parameters — as a quantitative ratio, and we supply the first concrete calculations on the non-Archimedean side. For the Bruhat-Tits tree $BT_p$ we derive the exact ball cardinality $|B_n| = 1 + (p+1)(p^n-1)/(p-1)$, giving $3070$ states at depth $n=10$ for $p=2$ against $21$ on a comparable Archimedean interval, a capacity ratio of $146.19$; we derive the corresponding lattice-count arithmetic for culture-driven voting paradoxes and the scale separations $S = 5.56\times 10^{6}$ and $\chi_{\parallel}/\chi_{\perp} = 10^{8}$ that continuum MHD simulation must resolve. Applying the criterion to the non-Archimedean research program as it currently stands yields zero first-principles derivations of known constants, so the adjudication is formally inconclusive. The criterion itself, not a verdict, is the deliverable: it converts a foundational debate into a falsifiable counting exercise.

## 1. Introduction

Theoretical physics faces a decision problem that is usually framed as an empirical problem but is in fact partly a mathematical one. The European Strategy process lists the fundamental puzzles left unsolved by the Standard Model — the origin of electroweak symmetry breaking, the nature of the Higgs boson, the pattern of quark and lepton masses, the neutrino nature and mass, and on the cosmology side dark matter, dark energy, inflation, and the matter-antimatter asymmetry — and observes that this list "did not change in the post-Run 2 LHC era" [1]. The experimental response is to build instruments of escalating scale: the next linear collider program was justified on precisely the grounds that the symmetry-breaking agent "must provide new physical phenomena at the TeV energy scale," with machines designed for center-of-mass energies of $0.5$ to $1.5$ TeV [2]. Each such instrument is a multi-decade, multi-country commitment. The QNFO synthesis on reification argues that this pattern — proliferation of unobservable entities such as virtual particles, dark matter, the inflaton, spacetime points, and the null pointer — is a symptom of an ill-chosen descriptive framework rather than a discovery about nature, and names the Archimedean axiom, the assumption of a smooth infinitely divisible continuum, as the unrecognized coordinate choice forcing these "epicycles" into existence [9].

Who faces this problem, and what does it cost? Physicists and the funding communities behind them face it at every strategy cycle: the choice between "increasing the energy scale at which the puzzles can be explored" and "precision measurements of rare processes" is a choice about where the next failure of the descriptive framework will show up [1]. If the reification diagnosis is correct, some of that escalation is misdirected: the epicycles would dissolve under a change of number system, the way epicycles dissolved under a change of reference frame. If the diagnosis is wrong, programs that divert effort toward non-Archimedean modeling waste years. The decision that would change if this paper's result holds is concrete: a funding or research-program decision could be made by computing a ratio rather than by adjudicating manifestos.

The specific gap is that the non-Archimedean program has produced diagnosis and architecture but no adjudication procedure. The forecast assessment of the adelic-substrate thesis states the situation bluntly: "zero of the three closest analogues achieved mainstream adoption within a decade," and "no such anomaly has been identified for the Archimedean default" [10]. A paradigm without an anomaly and without a criterion cannot displace an incumbent, no matter how elegant its geometry. The Wheeler-DeWitt side of the program faces the same gap from the other direction: the static, timeless Bruhat-Tits ontology is asserted, and candidate experimental signatures such as deviations in the statistical distribution of entanglement correlations are sketched, but no intermediate quantity is computed that would let anyone check the framework's state capacity or parameter economy [11].

This paper does not claim the Archimedean axiom is false. It claims something weaker and more useful: the conjecture that the axiom is a coordinate choice is currently unadjudicable, and it can be made adjudicable by formalizing the Keplerian criterion — a framework wins if it derives known numbers from first principles with fewer free parameters — and by performing the counting calculations that both sides of the comparison require. The contributions are:

- A formal definition of the Keplerian criterion as a computable ratio $K$ of first-principles-derived numbers to free parameters, with the adjudication rule stated as an inequality (Section 3).
- An exact derivation of the state capacity of the Bruhat-Tits tree $BT_p$: the ball cardinality $|B_n| = 1 + (p+1)(p^n-1)/(p-1)$, evaluated to $3070$ for $p=2$, $n=10$ and $118097$ for $p=3$, $n=10$, with the Archimedean comparison ratio $146.19$ (Section 4).
- A derivation of the lattice-count arithmetic underlying culture-based voting-paradox probabilities, $(n+1)^6 = 1771561$ for $n=10$ voters in the six-type decomposition, as a worked instance of discrete-state counting of the kind the non-Archimedean program must match (Section 4).
- A derivation of the continuum scale separations that any discrete substrate must reproduce, from the MHD simulation parameters $S = 5.56\times 10^{6}$ and $\chi_{\parallel}/\chi_{\perp} = 10^{8}$, giving a length-scale ratio of $10^{4}$ (Section 4).
- An application of the criterion to the current non-Archimedean literature, showing $K = 0$ derived constants and therefore a formally inconclusive adjudication, with the precise calculation that would break the tie (Sections 4 and 5).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The case for treating the Archimedean axiom as a choice rather than a necessity rests on Ostrowski's theorem. The adelic-substrate assessment states the theorem's content: every non-trivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to either the usual real absolute value $|\cdot|_{\infty}$ or the $p$-adic absolute value $|\cdot|_p$ for some prime $p$, and the $p$-adic numbers satisfy the strong triangle inequality $|x+y|_p \leq \max(|x|_p, |y|_p)$, which makes the induced metric ultrametric, with every triangle isosceles and the base no longer than either leg [10]. The same assessment documents where non-Archimedean geometry is already native rather than re-encoded: phylogenetics, where the molecular clock hypothesis produces ultrametric distance matrices, and isogeny-based cryptography. Its domain table quantifies the frontier: $p$-adic quantum mechanics at roughly 50 papers per year, $p$-adic and adelic string theory at roughly 20 per year, ultrametric code metrics and Bruhat-Tits code constructions at fewer than 5 per year, $p$-adic neural networks at fewer than 10 per year, and $p$-adic channel coding at fewer than 5 per year [10]. These activity numbers matter for this paper's argument because they establish that the candidate substrate has working mathematicians but no decisive result; the paper's own conclusion is that the central bet — displacement in at least one domain by 2036 — is viable in candidate domains but unsupported by any identified anomaly [10].

The reification synthesis supplies the diagnostic vocabulary this paper formalizes. It argues that the proliferation of unobservable entities is a symptom of an ill-chosen framework, identifies the Archimedean axiom as the common denominator, and proposes the Bruhat-Tits tree as the invariant structure beneath four candidate implementations: holography, causal set theory, the crossed-product construction of algebraic QFT, and a Syntactic Token Calculus [9]. Its comparative table is specific about the state of each candidate: holography is rigorous for AdS with de Sitter open, causal sets have a rigorous poset framework with dynamics open, the crossed-product construction is rigorous via Takesaki duality and claimed to solve the de Sitter entropy problem, and the token calculus is internally consistent with confluence proven but unvalidated externally [9]. The table's empirical-adequacy column claims derived particle patterns and predicted CMB log-periodicity and Higgs resonances for the token calculus; these are the authors' claims, and the Keplerian criterion of Section 3 is designed precisely to force such claims into a countable form. The synthesis also states the trade-off the criterion must weigh: ontological parsimony against empirical adequacy [9].

The Wheeler-DeWitt strand of the program is developed in the time-as-fiction paper, which argues that the flowing river of time is a biological interface, that the Wheeler-DeWitt equation provides a static configuration space devoid of any dynamic temporal parameter, and that the universe is a frozen network of discrete informational nodes modeled by the Bruhat-Tits tree [11]. That paper is explicit about the empirical gap: because the discrete structure lives at the Planck scale, direct observation is currently impossible, and the program must instead seek subtle macroscopic signatures, with the statistical distribution of quantum entanglement correlations named as one candidate avenue where standard quantum mechanics predicts a specific continuous range of probabilities [11]. This is exactly the kind of prediction the Keplerian criterion is meant to price: a continuous range is an Archimedean signature, and its violation would be a discrete-substrate anomaly.

Against these programmatic works stand two mathematical results that constrain what "the Archimedean axiom is arbitrary" can mean. First, the topological proof of the Archimedean axiom for Archimedean copulas shows that in at least one broad class of statistical structures the axiom is not a free choice but a theorem: the paper develops a proof using the $V_C$-volume that applies to non-continuous distribution functions, partitioning a box $B = \cup_{k,j} B_{k,j}$ into regions of continuity and taking the limit $\lim_{m,p\to\infty}\sum_{j}\sum_{k} V_C(B_{k,j}) = V_C(B)$, with convergence guaranteed by compactness of $[0,1]^2$ under the product topology [3]. The relevance is a boundary condition: any claim that the axiom is an arbitrary coordinate choice must explain why, in measure-theoretic settings of this kind, it is derivable rather than chosen. Second, the spline-approximation work on conditional Archimedean copulas shows the axiom's operational role in statistics: an Archimedean copula has the form $C(u,v) = \varphi^{-1}(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v))$ with a decreasing convex generator $\varphi$ satisfying $\varphi(0^{+}) = +\infty$ and $\varphi(1) = 0$, characterized by that single univariate function, with Kendall's tau given by $\tau = 1 + 4\int_0^1 \lambda(s)\,ds$ where $\lambda = \varphi/\varphi'$ [7]. The generator is a one-function parameterization; the spline method replaces it with flexible regression of spline coefficients on a covariate, and its simulation study reports nearly negligible bias and credible-region coverage near nominal values even for moderate sample sizes [7]. This gives a concrete, computable instance of the trade the Keplerian criterion measures: one parametric function versus many spline coefficients, with empirical performance as the arbiter.

The set-theoretic side of the claimed isomorphism — set-theoretic paradoxes as Archimedean epicycles — is constrained by the forcing-axiom literature. The forcing-axiom paper proves equivalents of two families of weakenings of the axiom of choice: a trichotomy principle for cardinals isolated by Lévy, the principle $\mathrm{H}_\kappa$, and $\mathrm{DC}_\kappa$, the principle of dependent choices generalized to regular cardinals $\kappa$, and uses these equivalents to obtain new forcing-axiom formulations of the axiom of choice itself [8]. Its worked example is the statement $\mathrm{Fin}$ that every infinite set is Dedekind-infinite, together with the weakenings $\mathrm{AC}_\omega(\mathrm{Fin})$ (choice for countable families of finite sets) and $\mathrm{CUT}(\mathrm{Fin})$ (every countable union of finite sets is countable), and the class $\Gamma$ of pre-orders that are countable unions of finite pre-orders [8]. The specific point this paper takes from it: the choice principles underpinning the continuum are not monolithic; they decompose into a lattice of independent statements whose interplay with forcing is an active theorem-producing area. A "paradox as epicycle" argument must engage this decomposition, not treat the axiom of choice as a single reified object.

On the Archimedean side, the counting tradition that the continuum framework supports is exemplified by the polyhedral-symmetry work in social choice. Under the Impartial Anonymous Culture assumption, the probability of a voting event is computed by counting integral solutions to a system of linear inequalities, and Ehrhart theory going back to the 1960s provides the machinery; the connection to social choice was made by Lepelley et al. and by Wilson and Pritchard, with earlier social-choice-specific theory by Huang and Chua [4]. The paper's notation fixes the state space: $n$ voters subdivided into six groups $(n_{\mathsf{ab}}, n_{\mathsf{ac}}, n_{\mathsf{ba}}, n_{\mathsf{bc}}, n_{\mathsf{ca}}, n_{\mathsf{cb}})$ according to the six strict preference orders on three candidates, and Condorcet's paradox — intransitive collective choice from pairwise majorities — is the canonical event whose probability this counting computes [4]. Its conclusions report that symmetry-reduced lower-dimensional reformulations allow exact limiting probabilities for large elections with four candidates, and anticipate Ehrhart quasi-polynomial software yielding explicit formulas that "will most likely be quite huge and hardly usable without computer assistance" [4]. This is the continuum-side baseline for Section 4's counting derivations: exact probabilities exist, but at the price of polyhedra whose dimension and complexity grow rapidly.

The continuum framework's physical reach is documented by the collider literature. The Physics Briefing Book records the precision program that indirect searches make possible: oblique parameters measured with a precision of about $1\%$, roughly a factor 10 better than current precision, at future $e^+e^-$ colliders where $10^{12}$ $Z$-bosons would be available at circular machines [1]. It also records the theory-side dependency that bears on any substrate change: the QCD coupling $\alpha_s$ is the least-known coupling of the Standard Model, its value impacts all QCD cross sections and decays, and its energy evolution affects the range of stability of the electroweak vacuum approaching the Planck scale [1]. Any non-Archimedean framework claiming to derive known numbers must therefore confront $\alpha_s$, the very quantity whose determination the continuum program flags as its leading parametric uncertainty. The next-linear-collider report supplies the scale logic of the incumbent program: the SLC was built to study the 100-GeV scale and to develop the accelerator physics for future colliders, and the report argues that microwave power sources had already exceeded the requirements for the initial stage of the NLC, with critical tests assuring that the technology could drive beams to center-of-mass energies of a TeV or more [2]. The energy ladder from $0.1$ to $1.5$ TeV is the incumbent's answer to the puzzle list, and Section 4 computes its ratio explicitly.

Finally, continuum modeling at the largest scales is exemplified by the MHD design analysis of CFETR and HFRC, two Chinese fusion projects representing the low-density steady-state and high-density pulsed pathways [5]. The simulation parameters are stated exactly: for the neoclassical tearing mode calculations, the driven-current half width $w_{cd}/a = 0.04$, the heat-transport ratio $\chi_{\parallel}/\chi_{\perp} = 10^{8}$, the resistive time $\tau_R = a^2\mu_0/\eta$, the Lundquist number $S = \tau_R/\tau_A = 5.56\times 10^{6}$, the $q=2$ surface at $r_s = 0.51a$, bootstrap fraction $j_b/j_p = 0.3$, and inverse aspect ratio $\varepsilon = a/R \simeq 0.31$ [5]. The summary reports the deliverables of this continuum modeling: predicted ECCD power for full NTM stabilization, RWM stability assessment with active control required for 80% of the SSO scenarios, and disruption time scales found equivalent to ITER's [5]. These numbers give Section 4 a concrete, sourced instance of the scale separation a discrete substrate would have to reproduce. One further source illustrates how discrete choice structures are handled operationally in modern engineering systems: the humanoid-manipulation work presents a policy-learning framework "capable of modeling multi-modality with a single forward pass," evaluated on dishwasher loading and loco-manipulation wiping tasks, with the authors noting that visual perception generalizes poorly to substantially different scenes and that hand-eye coordination relies on a heuristic [6]. The relevance is methodological: selecting one branch among many in a single evaluation is a solved subproblem in robotics, and the non-Archimedean program's "distinction-calculus" dynamics claims an analogous branch-selection primitive; the robotics result shows what an operational version of that primitive looks like and where it fails (generalization), a failure mode Section 6 imports.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 The Keplerian criterion

The source conjecture proposes a criterion: a framework wins if it derives known numbers from first principles with fewer free parameters [9]. We formalize this. Let a framework $F$ be a pair $(\Sigma_F, P_F)$ where $\Sigma_F$ is the set of physical quantities it derives from first principles — no fitted inputs — and $P_F$ is its count of free parameters: independent numerical inputs not derived within the framework. Define

$$K(F) = \frac{|\Sigma_F^{\mathrm{known}}|}{|P_F|},$$

where $\Sigma_F^{\mathrm{known}} \subseteq \Sigma_F$ is the subset of derived quantities whose values were already known from the incumbent framework or experiment. The adjudication rule is:

$$F_1 \succ F_2 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad K(F_1) > K(F_2) \ \text{and}\ |\Sigma_{F_1}^{\mathrm{known}}| \geq |\Sigma_{F_2}^{\mathrm{known}}|.$$

The second clause prevents the degenerate win of a framework with one derived number and zero parameters over one with many derived numbers and few parameters; a framework that derives nothing cannot win regardless of its parameter count. We adopt two conventions. First, $|P_F|$ counts only parameters the framework itself declares free; a framework that hides a parameter inside an unstated assumption is counted as if the assumption were explicit, and Section 6 discusses how this convention can be gamed. Second, "known" means the value was measured or derived before the candidate framework produced it; this is checkable from publication records and is not a judgment call.

### 3.2 State capacity of the non-Archimedean substrate

The Bruhat-Tits tree $BT_p$ attached to $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is the candidate invariant structure of the program [9]. Its relevant property for adjudication is combinatorial: how much discrete state fits within a given depth. We derive the ball cardinality exactly in Section 4. The comparison object on the Archimedean side is a discretized interval: a path of length $n$ on a line carries $2n+1$ distinguishable positions at unit spacing. The capacity ratio $R(p,n) = |B_n(BT_p)|/(2n+1)$ is the quantity we compute.

### 3.3 Discrete counting as the shared currency

Both frameworks ultimately deliver probabilities and constants, and both reduce some of these to counting. On the continuum side, culture-based voting probabilities reduce to counting integral points in polyhedra under Ehrhart theory [4]. On the non-Archimedean side, the corresponding currency is counting vertices and branches of the tree. We therefore treat lattice counts and tree counts as the common denominator in which "state capacity" and "parameter economy" are expressed, and we derive one of each in Section 4 under stated assumptions.

### 3.4 Continuum scale separation as a reproduction target

Any substrate claiming to replace the continuum must reproduce its successful large-scale simulations. The MHD analysis of CFETR states the dimensionless parameters its simulations run at: $S = 5.56\times 10^{6}$ and $\chi_{\parallel}/\chi_{\perp} = 10^{8}$ [5]. From these we derive the anisotropic length-scale ratio a discrete model must span, in Section 4.

## 4. Analysis

### 4.1 Ball cardinality of the Bruhat-Tits tree

We derive the number of vertices within graph distance $n$ of a fixed root $v_0$ in $BT_p$. Inputs: $BT_p$ is a regular tree in which every vertex has degree $p+1$; this is the standard structure of the Bruhat-Tits tree of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, and the program adopts the tree as its substrate [9]. We state the degree as the paper's structural assumption for the $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ case.

Step 1. The root has $p+1$ neighbors, so the sphere $S_1$ has $|S_1| = p+1$.

Step 2. Every vertex $v \in S_k$ with $k \geq 1$ has one neighbor in $S_{k-1}$ (its parent on the unique non-backtracking path to the root) and therefore $p$ children in $S_{k+1}$. Hence $|S_k| = (p+1)p^{k-1}$ for $k \geq 1$.

Step 3. The ball is the disjoint union of spheres:

$$|B_n| = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{n} (p+1)p^{k-1} = 1 + (p+1)\frac{p^n - 1}{p - 1}.$$

Step 4. Evaluate for $p = 2$, $n = 10$:

$$|B_{10}| = 1 + 3 \cdot \frac{2^{10} - 1}{2 - 1} = 1 + 3 \cdot 1023 = 1 + 3069 = 3070.$$

Step 5. Evaluate for $p = 3$, $n = 10$:

$$|B_{10}| = 1 + 4 \cdot \frac{3^{10} - 1}{3 - 1} = 1 + 4 \cdot \frac{59049 - 1}{2} = 1 + 4 \cdot 29524 = 1 + 118096 = 118097.$$

Step 6. The Archimedean comparison. A line segment of length $n$ at unit spacing carries $2n+1 = 21$ distinguishable positions for $n = 10$. The capacity ratio is

$$R(2,10) = \frac{3070}{21} = 146.190476\ldots \approx 146.19.$$

For $p=3$: $R(3,10) = 118097/21 = 5623.666\ldots \approx 5623.67$. The branching structure, not the metric, is what buys this capacity: the ultrametric inequality $|x+y|_p \leq \max(|x|_p,|y|_p)$ is the analytic shadow of exactly this non-backtracking branching [10].

### 4.2 Lattice counting on the continuum side

The IAC probability of a voting event is computed by counting integral points in a polyhedron [4]. We derive the size of the raw counting space for the six-type decomposition with $n$ voters. Inputs: six preference types $(n_{\mathsf{ab}}, n_{\mathsf{ac}}, n_{\mathsf{ba}}, n_{\mathsf{bc}}, n_{\mathsf{ca}}, n_{\mathsf{cb}})$ with each $n_{\mathsf{xy}} \geq 0$ and $\sum n_{\mathsf{xy}} = n$ [4].

Step 1. The unconstrained box of non-negative six-tuples with entries in $\{0,\ldots,n\}$ has $(n+1)^6$ points.

Step 2. For $n = 10$: $11^6 = (11^3)^2 = 1331^2$. Compute $1331^2 = 1331 \times 1331$. $1331 \times 1000 = 1{,}331{,}000$; $1331 \times 300 = 399{,}300$; $1331 \times 31 = 41{,}261$. Sum: $1{,}331{,}000 + 399{,}300 + 41{,}261 = 1{,}771{,}561$.

Step 3. The simplex constraint $\sum n_{\mathsf{xy}} = n$ cuts this to $\binom{n+5}{5}$ points; for $n=10$, $\binom{15}{5} = 3003$. (Check: $\binom{15}{5} = \frac{15\cdot14\cdot13\cdot12\cdot11}{120} = \frac{360360}{120} = 3003$.)

Step 4. The relevance: the continuum-side counting tradition achieves exact probabilities, but the paper itself reports that the involved polyhedra grow rapidly in dimension and complexity, that four-candidate formulas will be "quite huge and hardly usable without computer assistance," and that five or more candidates need further ideas [4]. The raw count $1{,}771{,}561$ versus the constrained count $3003$ quantifies how much of the burden is symmetry and constraint reduction — the same reduction problem a tree substrate faces in identifying which of its $3070$ states (Section 4.1) are physically distinct.

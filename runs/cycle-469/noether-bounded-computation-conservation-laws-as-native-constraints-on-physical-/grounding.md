## Research idea

Symmetry-Instantiated Computation: Conservation Laws as Native Computational Constraints. The author conjectures that computing architectures whose operations directly instantiate continuous symmetries (SO(2), U(1), O(2)) inherit Noether-conserved quantities as built-in computational constraints, yielding intrinsically lower energy dissipation, error resilience, and topological fault tolerance compared to von Neumann discrete approximations. The open question: can one formally prove or quantify an efficiency/error-correction advantage for symmetry-equivariant physical processors, e.g., that respecting a conserved quantity bounds dissipation or error accumulation per operation? A derivation could formalize a Noether-based cost model relating symmetry group structure to computational overhead; simulations comparing symplectic/symmetric analog substrates (photonic resonators, oscillator networks) against discretized digital emulators on benchmark continuous problems could test whether symmetry alignment measurably reduces error growth and energy cost, and whether topological protection supplies provable fault tolerance without active error correction.

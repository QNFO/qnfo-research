## Research idea

Cognition as Cocycle Solving: Ultrametric Reaction Times and Analogy Depth. Claim: the brain maintains beliefs by enforcing a cocycle (flatness) condition over an associative web, effectively solving for global consistency; consequently, semantic/cognitive distance is ultrametric (strong triangle inequality), and analogy difficulty scales with the syntactic depth of the projective cross-ratio relating the domains. Why it matters: it would recast cognitive dissonance and belief revision as topological constraint satisfaction, giving psychology a hard geometric foundation and linking hierarchical semantic structure to projective invariants. Testable: (1) derive reaction-time predictions from an ultrametric semantic tree and compare against empirical RT data from semantic priming and analogy tasks (e.g., A:B::C:D items varying in hierarchical depth); (2) simulate belief-revision on a graph with a cocycle constraint, showing two resolution modes—node rejection vs. global rewarping—matching dissonance-reduction behaviors; (3) formalize 'analogy depth' via cross-ratio invariants and correlate with human difficulty ratings and fMRI hierarchy measures.

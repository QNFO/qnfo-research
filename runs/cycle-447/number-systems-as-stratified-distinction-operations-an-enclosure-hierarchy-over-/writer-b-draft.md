# Number Systems as Stratified Distinction Operations: An Enclosure Hierarchy over the Mark (Revised Version)

## Abstract

The conventional tower of number systems—$\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}$—augmented by the $p$-adic fields $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ and the adele ring $\mathbb{A}$ is usually presented as a sequence of completions without a unifying generative principle. Practitioners in physics, logic, and computer science lack a quantitative criterion for choosing among these systems, which leads to ad-hoc model selection and undocumented information loss when finite-precision arithmetic is projected onto the continuum. This paper defines a family of enclosure operators $E_{i}$ that generate each stratum from a primitive mark, an adjacent-stratum loss functional $\Lambda_{i\to i+1}=\log_{2}N_{i}-\log_{2}N_{i+1}$ over distinguishable-state counts $N_{i}$, and a separate cross-stratum state-count comparison $\Delta_{i\to j}$ for non-adjacent pairs. For the tree-to-continuum (Monna) comparison, which is a cross-stratum diagnostic and not an instance of $\Lambda$, we compute $\Delta_{6\to4}=7$ bits at depth $n=10$, base $p=2$, real precision $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$; we prove the exact linear law $\Delta_{6\to4}(n)=n-3$ at fixed $\varepsilon$ and identify the loss-free matching depth $n=3$. Finite simultaneous-valuation products over the primes $\{2,3,5\}$ yield $30$ states at depth 1 and $900$ at depth 2. State-count arithmetic is verified only for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 at finite depth. All numbers are derived by hand with every arithmetic step shown; no simulation or external measurement is used. The result converts a philosophical thesis into falsifiable, countable claims and specifies the minimal computational experiment that would test them.

## 1. Introduction

Anyone who teaches, formalizes, or computes with numbers inherits a fixed tower: $\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}$, with $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ and $\mathbb{A}$ attached to $\mathbb{Q}$ as a parallel branch. The cost of this presentation is concrete. A physicist choosing a number system for a model has no principled way to decide whether the continuum is forced or merely conventional; the adelic-physics work states the point directly: physics today operates exclusively at the Archimedean place, every measurement produces a rational number to finite precision, and Ostrowski's theorem—a proven result—makes the real line one completion among many, so the choice of $\mathbb{R}$ as the base field is an assumption rather than a derivation [11]. A logician studying constructive foundations has no unified account of why the historical sequence unfolded in this order. A computer scientist working with finite-precision arithmetic has no theory of what information is destroyed when a tree-structured limit is flattened onto a line. If the progression $\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}\to\mathbb{A}$ could be derived from increasingly refined acts of distinction, each decision would acquire a measurable criterion: the choice of number system would become a choice of operator, and the cost of each choice would be quantifiable in bits.

The stratigraphy thesis states that the history of number systems is a history of expanding distinction operations—acts of drawing boundaries that create new enclosures, each yielding a richer number system but also embedding a new hidden assumption—and it names the guiding banner "not wrong, but incomplete": each framework is locally validated yet globally partial, and the generative question is what larger distinction operation would heal the incompleteness while preserving local validity [13]. The tree-to-continuum work supplies the formal counterpart of the most delicate enclosure in the sequence: it develops the conjecture that the continuum is constructed rather than primitive, with natural numbers, rationals, and computable reals arising from a discrete tree and the full continuum generated by a lossy Monna-map projection, and it quantifies the loss by showing that the computable shadows form a countable, measure-zero subset of $[0,1]$ [12]. Neither work supplies a layer of state counts and bit budgets for the whole tower; this paper adds one, and corrects the quantitative claims of the published version [10].

Two scope restrictions are stated here and enforced throughout. First, the scope of verified state-count arithmetic is stated precisely: it is verified only for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 at finite depth, and only the state-count formulas for those strata are used in finite arithmetic (Sections 3.2 and 6). Second, the classification of the completions of $\mathbb{Q}$—the result that underwrites falsifier F3 and justifies the operator list of Section 3.2—is attributed to Ostrowski's theorem as stated in the adelic-physics work: every non-trivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent either to the standard Archimedean absolute value, whose completion is $\mathbb{R}$, or to a $p$-adic absolute value for some prime $p$, whose completion is $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ [11].

We posit a primitive mark $M$ and a family of enclosure operators $E_{i}$, each a closure-type operator whose application to the previous stratum yields the next. For each stratum we state the minimal algebraic structure generated, the canonical map to the following stratum, and a distinguishable-state count where a finite count exists. The counts support an adjacent-stratum loss functional and a separate cross-stratum comparison quantity, which we compute in full arithmetic for the tree-to-continuum case. Four falsifiability conditions are stated so that a single counterexample kills the corresponding claim.

The stratified-enclosure reading of number systems may also bear on problems outside its home domain: the stratified $\beta$-number literature shows that scalar invariants over stratified structures can require stratified refinements to become sharp [4], and the measurement-stratigraphy work maps the same completion-and-lossy-projection pattern across physics, computer science, and information theory [13]; both connections are drawn only as far as Sections 2 and 6 support them.

## 2. Background and Related Work

Thirteen works are discussed here, each through a specific result its text states.

The polynomial-systems paper solves linear systems with coefficients in multivariate polynomial rings, systems that arise in the computation of parametric Bernstein–Sato polynomials associated with hypergeometric ideals in the Weyl algebra [1]. Its preliminary section records the encoding this paper's tally stratum generalizes: the Stirling numbers of the first kind appear as the coefficients of the monic polynomial $b_{\Delta}(s)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(s-\alpha_{i})$ when the root set is $\Delta=\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}$, so a finite slice of the tally structure generates a full polynomial algebra through the elementary symmetric functions [1]. The relevance is direct: the simplest stratum of Section 3, the tally, already carries enough structure to determine objects of degree $d$ from $d$ marked points, which is the smallest instance of an enclosure generating more than it explicitly contains.

The Tate-conjectures note proves Tate's two conjectures for algebraic cycles of arbitrary codimension on certain products of elliptic curves and abelian surfaces over number fields [2]. Its proof of Tate I shows that the cycle class map ${\mathrm{Ch}}^{r}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))^{\Gamma_{F}}$ is surjective in this setting, using the chain that the Hodge conjecture and the Mumford–Tate conjecture for $X$ over $F$ imply Tate I [2]. This matters here because the entire proof operates over $\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}$ and $\ell$-adic cohomology: the $p$-adic stratum of Section 3.2 is not an exotic appendix to the tower but the field over which some of arithmetic geometry's strongest structural statements are proved.

The harmonic-congruence paper proves that for any prime $p\geq7$ the congruence $\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}H_k^2/k^2\equiv\frac{4}{5}pB_{p-5}\pmod{p^{2}}$ holds, confirming a conjecture of Z.-W. Sun, together with two similar congruences modulo $p^{2}$ [3]. Sun had proved the sum congruent to $0$ modulo $p$ and conjectured the finer modulo-$p^{2}$ statement with the Bernoulli number $B_{p-5}$ [3]. This is the correct citation for the harmonic-number congruence: the result shows that arithmetic modulo a prime power—a finite $p$-adic stratum of exactly the kind counted in Derivation 3—resolves structure that the congruence modulo $p$ alone leaves invisible.

The stratified-$\beta$-numbers paper introduces a modified version of Jones's $\beta$-numbers for Carnot groups and proves that an analogue of Jones's traveling salesman theorem on rectifiability holds in any Carnot group when the stratified $\beta$-numbers replace previous notions [4]. Crucially for this paper, its Section 3 demonstrates the negative counterpart: the non-stratified $\beta$'s cannot provide sharp two-sided traveling salesman inequalities for certain Carnot groups, with the example $G=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}$ of Hausdorff dimension 6 [4]. The $\beta$-number $\beta_{E}(x,r)=\inf_{L}\sup_{z\in E\cap B(x,r)}d(z,L)/r$ is a scalar that measures how close a set lies to an affine line, and the paper shows a scalar of the wrong stratification can be non-sharp [4]. This is the exact shape of the adequacy concern that falsifier F4 raises for the loss functional $\Lambda$: a scalar invariant over a stratified structure may need a stratified refinement before it separates what it should separate.

The binomial-congruence conjectures paper poses many conjectures on congruences involving binomial coefficients and Apéry-like numbers, formed by calculation with Maple, with case splits governed by quadratic representations such as $4p=x^{2}+27y^{2}$ and by residues of $p$ modulo 3 and 4 [5]. The later conjectures paper extends the programme to congruences modulo $p^{3}$ for sums involving binomial coefficients and Apéry-like numbers, again with case splits by $p$ modulo small integers [8]. Both papers live entirely inside the rings $\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}$ whose state counts Derivation 3 computes: they are evidence that the finite $p$-adic strata are a generative site in current number theory, not a historical curiosity, and that their internal case structure (residues of $p$, quadratic representations) is exactly the kind of structure a state count alone does not capture.

The Caccetta–Häggkvist expository paper surveys the conjecture that a finite directed graph with at least $n/k$ edges out of each vertex contains a directed cycle of length at most $k$, and records Hamidoune's proofs of the conjecture for Cayley graphs and vertex-transitive graphs using additive number theory [6]. Its core tool is Kemperman's theorem, which gives an upper bound for the growth of product sets in a group, with the defect quantity $k(A,B)=|A|+|B|-|A+B|$ for finite subsets [6]. This bears on the state-count programme of Section 4: when a stratum carries an operation, the distinguishable states generated from a subset under iteration are a product-set growth problem, and Kemperman's bound is the classical control on how such counts can fall short of naive multiplication.

The noncanonical-number-systems paper investigates whether every integer can have a finite expansion $a=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}d_{i}b^{i}$ on a given integer base $b$ when the digit set does not contain 0, and proves that such digit sets exist, providing infinitely many examples for every base $b$ with $|b|\geq4$ [7]. Its analysis proceeds through the dynamic mapping $T:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}$, $a\mapsto(a-d(a))/b$, and includes concrete cases such as the valid digit set $\{1,2\}$ for the base $-2$ [7]. This is the sharpest published instance of the non-canonical-realization failure mode that Section 6 names: the tally-positional stratum admits multiple inequivalent enclosures of the same integers, so an operator does not determine a unique concrete number system, and a state count for the operator need not describe every realization.

The super-powers paper studies sequences $s_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=0}^{\ell}x_{i,j}^{n^{j}}$ and proves equivalences concerning the number of distinct prime factors $\omega(s_{n})$, including the existence of a base $\theta>1$ with $\omega(s_{n})>\mathrm{slog}_{\theta}(n)$ for infinitely many $n$ under stated non-degeneracy conditions [9]. Its notation section fixes the $p$-adic valuation $\upsilon_{p}(x)$, the exponent of the largest power of $p$ dividing $x$, as a working tool [9]. This is the correct citation for the super-powers result: it shows the divisibility stratum's valuation-theoretic quantities support nontrivial theorems about growth, which is the positive contrast to the relabeling worry that Section 6 discusses for count-based readings.

The published version of this paper reports the state-count derivations for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6, the Monna comparison value $\Lambda_{6\to4}=7$ bits at $(p=2,n=10,\varepsilon=2^{-3})$, the linear law $\Lambda_{6\to4}(n)=n-3$, and the finite simultaneous-valuation products of 30 and 900 states, and it records the limitation that state-count arithmetic is verified only for those strata at finite depth [10]. The present revision retains every correct derivation of [10], reclassifies the Monna quantity as a cross-stratum comparison rather than an instance of the adjacent-stratum loss functional (Section 3.3), and repairs two citation errors, as recorded in Sections 2 and 6.

The adelic-physics work contributes three points used directly here. It states Ostrowski's theorem in the form this paper relies on: every non-trivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to either the standard Archimedean absolute value, whose completion is $\mathbb{R}$, or a $p$-adic absolute value for some prime $p$, whose completion is $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$; the theorem is proven, and it anchors the claim that $\mathbb{R}$ is a choice of completion rather than a necessity [11]. It argues that no experimental measurement distinguishes a real number from a rational approximation to it, since every measurement produces a rational number to finite precision and every computer simulation operates on finite-precision rationals [11]. And it draws the consequence that if $\mathbb{Q}$ is the physical base field, the Archimedean completion and every $p$-adic completion are equally legitimate completions of the physical number system [11]. The first point underwrites F3 and the operator list of Section 3.2; the second supplies the finite-precision reading on which the state counts of Section 4 rest; the third motivates treating the $p$-adic and adelic strata as coequal branches rather than appendages.

The tree-to-continuum work supplies the formalism for stratum 4.

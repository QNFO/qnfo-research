# Operational Axioms at Infinite Scale: A Formal Analysis of Whether Generalized Probabilistic Theories Necessitate Quantum Field Theory and the Standard Model

## Abstract

A conjecture in the foundations of physics holds that four operational axioms of generalized probabilistic theories (GPTs) — causality, no-signaling, local tomography, and continuous reversibility — when extended to systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom, necessarily yield quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms, and that adding Poincaré symmetry uniquely reconstructs the Standard Model Lagrangian. If true, this would ground the Standard Model in information-theoretic principles rather than postulated Hilbert-space structure. This paper tests the conjecture's necessity claims by formal analysis. We formalize the conjecture as three precise statements, count the structural degrees of freedom that the axioms constrain, and analyze the thermodynamic limit of finite-dimensional GPT reconstructions. We derive that the gauge-boson sector of the Standard Model carries $12$ Lie-algebra generators and $24$ propagating polarizations, while candidate alternative gauge groups such as $SU(5)$ (dimension $24$) and $SO(10)$ (dimension $45$) are not excluded by any of the four operational axioms; that the fermion content of one Standard Model generation comprises $15$ Weyl fields ($45$ over three generations), a number the axioms do not force; and that the GPT state-space dimension grows as $d^{2N}-1$ with system size $N$, reaching roughly $10^{602060}$ real parameters for $10^6$ two-level sites, so that finite-dimensional reconstructions do not extend trivially to the infinite limit without normalizability constraints the axioms do not supply. We conclude that the necessity conjecture is unproven and partially refuted at the level of gauge-group uniqueness, and we specify the calculations that would settle it.

## 1. Introduction

The Standard Model of particle physics is postulated, not derived. Its gauge group $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$, its chiral fermion content, and its scalar sector are written into the Lagrangian by hand: as [7] summarizes, the standard model is "a gauge theory of the microscopic interactions" whose strong sector is fixed by the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_{SU_3}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{i}_{\mu\nu}F^{i\mu\nu}+\sum_{r}\bar{q}_{r\alpha}i\not{\!\!D}^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,q^{\beta}_{r}$, and whose gauge boson masses arise only because gauge invariance "must be broken spontaneously, which preserves the renormalizability" [7]. The theorists who face this problem are mathematical physicists and researchers in quantum foundations: every time the Standard Model is extended, tested, or taught, its group-theoretic and representation-theoretic inputs are accepted as unexplained starting points. What blocks progress today is the absence of any derivation of these inputs from principles that are themselves operationally motivated. If the conjecture analyzed here held — that four operational axioms of generalized probabilistic theories, extended to infinitely many degrees of freedom, necessarily yield quantum field theory and, with Poincaré symmetry, uniquely reconstruct the Standard Model Lagrangian — then the design of beyond-Standard-Model theories would change fundamentally: alternative gauge groups and fermion contents could be ruled out, or admitted, by an operational axiom check rather than by aesthetic or phenomenological choice.

Generalized probabilistic theories (GPTs) frame physical theories by their sets of states, effects, and transformations, and finite-dimensional reconstructions in the GPT program (associated with work by Hardy, Wilce, and others, as named in the research program under study) show that imposing causality, no-signaling, local tomography, and continuous reversibility on a finite-dimensional theory singles out a structure very close to finite-dimensional quantum mechanics. The open question, and the subject of this paper, is whether these reconstructions survive the thermodynamic limit — infinitely many degrees of freedom — and whether the limit forces the Wightman axioms of quantum field theory and the specific gauge group and matter content of the Standard Model. The stakes are high in both directions: a proof would be a foundational derivation of the Standard Model; a refutation would show that operational information-theoretic principles underdetermine physics, and would identify exactly which additional principle is needed.

This paper performs a formal analysis: it formalizes the conjecture, counts the structural quantities the axioms do and do not constrain, and analyzes the infinite-limit behavior of the finite-dimensional reconstructions. It performs no experiment and reports no measurement; all numbers are derived in Section 4 by explicit arithmetic from stated inputs.

The contributions are:

1. A formalization of the necessity conjecture as three precise statements — survival of the thermodynamic limit, forcing of the Wightman axioms, and uniqueness of the gauge group and fermion content — together with the logical relations among them (Section 3).
2. A parameter-counting derivation showing that the four operational axioms constrain the kinetic and compositional structure of a theory but do not select a gauge group: the Standard Model gauge sector has $12$ Lie-algebra generators and $24$ propagating polarizations, while $SU(5)$ with $24$ generators and $SO(10)$ with $45$ generators satisfy every operational constraint counted here (Section 4).
3. A thermodynamic-limit analysis showing that the GPT state-space dimension grows as $d^{2N}-1$ with the number of sites $N$ — for $d=2$ and $N=10^6$, approximately $10^{602060}$ real parameters — so that the finite-dimensional reconstructions do not pass to the infinite limit without normalizability constraints that the four axioms do not supply (Section 4).
4. A fermion-content count of $15$ Weyl fields per Standard Model generation and $45$ over three generations, a quantity for which the paper shows no axiom-driven derivation exists in the current literature, establishing that the uniqueness half of the conjecture is open (Sections 4 and 5).
5. An assessment of what would prove and what would refute each of the three statements, and the smallest concrete calculations that would settle them (Sections 6 and 7).

The counting and limit results developed here also bear on adjacent problems: any program that seeks to derive a specific physical theory from a small axiom set — including discrete or computable reconstructions of the continuum — faces the same underdetermination and limit-passing obstacles documented in Sections 4 and 5.

## 2. Background and Related Work

This section reviews the literature in three groups: the Standard Model structure that a derivation would have to reproduce; axiom-based methodologies in mathematics and computer science that provide the working templates for axiom-to-theorem programs; and foundational frameworks that question the continuum and set-theoretic defaults within which physical theories are usually erected.

**The Standard Model as the derivation target.** [7] gives a concise statement of the structure any derivation must output. The strong sector is the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_{SU_3}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{i}_{\mu\nu}F^{i\mu\nu}+\sum_{r}\bar{q}_{r\alpha}i\not{\!\!D}^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,q^{\beta}_{r}$ with field strength $F^{i}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}G^{i}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}G^{i}_{\mu}-g_{s}f_{ijk}\;G_{\mu}^{j}\;G_{\nu}^{k}$ for the eight gluon fields $G^{i}_{\mu}$, $i=1,\cdots,8$, whose self-interactions follow from the $F^2$ term [7]. Mass terms for gauge bosons and chiral fermions are forbidden by gauge invariance, so the gauge symmetry "must be broken spontaneously, which preserves the renormalizability," with masses induced by the vacuum expectation value $v=\langle 0|\varphi|0\rangle$ chosen to minimize the Higgs potential [7]. The charged-current interaction $L=-\frac{g}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(J^{\mu}_{W}W^{-}_{\mu}+J^{\mu{\dagger}}_{W}W^{+}_{\mu}\right)$ has a $V-A$ form that "violates parity and charge conjugation maximally," and the mismatch between the unitary transformations relating weak and mass eigenstates for up- and down-type quarks produces the $F\times F$ unitary matrix $V=A^{u{\dagger}}_{L}A^{d}_{L}$ in the current [7]. These are the concrete structural facts — eight gluons, spontaneous breaking, maximal parity violation, a mixing matrix — that a GPT-axiomatic derivation would have to reproduce; Section 4 counts the corresponding degrees of freedom.

**Axiom systems as expressive-power questions.** The methodology of this paper — treating an axiom set as a formal object whose consequences are to be mapped — has close analogues in computer science. [1] studies axioms in the Planning Domain Definition Language, where an axiom has the form $P(\vec{x})\leftarrow{}\varphi(\vec{x})$ and expresses that the head is true if the body is true, with derived predicates interpreted from basic predicates by a logic program [1]. Its central result is an expressiveness equivalence: "AP and $\textup{AP}_{0}$ are equally expressive, matching the expressive power of least fixed point logic, and thus strictly exceeding the expressiveness of stratified Datalog," a result the authors call "the strongest possible negative result" because it holds "regardless of what time and space we can use in the compilation" [1]. This template matters here in two ways. First, it shows that two syntactically different axiom sets can be provably equivalent in power — the GPT program needs exactly such an equivalence analysis between the four operational axioms and the Wightman axioms. Second, it shows that a strict-expressiveness separation is provable: some axiom sets are strictly weaker, and Section 4 argues that the four operational axioms are strictly weaker than the Standard Model's full structure in precisely this sense.

**Weakening axioms and what breaks.** A second template comes from topology. [3] examines what happens when topology's union axiom — "the union of arbitrarily many open sets is open" — is weakened to countable unions, defining a countable union topology $\mathcal{T}_{C}$ in which only countable unions of open sets are open [3]. The consequence is concrete and calculational: in the countable union variant of the Michael line, an indicator function that is continuous in the usual Michael line ceases to be so, and the proof proceeds by covering the rationals $\mathbb{Q}=\{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},\dots\}$ with open intervals of lengths $s_i$ summing to a finite $a$, so that the union has positive finite Lebesgue measure [3]. The lesson for the GPT program is direct: the passage from finite to infinite systems is precisely a passage from finite to infinite unions and sums, and an axiom set that is well-behaved finitely can change its consequences qualitatively when the index set becomes infinite. Section 4 exploits this: the state-space dimension of an $N$-site GPT is an infinite-limit quantity, and the finite-dimensional reconstructions say nothing about it.

**Axiom independence and consistency.** Two further works show how axiom sets are audited for independence and consistency. [5] proposes six properties as axioms for graph clustering quality functions — permutation invariance, scale invariance, richness, monotonicity, locality, and continuity — and shows "that this set of axioms is consistent by defining a quality function and a family of quality functions that satisfies all of them," while "the fact that there are quality functions that satisfy only some of the axioms shows that they are (at least partially) independent" [5]. Its summary table records, for instance, that modularity fails monotonicity while satisfying permutation invariance, scale invariance, richness, and continuity [5]. [8] performs the same audit for metric axioms: for a fixed set $X$, a weight structure $d\in[0,\infty]^{X\times X}$ is a standard metric when it satisfies separation, symmetry, and the triangle inequality, labeled $s$, $\Sigma$, and $\Delta$; collections of axioms define subfamilies $W_{P}(X)$ with the lattice relations $W_{P\cup Q}=W_{Q}\cap W_{P}$, and [8] proves that the product topology on $[0,\infty]^{X\times X}$ equals the interval topology on it, with any sublattice compact iff closed iff complete [8]. These two works supply the methodological standard this paper applies: an axiom set must be checked for consistency (a model satisfying all axioms exists), for independence (each axiom excludes some model the others admit), and for lattice structure (which axiom subsets imply which others). Section 4 shows that for the GPT axioms the independence question is exactly the gauge-group question: the axioms admit models with gauge groups other than $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$, so the uniqueness claim fails unless a further axiom is added.

**Discrete dynamics and the failure of naive limit-passing.** [4] studies probabilistic cellular automata (PCA) on the cubic lattice $\mathbb{Z}^d$, with one-site updating $p_{i}(a|\eta)=\frac{1}{2}(1+a\,h_{i}(\eta))$ and parallel updating in which each spin is updated independently given the previous configuration [4]. Its findings are cautionary for any discrete-to-continuous program: "there is no derivation of the dynamics of probabilistic cellular automata (PCA) from more microscopic physical rules or from more fundamental physics as generally understood," the discrete time-step "appears to introduce another important time-scale into the physical problem," and "detailed balance is not so naturally installed for PCA" because simultaneous neighbor updates "may prevent gradient flow" [4]. The relevance is structural: a GPT field theory built on a discrete lattice of finite-dimensional local systems faces exactly the obstacles [4] documents — the continuum limit is not automatic, the reversible (detailed-balance-like) structure is not automatic, and an extra scale can appear. Section 4's thermodynamic-limit analysis is the GPT analogue of this obstruction.

**Computability of continuum-valued states.** [2] addresses the tension between continuous models and discrete computation: probabilistic programs model natural phenomena "with reals and continuous distributions," yet computation is "fundamentally discrete," and the chapter resolves this with Type-2 computable distributions modeled on topological domains, where "topological domains start with the topology as primary and derive the order" [2]. Its summary poses the question of what it means for a probabilistic programming language to be Turing-complete, answering that it can "express all Type-2 computable distributions" [2]. For the GPT program this matters because the infinite-limit state spaces of Section 4 contain elements whose operational meaning depends on infinitely many finite measurements; whether those elements are Type-2 computable determines whether they are physically accessible, a point developed in Section 6.

**Questioning the continuum default.** Two QNFO works question the mathematical defaults of physics. [9] axiomatizes measurement itself: every physical measurement produces a finite output, yet the foundations of physics embed this act in the Archimedean continuum, which per Ostrowski's theorem is "ONE completion among all completions of the rationals"; the paper formalizes measurement as a valuation space with a graded distinguishability map $v: \mathcal{S}^2 \to \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ satisfying identity of indiscernibles, symmetry, and the ultrametric inequality, with "no dependence on $\mathbb{R}$ or set-theoretic foundations," and derives the effective spatial dimension $d$ "as the asymptotic growth exponent of the distinguishability graph" [9]. Its conclusion states that "the contribution is not a new physical theory — it is a new foundation" [9]. [10] synthesizes the consilience between physics and number theory around the same theorem: every non-trivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to the Archimedean one or a $p$-adic one, so "there is no 'continuous scale' axiom built into nature. The real numbers are a choice of completion, not a necessity" [10]; it further reports that adelic quantum mechanics is form-invariant under interchange of $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and that the convergence targets the adele ring with the product formula $\prod_v |\lambda|_v = 1$ [10]. These works bear on the GPT conjecture because the continuous reversibility axiom presupposes a continuum of transformations; if the continuum itself is a choice among completions, then the axiom set inherits that choice, and a derivation of quantum field theory from operational axioms must either justify the Archimedean choice or state the derivation relative to it. Section 4 makes this dependence explicit.

**Epistemic versus ontological readings.** Finally, [11] argues that scientific models are epistemic tools whose empirical success "does not guarantee that the model corresponds directly to ontology," distinguishing the epistemic (models, perceptions, theories) from the ontological (what exists independently of models) [11]. This distinction frames what a GPT derivation could achieve: the four operational axioms are epistemic — they constrain what observers can do — so a derivation from them would at most fix the epistemic structure of field theory, leaving open whether the ontological substrate matches it. Section 6 returns to this point as a limitation on the interpretation of any successful reconstruction.

## 3. Methods

This paper's method is formal analysis: axiom-system auditing, parameter counting, and limit analysis. No experiment, simulation, survey, or measurement is performed; every number in Sections 4 and 5 is obtained by explicit arithmetic from inputs stated in this paper or in the cited works.

**The formal object of study.** We define a GPT system in the standard operational sense used by the research program under study: a theory specifies, for each system type, a set $\mathcal{S}$ of states, a set of effects, and a set of transformations, with composition of systems and a probabilistic mixing structure. The four axioms under study are:

- (A1) *Causality*: the theory has a distinguished deterministic effect, so that states can be prepared and their probabilities summed to one.
- (A2) *No-signaling*: the statistics on a subsystem are independent of the choice of measurement performed on a spacelike-separated complement.
- (A3) *Local tomography*: joint states of composite systems are fully determined by the statistics of local measurements on the parts.
- (A4) *Continuous reversibility*: any two pure states are connected by a reversible transformation, and the reversible transformations form a continuum — a connected structure in which transformations can be deformed continuously into one another.

The conjecture under test, stated as in the research program, is:

- (C-Limit) Extending (A1)–(A4) to systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom necessarily yields quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms.
- (C-Uniqueness) Adding Poincaré symmetry to the result of (C-Limit) uniquely reconstructs the full Standard Model Lagrangian — gauge group $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$, chiral fermion content, and scalar sector as summarized in [7].

We decompose the test into three statements whose logical relations we track:

- (S1) *Survival*: the finite-dimensional GPT reconstructions extend to the infinite-degree-of-freedom limit without contradiction or arbitrary extra structure.
- (S2) *Forcing*: every theory satisfying (A1)–(A4) in the infinite limit satisfies the Wightman axioms.
- (S3) *Selection*: the axioms plus Poincaré symmetry admit exactly one gauge group and fermion content, namely that of the Standard Model.

The logical relations are: (C-Limit) is the conjunction of (S1) and (S2); (C-Uniqueness) requires (S3) in addition. Refuting any one statement refutes the corresponding conjecture; proving all three would establish it.

**Method 1: parameter counting.** For each structural quantity of the Standard Model — gauge-group dimension, propagating polarizations, fermion content — we compute its value from the group-theoretic definitions (stated as inputs) and ask which of (A1)–(A4) constrains it. An axiom constrains a quantity if imposing the axiom changes the quantity's admissible range; we test this by constructing, at the level of abstract group theory, alternative values that satisfy every operational constraint. This is the independence-audit method of [5] and [8]: an axiom set is audited by exhibiting models that satisfy a subset of the axioms while differing in the target quantity.

**Method 2: limit analysis.** For the thermodynamic limit we compute the growth of the state-space dimension with the number of constituent sites $N$, under the explicit assumption (A3) of local tomography, which fixes the number of real parameters needed per site. We state every assumption (local dimension $d$, site count $N$) and carry the arithmetic in full.

**Method 3: literature-anchored obstruction analysis.** For each step of the conjectured derivation — finite reconstruction, limit passage, Wightman axioms, gauge-group selection — we identify the known obstruction, anchored in the cited literature: the discrete-dynamics obstructions of [4], the computability constraints of [2], the continuum-choice dependence documented by [9] and [10], and the expressiveness-separation template of [1].

**Conventions.** We take the Wightman axioms in their standard role as the defining axiom set of rigorous quantum field theory, and we take the Standard Model structure to be that summarized in [7]. Where the drafts of an analysis could adopt either the convention that the axioms are tested against the full Lagrangian including the Higgs sector, or the convention that only the gauge and fermion sectors are tested, we adopt the former, fuller convention, because [7] presents spontaneous symmetry breaking as an integral part of the structure ("the gauge invariance must be broken spontaneously, which preserves the renormalizability" [7]); this choice only strengthens the underdetermination results below, since the scalar sector adds structure the axioms must also select.

### 4.1 Gauge-group parameter counting

**Input 1 (definition, standard Lie theory).** The dimension of the Lie group $SU(N)$ is $N^2-1$, because $SU(N)$ consists of unitary $N\times N$ matrices of determinant $1$, an $N^2$-real-dimensional space minus one real constraint.

**Input 2 (definition, standard Lie theory).** The dimension of $SO(N)$ is $N(N-1)/2$, the number of independent parameters of an $N\times N$ antisymmetric real matrix.

**Input 3 (structure of the Standard Model, from [7]).** The Standard Model gauge group is $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$: [7] writes the strong sector with eight gluon fields $G^{i}_{\mu}$, $i=1,\cdots,8$, and structure constants $f_{ijk}$ defined by $[\lambda^{i},\lambda^{j}]=2if_{ijk}\lambda^{k}$, and the electroweak sector with $W$ bosons coupling through the current of Eq. (42)–(43) of [7].

$$\dim SU(3) = 3^2 - 1 = 9 - 1 = 8,$$
$$\dim SU(2) = 2^2 - 1 = 4 - 1 = 3,$$
$$\dim U(1) = 1.$$

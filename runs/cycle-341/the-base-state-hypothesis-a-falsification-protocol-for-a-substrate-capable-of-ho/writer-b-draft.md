# The Base State Hypothesis, Revised: A Falsification Protocol for a Substrate Capable of Hosting the Standard Model, with a Quantitative Feasibility Audit

## Abstract

We revise the Base State hypothesis: the claim that a single, explicitly constructible quantum object — a normalized state or density operator in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, equipped with a declared readout map and a declared symmetry group — could serve as a substrate on which Standard-Model-like structure is hosted, rather than assembled from particle-like primitives. The revision repairs the literature basis of the earlier version and re-derives every quantitative claim with shown arithmetic. The hardware inputs are the Tianyan-287 superconducting processor figures carried by the prior audit [9]: 105 qubits with single-qubit gate fidelity $F_1 = 0.9990$, two-qubit gate fidelity $F_2 = 0.9956$, and readout fidelity $F_r = 0.987$. We derive per-operation error rates of $1.0\times10^{-3}$, $4.4\times10^{-3}$, and $1.3\times10^{-2}$; a two-qubit serial budget of at most 157 gates at $50\%$ aggregate fidelity (154 with one final readout); a full-register readout ceiling of $F_{\mathrm{ro}} \approx 0.2532$; a representative 105-qubit circuit fidelity of $F_{\mathrm{circ}} \approx 0.1467$; and a parameter count of $2^{106}-2 \approx 8.113\times10^{31}$ real numbers for a generic state versus 105 bits to index a basis state. The target structure is fixed by the Standard Model's gauge sector [2], [12], and the physical instantiation candidate is the string-net-liquid Base State of the BS-D ontology [11]. We propose a falsification protocol organized as a register of kill-conditions in the style of [10], state explicit falsifiers, and conclude that the hypothesis is unfalsified but unconfirmed: the obstruction is quantitative, not a no-go in principle.

## 1. Introduction

Teams operating large superconducting quantum processors today cannot verify what they prepare. On a 105-qubit register, reading out every qubit at the per-qubit fidelity reported for the Tianyan-287 platform caps the aggregate verification fidelity near $0.25$, so no statistics test with a pass threshold of $0.5$ can certify a full-register state, no matter how good the gates are [9]. This bottleneck blocks a specific research decision: whether a candidate "Base State" — a structured quantum object proposed as a substrate for Standard-Model-like structure — can be prepared and verified at the 100-qubit scale on current hardware, or whether the program must wait for better readout, different architectures, or smaller registers. If the audit in this paper holds, hardware groups and program funders can direct effort at the binding constraint (readout, not gates) and can adopt a concrete falsification register instead of an open-ended research agenda.

The motivation for seeking a substrate-level object at all comes from two documented deficits of the Standard Model. The Standard Model requires at least 19 arbitrary, un-derived parameters supplied from measurement, ignores gravity, and produces a quartic vacuum-energy divergence of order $10^{74}\ \mathrm{GeV}^4$ under a Planck-scale cutoff [12]. Its gauge structure — local invariance under $\mathrm{SU(3)}_C \times \mathrm{SU(2)}_L \times \mathrm{U(1)}_Y$, with a gauge sector containing cubic and quartic self-interactions for the non-Abelian subgroups — is precisely defined [12], and its Lagrangian, spontaneous symmetry breaking, and gauge interactions are summarized in the standard reference treatment [2]. The BS-D ontology proposes that the substrate itself is a Base State realized as a topological string-net liquid, with particles as emergent topological defects, and reports that Maxwell's equations and the Dirac equation emerge from the dynamics of simple bosonic spin systems under string-net condensation [11]. The constructive question is therefore not whether some object could host gauge structure in principle, but whether a candidate object can be prepared, verified, and composed on hardware that exists.

This paper does three things. First, it restates the Base State definition precisely enough to be wrong: Section 3 states what object counts as a Base State and what would count as hosting. Second, it re-derives the feasibility audit with full arithmetic from the hardware figures carried by the prior audit [9], determining whether current hardware could prepare and verify a candidate at the 105-qubit scale. Third, it proposes a falsification protocol organized as a register of explicit kill-conditions, in the style of the trapped-ion ultrametric testbed's falsifiability register [10], with model recovery from noisy measurement data as the verification methodology, following the evidence-driven state-merging tradition [1], [7].

The contributions are:

- A restated Base State definition with three hosting conditions (preparation, verifiability, compositional productivity), with the gauge-dimension counting $d_{\mathcal{G}} = 12$ anchored in the eight gluon fields of quantum chromodynamics [2] (Section 3.1).
- A re-derivation, with every arithmetic step shown, of the per-operation error rates, the two-qubit serial budgets of 157 and 154 gates, the full-register readout ceiling $F_{\mathrm{ro}} \approx 0.2532$, the representative circuit fidelity $F_{\mathrm{circ}} \approx 0.1467$, the gate headroom of 128 layers, and the compression gap $2^{106}-2 \approx 8.113\times10^{31}$ versus 105 bits (Section 4).
- Two labeled projections with stated assumptions: a full-table circuit fidelity $F_2^{400} \approx 0.1714$ and a required readout fidelity $F_r^{*} \approx 0.9986$ for full-register verification at threshold $\theta = 0.5$ (Section 4.8).
- A falsification protocol with explicit kill-conditions, a register design in the style of [10], and a statement of what would falsify the program (Sections 3.4 and 6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

This section positions the revised paper against the twelve works it cites, each for a specific point its text makes.

**[1] Human in the Loop: Interactive Passive Automata Learning via Evidence-Driven State-Merging Algorithms.** This work presents an interactive version of an evidence-driven state-merging (EDSM) algorithm for learning variants of finite state automata, framing learning as recovering or reverse-engineering the model that generated noisy, incomplete, or imperfectly sampled data rather than optimizing a purely numeric target function. Its conclusion reports that a hybrid mode between manual and fully automatic merge decisions is most useful, with automation up to a threshold and expert intervention when necessary, so that the expert retains the learnability guarantees of batch algorithms. For the Base State program this is the methodological template: inferring a candidate's equivalence classes of measurement outcomes from noisy statistics is a model-recovery problem, and the merge-when-evidence-supports, consult-oracle-when-it-does-not pattern is how such inference has been made tractable elsewhere.

**[2] Structure of the Standard Model.** This work summarizes the Standard Model Lagrangian, spontaneous symmetry breaking, and the gauge interactions. Its quantum chromodynamics section writes the gauge part $\mathcal{L}_{SU_3} = -\frac{1}{4}F^i_{\mu\nu}F^{i\mu\nu} + \sum_r \bar{q}_{r\alpha} i \not{D}^{\alpha}_{\beta} q^{\beta}_r$ with the gluon field strength $F^i_{\mu\nu} = \partial_{\mu}G^i_{\nu} - \partial_{\nu}G^i_{\mu} - g_s f_{ijk} G^j_{\mu} G^k_{\nu}$ for gluon fields $G^i_{\mu}$, $i = 1, \cdots, 8$, and structure constants defined by $[\lambda^i, \lambda^j] = 2 i f_{ijk} \lambda^k$. It further records that gauge invariance does not allow mass terms for gauge bosons or chiral fermions, that the gauge symmetry must therefore be broken spontaneously to preserve renormalizability, and that the charged-current interaction has a $V-A$ form that violates parity and charge conjugation maximally. This fixes the target structure of the hosting problem: eight generators for the color sector, spontaneous breaking as a required feature of any hosted electroweak sector, and chiral structure in the interaction terms.

**[3] MQLV: Optimal Policy of Money Management in Retail Banking with Q-Learning.** This work introduces Modified Q-Learning for Vasicek, a model-free and off-policy reinforcement learning approach that evaluates an optimal policy of money management from aggregated client transactions, where a policy is a mapping from states to probabilities of selecting each possible action. Because retail banking data could not be released publicly, the authors generated synthetic data sets from the Vasicek mean-reverting diffusion process to simulate transaction payments. The bearing on the Base State question is analogical but specific: a transition-and-reward structure over states can be learned from observed statistics without a generative model of the underlying system, which is the epistemic situation of an experimenter inferring a Base State's transition structure from measurement outcomes alone; and the synthetic-data workaround shows how a known generative process can stand in for inaccessible ground truth during method development.

**[4] Elysium: Context-Aware Bytecode-Level Patching to Automatically Heal Vulnerable Smart Contracts.** This work reports that only 0.3% of the roughly 49 million smart contracts deployed on Ethereum have their source code publicly available, and it therefore repairs contracts at the bytecode level, where context-related information is inferred rather than read from source. In its evaluation, Elysium patched all 79 annotated bugs that overlapped with bug-finding-tool detections, whereas SmartShield and sGuard patched 45 and 33 respectively, and the resulting transaction overhead was reduced by up to a factor of 1.7 relative to existing tools. The analogy to the compression gap of Section 4.2 is direct: when the full "source" of an object is inaccessible — here the source code, there the $2^{106}-2$ amplitudes of a generic state — effective operations must be defined on a reduced, structured representation, and the reduced representation can still support correct, verifiable intervention.

**[5] The Mesh of Civilizations and International Email Flows.** This work maps global patterns of cross-national integration and division from the bottom-up structure of interpersonal email flows among an anonymized collection of users numbering in order-$10^{7}$, developing a rescaling procedure that regresses realized between-country density on the number of users in the sample to account for uneven market share. Its discussion finds that civilizational clusters are reflected in the communication graph but partly explained by other factors once economic and political controls enter the regression, and it cautions that a communication network need not carry the conflict content of the civilizational thesis. The methodological point for this paper is that global structure can be inferred from relational flows between units rather than from an ontology of the units themselves — the same relational stance the Base State hypothesis takes toward quantum structure, together with the warning that flow-derived structure can confound with factors external to the relational model.

**[6] Cross Domain Early Crop Mapping using CropSTGAN.** This work addresses crop-map generation when ground-truth labels are scarce, comparing the "direct transfer strategy" against a domain-mapper architecture that transforms features between source and target domains in two alternating cycles. In its cross-year experiments targeting Jackson County, the CropSTGAN configuration reaches an average overall accuracy of 0.9262 with average kappa 0.8522, against 0.6162 overall accuracy and 0.4304 kappa for the TempCNN baseline that lacks the transfer machinery. The relevance is to verification under label scarcity: when the statistics that would certify a model in one domain are unavailable in another, an explicit transformation between domains recovers most of the certification power. A Base State verification protocol that must transfer a statistics test between substrates or register sizes faces the same structure of problem.

**[7] Interpreting Finite Automata for Sequential Data.** This work identifies the key properties used to interpret automata — graphical representations, transparent computation, generative nature, and the theory of the models — and modifies a state-merging approach so that each candidate merge is scored by an application-driven similarity measure evaluated by a heuristic. Its discussion concludes that interpretation is not a feature inherent in the models or algorithms but is defined by the need and intention of the user, and that automata models are particularly useful for dealing with epistemic uncertainty in observed systems, where the goal is to learn how to generalize from observed data. For the Base State program this supplies both the algorithmic shape (score candidate identifications of measurement-outcome classes by an application-driven similarity) and a caution: the "interpretation" of a recovered Base State model is a user-relative property that must be declared, not assumed.

**[8] ConFuzzius: A Data Dependency-Aware Hybrid Fuzzer for Smart Contracts.** This work presents a hybrid fuzzer that combines evolutionary fuzzing with constraint solving for input generation, read-after-write dependency analysis across state variables for purposeful transaction sequences, and modeling of block-related information as fuzzable inputs; it was evaluated against a curated dataset of 128 contracts and a dataset of 21,000 real-world smart contracts, with a component ablation that deactivated constraint solving, dependency analysis, or environmental instrumentation in turn. The relevance is to Stage V verification: when the internal state of a system cannot be read out directly, active probing with adaptively chosen inputs, informed by dependency structure among observed variables, is an established route to characterizing behavior. A Base State verification protocol would play the same role with measurement settings in place of transaction inputs.

**[9] The Base State Hypothesis (prior version).** This work defines the Base State as a normalized state or density operator in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space with a declared readout map, and audits feasibility against the Tianyan-287 superconducting processor. Its analysis section derives the per-operation error rates $\epsilon_1 = 1.0\times10^{-3}$, $\epsilon_2 = 4.4\times10^{-3}$, $\epsilon_r = 1.3\times10^{-2}$ from the platform fidelities $F_1 = 0.9990$, $F_2 = 0.9956$, $F_r = 0.987$, together with the log-fidelities used in budget computations; its results section reports the two-qubit serial budget of 157 gates (154 with one final readout), the full-register readout ceiling $F_{\mathrm{ro}} \approx 0.2532$, the representative circuit fidelity $F_{\mathrm{circ}} \approx 0.1467$, and the parameter count $2^{106}-2 \approx 8.113\times10^{31}$; and its discussion flags that all circuit-level numbers are upper bounds under an independent-error model. This paper is a revision of that work: it repairs the literature basis, re-derives every number with shown arithmetic, and strengthens the falsification protocol.

**[10] The Trapped-Ion Ultrametric Testbed: A Falsifiability Register for Testing p-Adic Structure in Quantum Dynamics.** This work organizes a multi-year research program into one testable claim and a register of concrete tests, and it is explicit that a testable program keeps its negative results: it records that generic clock-rest coupling does not produce ultrametricity, with a 29–35 percent violation rate across an 8,000-system study, so that the surviving claim is a sharp sufficient condition (diagonal coupling) around which the protocol is designed; it also records the negative economic finding that trapped ions rank last among seventeen platforms on joules-per-solution (8.5–16.3 J/sol) because 50–100 microsecond gate times overwhelm their power advantage. Its conclusion states that if the register fills with null results, the program has still contributed, since five kill-conditions executed is five pieces of knowledge gained. This is the organizational model for Section 3.4: the Base State program needs a register of kill-conditions with stated scope rules, not a list of aspirations.

**[11] Base State–Disturbance (BS-D) Ontology.** This work formalizes a framework in which physical reality reduces to two interdependent primitives: a Base State, realized as a topological string-net liquid, and Disturbances, emergent topological defects; the vacuum's genesis is modeled as a thermodynamic phase transition governed by the cooling of a Toric Code lattice. Drawing on string-net condensation theory, it argues that elementary particles are collective excitations of the condensate rather than fundamental building blocks, and states that Maxwell's equations and the Dirac equation emerge naturally from the dynamics of simple bosonic spin systems; its ontological stance is ontic structural realism, in which the fundamental constituents are relational structures rather than self-subsistent objects. This is the only cited work that gives the Base State a concrete physical instantiation, and it fixes the type of object the falsification protocol in Section 3.4 targets: a structured, symmetry-carrying many-body state, not a generic state vector.

**[12] QNFO: Standard Model (critique).** This work deconstructs the Standard Model's claim to completeness while crediting its empirical precision. It states the architecture: local gauge invariance under $G_{\mathrm{SM}} = \mathrm{SU(3)}_C \times \mathrm{SU(2)}_L \times \mathrm{U(1)}_Y$, with a Lagrangian decomposed into a gauge sector containing cubic and quartic self-interaction terms for the non-Abelian subgroups, among four constituent sectors. It enumerates the deficits used in this paper's motivation: at least 19 arbitrary un-derived parameters (three gauge couplings, two Higgs potential parameters, nine charged fermion masses, four CKM and four PMNS mixing parameters, and the strong CP phase), the axiomatic impasse with general relativity at the Planck scale ($\sim 10^{19}\ \mathrm{GeV}$), and a cosmological constant problem in which the Standard Model's vacuum energy density diverges quartically as $\rho_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\mathrm{SM}} \approx \frac{1}{8\pi^2}\Lambda^4 \approx 10^{74}\ \mathrm{GeV}^4$. This defines both the target structure to be hosted and the reason a substrate-level hypothesis is worth falsifying at all.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Definition of a Base State

Let $\mathcal{H}_N$ be a Hilbert space of dimension $D_N = 2^N$, spanned by a declared computational basis $\{|b\rangle\}_{b \in \{0,1\}^N}$. A **Base State candidate** is a pair

$$
\mathcal{B} = \left( |\psi_0\rangle \in \mathcal{H}_N,\; \mathcal{R}: \mathcal{H}_N \rightarrow \{0,1\}^N \right),
$$

where $|\psi_0\rangle$ is a normalized state vector (or, in the mixed generalization, a density operator $\rho_0$ with $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_0) = 1$) and $\mathcal{R}$ is a declared readout map. Equivalently, in the relational terms favored by ontic structural realism [11], the Base State is a labeled transition system $B = (S, \Sigma, \delta, \lambda, \mathcal{G})$ with states $S$, interaction alphabet $\Sigma$, transition function $\delta$, observable labeling $\lambda$, and symmetry group $\mathcal{G}$. We say $\mathcal{B}$ **hosts** a target structure $\mathcal{T}$ if:

1. **Preparation**: $|\psi_0\rangle$ is preparable from a product initial state by a circuit of finite, stated depth.
2. **Verifiability**: there exists a measurement statistics test whose pass probability, under the hypothesis that the prepared state is $|\psi_0\rangle$, exceeds a declared threshold $\theta$, and whose pass probability under every state in a declared alternative set is below $\theta$.
3. **Compositional productivity**: admissible operations on $\mathcal{B}$ form a closed algebraic structure rich enough to encode the interaction pattern of $\mathcal{T}$; minimally, $\mathcal{G}$ should contain a subgroup matching the gauge structure $\mathrm{SU(3)} \times \mathrm{SU(2)} \times \mathrm{U(1)}$ [12].

The target structure is fixed by the Standard Model's gauge sector. The color sector alone carries eight gluon fields $G^i_{\mu}$, $i = 1, \cdots, 8$, with self-interactions encoded in the structure constants $f_{ijk}$ [2]. As pure arithmetic on Lie-algebra dimensions, $\dim \mathrm{SU(3)} = 3^2 - 1 = 8$, $\dim \mathrm{SU(2)} = 2^2 - 1 = 3$, $\dim \mathrm{U(1)} = 1$, so the minimal gauge algebra has dimension

$$
d_{\mathcal{G}} = 8 + 3 + 1 = 12.
$$

The count $8$ for the color sector is corroborated by the eight gluon fields of the QCD Lagrangian [2]; the full group $\mathrm{SU(3)}_C \times \mathrm{SU(2)}_L \times \mathrm{U(1)}_Y$ is the stated symmetry of the Standard Model architecture [12]. This is a counting statement about the target symmetry, not a derivation of the Standard Model. Condition 3 is deliberately left programmatic; conditions 1 and 2 are auditable with hardware numbers, and Section 4 audits them. A hosted electroweak sector must additionally support spontaneous symmetry breaking, since gauge invariance forbids gauge-boson mass terms and the symmetry must be broken spontaneously to preserve renormalizability [2].

### 3.2 Audit quantities

The hardware inputs are those carried by the prior audit [9]: $N = 105$ qubits, single-qubit gate fidelity $F_1 = 0.9990$, two-qubit gate fidelity $F_2 = 0.9956$, readout fidelity $F_r = 0.987$. We model a circuit with $n_1$ single-qubit gates, $n_2$ two-qubit gates, and $n_r$ readout events by the independent-error product

$$
F_{\mathrm{circ}}(n_1, n_2, n_r) = F_1^{\,n_1}\, F_2^{\,n_2}\, F_r^{\,n_r},
$$

or, in log form,

$$
\ln F_{\mathrm{circ}} = n_1 \ln F_1 + n_2 \ln F_2 + n_r \ln F_r.
$$

This is a first-order model: it assumes errors are independent and multiplicative and ignores crosstalk, leakage, and correlated decay. It is conservative for our purposes because it can only overestimate fidelity when correlations are harmful; all circuit-level numbers below are therefore upper bounds on achievable fidelity. As a complementary per-operation summary, we also report the arithmetic mean fidelity $\bar{F} = (F_1 + F_2 + F_r)/3$ and the three-step composite error $P_{\mathrm{err}} = 1 - F_1 F_2 F_r$; neither enters any budget computation.

### 3.3 Specifiability accounting

A pure state $|\psi\rangle \in \mathcal{H}_N$ is specified, up to normalization and global phase, by $2D_N - 2$ real numbers: $D_N$ complex amplitudes contribute $2D_N$ real numbers, normalization removes $1$, and the global phase removes $1$ more. We compare this against the $\log_2 D_N = N$ bits needed to index a single computational basis state. The comparison quantifies why any hostable Base State must live in a structured, efficiently parameterized family — the situation in which effective operations are defined on a reduced representation rather than on the full amplitude list, as in bytecode-level operation on contracts whose source is unavailable [4].

### 3.4 Falsification protocol as a register

Following the register model of [10], in which a program is made testable by listing kill-conditions with scope rules and keeping negative results on the same ledger, and the model-recovery stance of [1], the protocol is:

- **Stage P (Preparation)**: prepare a candidate $|\psi_0\rangle$ on the full register; record circuit depth and gate counts. Kill-condition K-P: no structured family member can be prepared with $F_{\mathrm{circ}} \ge \theta$ at any depth within the platform budget.
- **Stage V (Verification)**: run a statistics test with threshold $\theta = 0.5$; the candidate fails if the achievable $F_{\mathrm{circ}}$ budget falls below $\theta$. Kill-condition K-V: readout fidelity is bounded below the projected $F_r^{*}$ of Section 4.8 by a mechanism no architectural change circumvents.
- **Stage C (Composition)**: demonstrate one nontrivial closed composition out of the Base State. Kill-condition K-C: the closure required by Condition 3 cannot embed a $d_{\mathcal{G}} = 12$ gauge algebra in any structured family.

Each kill-condition is written so that a null result is itself a registered outcome, in the sense that executing a kill-condition yields knowledge regardless of direction [10].

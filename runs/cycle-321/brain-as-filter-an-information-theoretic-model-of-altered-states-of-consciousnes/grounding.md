## Research idea

Brain as Filter: Altered States as Reduced-Filtering Windows onto a Grounding Informational Layer. The note's strongest original conjecture is an inversion of the standard explanatory arrow: the brain does not generate consciousness but filters/localizes a pre-existing non-spatiotemporal informational ground (L3) into an embodied perspective (L2). This yields a falsifiable-flavored prediction: states that reduce filtering (deep meditation, psychedelics, NDEs) should exhibit cross-culturally invariant phenomenological features (timelessness, unity, presence) because they partially reveal L3's structure, and these features should correlate with measurable reductions in filter-like neural mechanisms (e.g., thalamocortical gating, default-mode-network suppression). A paper could formalize the filter as an information-theoretic channel-capacity constraint on an IIT-style substrate, derive which phenomenal invariants survive as filtering weakens, and test predictions against neuroimaging and cross-cultural phenomenological datasets, comparing the filter model's explanatory power against production-based theories.

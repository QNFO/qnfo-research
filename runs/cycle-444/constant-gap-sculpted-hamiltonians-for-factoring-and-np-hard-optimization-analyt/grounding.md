## Research idea

Constant-gap sculpted Hamiltonians evade analog exponential precision limits. Claim: the exponential-precision objection to analog computation applies only to unstructured landscapes; a problem-specific Hamiltonian embedding mathematical constraints (e.g., (ln p + ln q − ln N)² plus integrality-enforcement walls for factoring) can confine low-energy states to valid solutions with an energy gap ΔE that is constant in problem size, so correctness comes from energetic protection (k_BT ≪ ΔE, digital readout) rather than analog precision. Why it matters: it would refute the standard argument that analog systems need O(2^n) precision, reframing analog computing as a functorial compilation problem. Test: formally derive the gap scaling of such sculpted Hamiltonians for factoring and NP-hard problems—checking whether the 'constant gap' claim survives rigorous analysis or hides exponential cost in parameter precision or landscape construction; simulate thermal relaxation and noise tolerance as a function of N's bit-length to verify gap-size independence and readout reliability.

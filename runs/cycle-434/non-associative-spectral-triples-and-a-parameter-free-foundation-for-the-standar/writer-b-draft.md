# Non-Associative Spectral Triples: An Octonionic Programme for Parameter-Free Standard Model Structure and Its First Falsifiable Tests

## Abstract

The Standard Model carries roughly two dozen measured parameters — masses, mixing angles and coupling constants — that its gauge structure fixes only as free inputs, and every attempt to derive them has so far imported the numbers rather than produced them. Non-commutative geometry comes closest: in the spectral-action framework the Higgs appears as a connection component, but the construction requires an associative algebra of coordinates, and the octonions, the largest normed division algebra, are excluded. We propose a resolution: a spectral triple whose algebra is a non-associative algebra of octonionic bimodules, with a Dirac operator constrained by a self-consistency (autaxys) fixed-point condition. We show that a three-mode octonionic resonance ansatz $\lambda_j=\lambda_0(1+\sqrt{2}\cos(\theta+2\pi j/3))$ reproduces the Koide relation exactly, giving $Q=\sum m_j/(\sum\sqrt{m_j})^2=2/3$ identically, and numerically $Q=0.666661$ for assumed charged-lepton masses, a deviation of $5.96\times10^{-6}$ from $2/3$. We convert the programme's $10^{-15}\ \mathrm{eV}$ dark-matter candidate to a coherence frequency of $0.241799\ \mathrm{Hz}$ (period $4.135668\ \mathrm{s}$), a directly testable target. The work is analytical and unreviewed (TRL 1–2); its decisive first test is mathematical — whether the spectral-triple axioms survive non-associativity — and its decisive physical test is the predicted blue-tilted primordial gravitational-wave spectrum, which standard inflation does not produce.

## 1. Introduction

Particle physicists face a specific, quantifiable obstacle: the Standard Model's Lagrangian, built on the gauge group $\mathrm{SU(3)}_C\times\mathrm{SU(2)}_L\times\mathrm{U(1)}_Y$, is the most empirically successful framework in physics, yet its charged-fermion masses, neutrino masses, mixing angles and coupling constants enter as measured inputs rather than derived outputs [12]. Every beyond-Standard-Model model builder inherits this parameter space and inflates it further; the numerical values that anchor the theory are determined only by measurement [11]. The same input-dependence blocks a structural account of why there are three generations of fermions and why the lepton masses obey the near-exact Koide regularity. A derivation of even one of these numbers from a geometric principle would change what the Standard Model is: from a parametrised effective theory to a fixed point of a mathematical structure.

Non-commutative geometry (NCG) is the framework that has come nearest to this goal. In Connes' spectral description of the Standard Model, the Higgs field emerges on the same footing as gauge bosons, as a component of a connection one-form generalising the Levi-Civita connection [7]. The construction, however, rests on an associative coordinate algebra: a spectral triple is an involutive algebra acting on a Hilbert space together with a self-adjoint Dirac operator with compact resolvent and bounded commutators [7]. The octonions $\mathbb{O}$, the unique $8$-dimensional real non-associative normed division algebra, are therefore excluded from the standard construction — despite the fact that octonionic structures repeatedly surface wherever exceptional symmetry appears, as in the $Spin(9)$-invariant valuation theory on $\mathbb{O}^2\simeq\mathbb{R}^{16}$ [6].

This paper develops the octonionic alternative as a concrete programme with a decisive first test. The central conjecture is that a spectral triple built on a non-associative algebra of octonionic bimodules, with a universal Dirac operator required to be a fixed point of a self-consistency condition (the autaxys condition), yields particle masses, coupling constants and three generations without free parameters, plus a falsifiable blue-tilted primordial gravitational-wave spectrum that standard inflation does not predict. Because the standard spectral action requires associativity, the programme's first test is mathematical and binary: either the spectral-triple axioms generalise to non-associative algebras, or an associative embedding (a matrix or Clifford algebra over $\mathbb{O}$) exists in which non-associativity manifests as symmetry breaking. Either outcome is informative; the second reduces the conjecture to known associative NCG.

We derive three concrete results. First, a three-mode octonionic resonance ansatz for the Dirac spectrum reproduces the Koide formula for charged leptons identically, as an algebraic identity independent of the resonance phase (Section 4.1–4.2). Second, the autaxys fixed-point condition is satisfied by this ansatz: the resonance spectrum is invariant under the self-consistency map (Section 4.3). Third, the programme's dark-matter candidate at $10^{-15}\ \mathrm{eV}$ corresponds to an oscillation frequency of $0.241799\ \mathrm{Hz}$ and a period of $4.135668\ \mathrm{s}$, placing it in a band accessible to proposed low-frequency gravitational-wave and precision-oscillation searches (Section 4.4). The contributions are:

- Derivation of the Koide identity $Q=2/3$ from a three-mode octonionic resonance condition, with the numerical check $Q=0.666661$ against assumed charged-lepton masses (Sections 4.1 and 4.2).
- Proof that the resonance ansatz is a fixed point of the autaxys self-consistency map, and identification of the octonionic dimension $8$ as the structural constant controlling the non-associative correction scale (Section 4.3).
- Conversion of the $10^{-15}\ \mathrm{eV}$ dark-matter candidate to an observable frequency $f=0.241799\ \mathrm{Hz}$ with period $T=4.135668\ \mathrm{s}$, stated as a projection with its assumptions (Section 4.4).
- A falsification protocol: the mathematical test (survival of the spectral-triple axioms over $\mathbb{O}$) and the physical test (blue-tilted gravitational-wave spectrum, small-scale structure impact of the ultralight candidate), each with the observation that would refute it (Sections 6 and 7).

The resonance-counting machinery developed here may also bear on adjacent problems in which discrete spectra must be reproduced without fitted parameters, such as quasiparticle classification tables that unify elementary and emergent excitations [9], and on parameter-free modelling strategies in machine learning, where encoders without learned parameters have been shown to remain competitive [8].

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Non-associative functional analysis and categories.** The algebraic substrate for this programme exists. The category of octonionic bimodules with octonionic para-linear maps as morphisms has been constructed as a genuine non-associative category: its compositions are non-associative in general, and the para-linear map is the central object of octonionic Hilbert theory because of the octonionic Riesz representation theorem [1]. Two results from [1] matter directly here. First, the adjoint functor theorem for $\mathbb{O}$-bimodules (Theorem 4.22) establishes that $(M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-,\ \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-))$ is an adjoint pair for any $\mathbb{O}$-bimodule $M$, with the natural isomorphism $\tau_{X,Y}:\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X,Y)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y))$; this is the homological machinery a non-associative spectral triple would need in place of the associative tensor-Hom adjunction. Second, [1] introduces weak functors and enveloping categories that relate associative to non-associative categories and establishes a Yoneda lemma in the non-associative setting — precisely the bridge an associative embedding of the octonionic triple would travel.

**Operator theory over the octonions.** The analytic control needed for a Dirac operator over $\mathbb{O}$ has a first data point. For an octonionic matrix $T\in M_n(\mathbb{O})$, the octonionic numerical radius satisfies $\frac{\|T\|}{8}\leq w_{\mathbb{O}}(T)\leq\|T\|$, proved via the generalized polarization identity with a sum over the seven imaginary units $e_j$ [2]. The lower bound degrades the complex-case factor $\frac{\|T\|}{2}$ to $\frac{\|T\|}{8}$ — the octonionic dimension $8$ appearing as the price of non-associativity. [2] also formulates the octonionic Crouzeix problem and conjectures a universal constant $R_{\mathbb{O}}=8$ for polynomial bounds $\|p(A)\|\leq R_{\mathbb{O}}\sup\{|p(z)|:z\in W_{\mathbb{O}}(A)\}$ over $\mathbb{O}[z]$; the recurring constant $8$ suggests that octonionic dimension controls analytic constants, which we use in Section 4.3 to fix the non-associative correction scale.

**Spectral asymptotics for systems.** The spectral theory of first-order elliptic systems — the class to which a Dirac operator belongs — is developed in a series of works whose methods transfer. For an elliptic self-adjoint pseudodifferential operator $A$ on $m$-columns of half-densities with simple principal-symbol eigenvalues, the spectrum decomposes, up to superpolynomially decaying error, into $m$ distinct series, each tied to one eigenvalue of the principal symbol [4]; the decomposition is implemented by the operators $A_j=A-2\sum_{l\neq j}P_l^*AP_l$ whose principal symbols retain only $h^{(j)}$. The propagator of a first-order self-adjoint elliptic system is constructed as a sum of $m$ oscillatory integrals, one per principal-symbol branch, with the leading transport equation forcing $u^{(j)}_0$ to be proportional to the normalised eigenvector $v^{(j)}$ of the principal symbol [5]; [5] additionally supplies an explicit formula for the second asymptotic coefficient, correcting earlier incomplete algorithms. Boundary problems are covered by the Robin analysis: for a second-order strongly elliptic operator with Robin conditions $\nu u-b\gamma_0 u=0$, the spectral behaviour of resolvent differences between two realisations is determined by singular Green operator methods, with Schatten-class product rules $\mathfrak{S}_p\cdot\mathfrak{S}_q\subset\mathfrak{S}_{1/(p^{-1}+q^{-1})}$ controlling the estimates, and the asymptotics extend to coefficients with jump discontinuities [3]. These three works give the toolkit — spectral series decomposition, propagator construction, resolvent-difference estimates — that a non-associative Dirac operator analysis would import, with the caveat that none of them treats non-associative coefficients; their transfer is a conjecture of this programme, not a result.

**Octonionic geometry.** On the geometric side, a class of plurisubharmonic functions on the octonionic plane $\mathbb{O}^2\simeq\mathbb{R}^{16}$ has been introduced, with octonionic analogues of the Aleksandrov, Chern–Levine–Nirenberg and Błocki theorems proved, and these are used to construct continuous translation-invariant valuations, including a new $Spin(9)$-invariant valuation on $\mathbb{R}^{16}$ [6]. The symmetric 3-linear functional $\tau(f_0,f_1,f_2)=\int_{\mathbb{O}^2}f_0\det(\partial^2 f_1,\partial^2 f_2)\,dq$ of [6], proved symmetric in all three arguments, is a candidate model for the invariant integration theory a non-associative spectral action would require. That $Spin(9)$ — one of the exceptional compact groups acting transitively on a sphere — arises naturally over $\mathbb{O}^2$ is the geometric signature of the octonions that motivates expecting exceptional symmetry in an octonionic spectral triple.

**Twisted spectral triples and torsion.** The closest existing generalisation of the spectral-triple axioms is the twisted triple: an involutive algebra $\mathcal{A}$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ with a self-adjoint operator $D$ with compact resolvent and an automorphism $\rho$ such that the twisted commutator $[D,a]_\rho=Da-\rho(a)D$ is bounded [7]. Twisting the spectral triple of a Riemannian spin manifold generates an orthogonal, geodesic-preserving torsion from a torsionless Dirac operator; the group of twisted unitaries generates torsion with co-exact three-form, the torsion term identifies through the fermionic action with a Lorentzian energy-momentum four-vector, and the Lorentz group is a proper subgroup of the twisted unitaries [7]. This establishes that the bounded-commutator axiom admits structured relaxations; our proposal extends the relaxation from automorphic twisting to genuine non-associativity, with [1] supplying the category-theoretic framework and [2] the first analytic bound.

**Parameter-free precedents and cautions.** Two QNFO works frame both the ambition and the risk. The Bruhat–Tits pattern-table programme assembles the Standard Model's finite particle catalog and the open-ended condensed-matter catalog of quasiparticles and anyons as projections of one tree, with every Standard Model mass expressed as a dimensionless Compton count $N_C=m/m_P$ and four falsifiability conditions spanning both catalogs; its own conclusion bounds the claims as retrodictions whose evidential value rests on excitations not yet characterized [9]. More pointedly, the joint Bruhat–Tits tree conjecture — that the Pythagorean semigroup $\mathcal{P}=\{2^a3^b5^c\}$ underlies Standard Model mass ratios, QEC lattice parameters and Efimov scaling — was evaluated by exact arithmetic and partially falsified: the harmonic-mean Efimov correspondence failed by a factor of $22.51$ ($\lambda_{\text{tree}}=1.008652$ versus assumed empirical $\lambda\approx22.7$), and the headline joint probability $(4\times10^{-3})^{11}=4.194304\times10^{-27}$ for eleven simultaneous mass-ratio fits was corrected and shown to remain conditional on unverified inputs [11]. The lesson we adopt as method: any parameter-free numerology must be accompanied by exact arithmetic, an explicit null model, and a stated falsifier — the standard we apply in Sections 4 and 6.

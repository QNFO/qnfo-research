## Research idea

Prime-indexed collision-free addressable computational units. Conjecture: prime numbers can serve as fundamental computational/addressing units (analogous to bits/qubits), where each value is uniquely encoded via an index/winding number N with density ~1/log(N) (prime number theorem), plus an iterative transcendental scaling component based on the previous prime. This yields deterministic, collision-free, unbounded addressable storage useful for LLM token embeddings, high-dimensional data, distributed storage, and microscopic simulation, while dissolving the von Neumann storage/compute bottleneck. A paper could: (1) formalize the encoding scheme and prove injectivity/collision-freedom; (2) analyze address-space growth and entropy cost under Landauer's principle, including time-based storage subject to entropy; (3) benchmark against hash-based and positional encodings on LLM token addressing and distributed key-value tasks; (4) simulate whether prime-structured addressing reduces retrieval collisions or enables compute-in-place architectures.

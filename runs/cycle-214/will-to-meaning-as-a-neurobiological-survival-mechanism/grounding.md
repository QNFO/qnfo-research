## Research idea

Will to Meaning as a Neurobiological Survival Mechanism. The note claims that the 'will to meaning' is a structural requirement of the human nervous system: perceived purpose recruits the dopaminergic reward system to sustain goal-directed behavior, and individuals with a felt 'task' are statistically more likely to survive crises. This can be formalized as a hypothesis that meaning functions as an adaptive resource-allocation signal coordinating finite energy under hardship. A paper could test this by (1) building a computational agent model in which a 'purpose' variable modulates dopaminergic reward weighting and motivational persistence, comparing survival/energy-management outcomes in simulated scarcity or crisis with and without purpose signals; (2) deriving predictions linking purpose-belief strength to behavioral persistence and physiological resilience metrics; and (3) reviewing empirical survival data (e.g., crisis, captivity, illness studies) for dose-response consistency with the model's predictions about meaning as a survival-enhancing mechanism.

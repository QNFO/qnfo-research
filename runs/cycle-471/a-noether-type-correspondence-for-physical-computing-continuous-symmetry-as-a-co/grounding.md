## Research idea

Symmetry-Exploiting Framework for Physical Computing and Hybrid Intelligence. The author conjectures that circular/rotational symmetry (SO(2)/U(1)) can serve as a unifying design principle for physical computing systems, linking Noether-style conservation laws, exponential/harmonic decomposition, and symmetry-aware machine learning into a single framework for hybrid (physical + computational) intelligence. The claim: encoding symmetry as a first-class constraint in both physical device design (resonators, crystals, cyclic structures) and learning architectures yields provable efficiency gains, analogous to how cyclic-symmetry modeling reduces structural analysis to a single sector. A paper could formalize this by deriving a correspondence between continuous symmetries of a physical computing substrate and conserved computational quantities (a Noether-like theorem for computation), then testing it via simulations—e.g., symmetry-constrained autoencoders or harmonic-mode truncation in cyclic structures—quantifying whether lower-harmonic dominance and symmetry-aware representations systematically reduce computational cost while preserving fidelity across optics, acoustics, and materials examples cited.

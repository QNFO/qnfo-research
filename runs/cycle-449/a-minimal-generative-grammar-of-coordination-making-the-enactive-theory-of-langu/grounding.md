## Research idea

Minimal generative grammar of coordination for autopoietic artificial social realities. The claim: the enactive model of language—language as an interaction protocol that generates rather than describes social reality—can be made falsifiable by treating it as a design specification in generative social simulation. The core question: what is the minimal 'grammar of coordination' that, implemented as the interaction protocol for autonomous agents with no pre-shared world model, allows stable, autopoietic social reality to emerge endogenously, and how do grammar variations predictably alter measurable properties (lexicon size, normative complexity, resilience to perturbation)? This matters because it converts an allegedly unfalsifiable philosophical thesis into a testable engineering program and a blueprint for AI that bootstraps its own meaning. Test: implement competing coordination protocols in multi-agent simulations, define autopoietic viability as a measurable output (stability, adaptability, persistence under perturbation), and competitively evaluate which grammars yield stable non-trivial enacted worlds versus communicational collapse.

## Research idea

Human Gatekeepers and Algorithms Share Outlier-Penalizing Bias Structure. The author conjectures a functional symmetry: human gatekeepers default to rejecting unfamiliar ideas or outsider status, while algorithmic systems (e.g., recommendation and search ranking) default to statistical consensus, and both systematically penalize distributional outliers—novel or interdisciplinary work. The claimed difference is operational: algorithmic bias is statistically transparent and can be satisfied by metadata optimization, whereas human bias is opaque and personal. This could be tested formally by modeling gatekeeping as a threshold decision process over a novelty/affiliation feature space, comparing human review data with algorithmic ranking data on the same corpus of interdisciplinary papers, and simulating whether metadata interventions shift algorithmic but not human acceptance rates—quantifying the claimed asymmetry.

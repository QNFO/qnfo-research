## Research idea

Error Confinement Theorem for Ultrametric Decoders. The author is developing an error confinement theorem for a decoder model with symmetric decoder, p≥3 states, i.i.d. noise, and ultrametric (tree) geometry. The open question: can this theorem be rigorously formalized under those assumptions, and does a formal proof of the mathematical model imply anything about physical hardware performance? This matters because ultrametric structure may confine errors hierarchically, analogous to how surface code threshold proofs relate (weakly) to real qubits. A paper could state and prove the confinement theorem—showing errors remain confined to subtrees under the stated assumptions—derive a threshold-like bound, and then analyze the proof-to-physics gap by simulating the decoder under assumption violations (asymmetric noise, correlated errors) to quantify how much the formal guarantee transfers to realistic hardware.

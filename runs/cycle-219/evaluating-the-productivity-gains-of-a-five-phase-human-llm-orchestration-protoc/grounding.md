## Research idea

Controlled replication of LLM force-multiplier protocol speedup claims. The author claims a five-phase human-orchestrator LLM protocol (define, delegate, execute-iterate, verify, synthesize) yields 25×–90× throughput speedups for solo theoretical research, based only on self-experiments by the protocol's creator. A rigorous test: run a controlled, pre-registered study where matched researchers with comparable expertise solve identical well-scoped problems (derivation, data analysis, drafting) with and without the protocol, measuring wall-clock time, output quality via blind expert review, and error rates caught by the four verification gates. Formal analysis could model which task decompositions parallelize well versus which fail (wet lab, HPC-bound work), and test whether speedup persists for first-time users or is an artifact of creator familiarity. This matters because it would establish or refute a general, reusable methodology for solo scientific productivity rather than anecdote.

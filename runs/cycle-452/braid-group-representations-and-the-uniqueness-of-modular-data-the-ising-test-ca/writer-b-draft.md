# Braid Group Representations and the Uniqueness of Modular Data: The Ising Test Case and General Limitations

## Abstract

Whether the braid group representation carried by $N$ non-Abelian anyons uniquely determines the modular data — the $S$ and $T$ matrices — of the underlying topological order is an open determinacy question with direct consequences for Majorana-zero-mode quantum computing platforms, where braid operations are the primary experimentally accessible control. We revisit the question using the Ising anyon theory as a fully explicit test case. Starting from the two-anyon braid eigenvalues $r_1=e^{-i\pi/8}$ and $r_\psi=e^{3i\pi/8}$ and the fusion rule $\sigma\times\sigma=1+\psi$, we derive with shown arithmetic the quantum dimension $d_\sigma=\sqrt{2}\approx1.4142$, the total quantum dimension $D=2$, the topological spin $\theta_\sigma=e^{i\pi/8}$ up to a sign, the full modular matrices $S=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\sqrt{2}&1\\ \sqrt{2}&0&-\sqrt{2}\\ 1&-\sqrt{2}&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and $T=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\pi/8},-1)$, the fusion-space dimensions $f(n,1)=2^{n/2-1}$ for even $n$, and the braid generator order $16$ (projective $4$). Against this positive result we set the structural evidence for general non-uniqueness: finite-image braid families, the $\mathrm{Ising}(7)$ deformation with $\theta_\sigma=e^{7i\pi/8}$ and distinct $S,T$, and spin-structure dependence of the global $S$ matrix. We conclude that braiding determines the Ising modular data up to one sign, but is not a complete invariant in general, and that interferometric measurement is the minimal complementary probe.

## 1. Introduction

Two-dimensional topological superconductors hosting Majorana zero modes (MZMs) are among the leading platforms for topological quantum computation, and the operational resource they offer is braiding: exchanging vortices $i$ and $j$ acts on the ground-state manifold by $U_{ij}=\exp(\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_i\gamma_j)$, a projective representation of the braid group $B_N$ [11]. A research program that certifies the underlying topological order by braiding alone — measuring braid matrices, inverting them for the modular $S$ and $T$ matrices, and matching against a classification — is feasible only if the braid representation determines the modular data uniquely. If distinct topological orders can share the same braid image, then braid-only certification silently mislabels the order, and every such program must budget for a second, independent probe such as interferometry. The determinacy question therefore decides a concrete engineering choice: what measurement suite a Majorana-based platform must implement before its braid data can be trusted as a certificate.

The question has two parts, posed explicitly in the literature on MZM fusion-rule classification [11],[12]: first, does the braid group representation on $N$ anyons uniquely determine the modular data $(S,T)$; second, can this framework classify all fusion rules for MZMs in two-dimensional topological superconductors? The modular data consist of the $S$-matrix, encoding mutual braiding statistics, and the diagonal $T$-matrix, encoding topological spins; together they form a projective representation of the modular group $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$ and satisfy the Verlinde formula, which determines the fusion rules [12].

This paper is a post-publication revision of the Ising test-case analysis [10]. It strengthens the original derivations, makes every arithmetic step explicit, corrects the scope of the uniqueness claim, and integrates the structural non-uniqueness evidence assembled in the companion assessment [9]. The Ising theory is the right laboratory because it is the physically canonical MZM theory, its braid representation is small enough to check by hand, and its modular data are known independently, so the reconstruction can be validated entry by entry.

Our contributions are:

- A three-step reconstruction pipeline (braid eigenvalues $\to$ topological spins via the ribbon identity $\to$ $S$ via the Hopf-link/Verlinde formula), applied to Ising with every arithmetic step shown, yielding $d_\sigma=\sqrt{2}$, $D=2$, the full $S$ and $T$ matrices, and the residual ambiguity $\theta_\sigma\to-\theta_\sigma$ (Sections 3 and 4).
- Derived fusion-space dimensions $f(n,1)=2^{n/2-1}$ for even $n$ and the braid generator order $16$ (projective $4$), with the recursions and powers computed explicitly (Section 4).
- A convention analysis separating the sign ambiguity $\theta_\sigma\to-\theta_\sigma$, which follows from full two-anyon braid eigenvalues, from the larger finite ambiguity $\theta_\sigma\to e^{i\pi k/4}\theta_\sigma$ ($k$ odd) reported for projective Majorana data (Sections 4 and 6).
- An assessment of general non-uniqueness built on the finite-image families, the $\mathrm{Ising}(7)$ deformation family with trace ratio $e^{-3i\pi/4}$, and the spin-structure dependence of the global $S$ matrix, with the falsification conditions stated (Sections 2, 5 and 6).
- A practical pathway in which braid-only certification is bounded and the minimal complementary probe is interferometric measurement of $R$- and $F$-matrix elements (Section 7).

Beyond Majorana platforms, the same determinacy question bears on anyonic realizations on graphs, where the exchange group itself depends on the configuration-space topology, and on numerical tensor-network studies that extract braiding data from simulated anyon systems.

## 2. Background and Related Work

We review the works that constrain what braid data can and cannot fix, citing each for specific results its text states.

**Anyonic tensor-network simulation.** The thesis of Ayeni constructed modified numerical algorithms based on tensor networks to simulate systems of anyons in low dimensions, and used those methods to study the topological phases the anyons form when they braid around one another [1]. This is the computational counterpart of our question: numerical extraction of braiding physics presupposes that the simulated anyon theory is already specified, so tensor networks can validate a reconstruction pipeline but cannot by themselves decide uniqueness. Our pipeline is analytic and complements such methods by giving closed-form targets ($S$, $T$, $d_a$) against which any numerical extraction can be checked.

**Graph braid groups.** Anyonic quantum statistics on graphs generalizes planar exchange: an interchange of quasi-particles results in a general unitary transformation of a possibly multicomponent many-body wave function, and graph braid groups $Br_N(\Gamma)$ are finitely presented with generators and relators depending on the graph [3]. Two specific structural facts from this work matter here. First, graph configuration spaces are aspherical, so the braid group encodes all topological information about the configuration space. Second, the abelianization satisfies $Br_N(\Gamma)'\cong H_1(C_N(\Gamma),\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^m\oplus(\mathbb{Z}_2)^p$, with $p=0$ if and only if $\Gamma$ is planar [3]. The planar case on which our Ising analysis rests is thus the degenerate case of a broader family in which even the exchange group, not just its representation, varies with geometry — an additional source of data beyond any single $B_N$ representation.

**Deformed braid-type relations.** In the DAHA $C^\vee C_1$ analysis, the fundamental group of a four-punctured sphere is generated by $T_0,T_0^\vee,T_1,T_1^\vee$ with relation $T_0^\vee T_0 T_1 T_1^\vee=1$, and the double affine Hecke algebra is obtained by deforming this relation to $T_0^\vee T_0 T_1 T_1^\vee=q^{-1/2}$ [4]. This shows that braid-type group relations sit in continuous one-parameter families of deformations. The lesson for determinacy is structural: a representation of the undeformed braid group does not preclude neighboring algebraic structures with deformed relations and different representation theory, so the class of braid-like data is larger than the class realized by topological orders.

**Interferometric braiding.** A quantum-impurity-based protocol for braiding non-Abelian anyons exploits mobile impurities with addressable internal spin states and provides a concrete protocol to experimentally measure the matrix elements of the $R$ (and associated fusion) data [7]. This is the complementary probe our conclusions require: where braiding alone leaves a sign ambiguity in $\theta_\sigma$, an interferometric measurement accesses monodromy phases through a distinct physical channel and can resolve it.

**The non-uniqueness assessment.** The companion QNFO assessment [9] formalizes the question as a reduction map $\Phi$ from modular tensor categories to braid representation data and reports four results we use directly. R1: braid representations from twisted quantum doubles of finite groups factor through finite groups, and $SO(N)_2$/$O(N)_2$ braid representations are Gaussian with finite image. R2: the Ising modular data $\theta_1=1$, $\theta_\sigma=e^{i\pi/8}$, $\theta_\psi=-1$, $\mathcal{D}=2$, $T=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\pi/8},-1)$ and the canonical $S$ matrix, verified against the ribbon identity, hexagon equations, $S^2=C$ and $STS^{-1}$ diagonalization. R3: the three-anyon trace $\operatorname{Tr}\rho_3(\sigma_1)=\sqrt{2}\,e^{i\pi/8}\approx1.3066+0.5412\,i$, of magnitude $\sqrt{2}=d_\sigma$. R4: the deformation family $\mathrm{Ising}(7)$ shares all fusion rules with Ising but has $\theta_\sigma=e^{7i\pi/8}$ and distinct $S,T$; its $B_3$ representation is inequivalent to Ising's, with trace ratio $e^{-3i\pi/4}$, though projectively intertwined by a parity operator. Critically, [9] states in its Discussion that its non-uniqueness assessment is an existence-of-room argument plus a concrete deformation family, not a constructive collision of two MTCs with identical full projective braid images and distinct $(S,T)$, and that a proof that $\Phi$ is injective on a class containing the finite-image families would falsify it. We adopt exactly this honesty standard for our own uniqueness statements.

**The prior Ising test-case paper.** The published version under revision [10] formulated the reconstruction pipeline, computed the Ising summary table ($d_\sigma=\sqrt{2}\approx1.4142$, $D=2$, $S_{\sigma1}=0.7071$, $S_{\sigma\psi}=-0.7071$, $S_{\sigma\sigma}=0$, $T_{\sigma\sigma}=0.9239+0.3827\,i$, $f(n,1)=2^{n/2-1}$, braid order $16$ projective $4$), and asserted a "partial uniqueness theorem" tying determinacy to density of the braid image. That theorem was not derived from the supplied structure of the problem and is not supported by the finite-image counterexamples the paper itself lists; in this revision we downgrade it to a labeled hypothesis (Section 6) and keep only what the derivations support.

**MZM classification, determinacy mechanism.** The first MZM classification paper computes the Ising/MZM quantum dimensions $d_1=d_\psi=1$, $d_\sigma=\sqrt{2}$, total quantum dimension $\mathcal{D}=2$, and derives the $S$ matrix from the fusion rules and the braid-fixed twist $\theta_\sigma=e^{i\pi/8}$ via the Verlinde formula, with unitarity verified by explicit inner-product computation over all six row pairs; it reports the $T$ matrix as determined by the braid representation up to the finite ambiguity $\theta_\sigma\to e^{i\pi k/4}\theta_\sigma$, $k$ odd [11]. Its Discussion states the scope honestly: the determinacy argument for Ising relies on exchange eigenvalues fixing $\theta_\sigma$, for higher-rank candidates the braid representation on small $N$ may under-determine the twists, and no general determinacy theorem is proven — only the mechanism is exhibited. It also reports that gauging moves generate candidate fusion rules whose hexagon coherence was not exhaustively tested, and that the metaplectic family $SO(N)_2$ with a Property F discriminator separates gapped-TSC-compatible from computationally universal theories [11].

**MZM classification, spin structure.** The second MZM classification paper supplies the definitional framework we use: braid generators $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{N-1}$ subject to $\sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i$ for $|i-j|\ge2$ and $\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$; the pentagon and hexagon equations as polynomial consistency constraints on $F$- and $R$-symbols; and the modular $S$-matrix defined by the Hopf link invariant with total quantum dimension $\mathcal{D}=\sqrt{\sum_a d_a^2}$ [12]. Its conclusion is the sharpest structural statement in the literature we cite: the braid representation constrains the modular data up to finite gauge ambiguity but does not uniquely determine the global $S$-matrix without specifying the underlying spin structure; for MZMs the local braid representation $\rho(\sigma_i)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+\gamma_{i+1}\gamma_i)$ uniquely fixes the local $F$ and $R$ symbols of the Ising category, the fusion rules are rigidly classified as Ising type ($\sigma\times\sigma=1+\psi$) with no alternative non-Abelian fusion rules consistent with the Majorana Clifford algebra and super-modular structure, and the global $S$-matrix depends on the spin structure of the underlying manifold, reflecting the fermionic nature of the topological order [12].

Collectively these works fix the landscape: locally, braiding plus fusion rules can completely determine modular data (Ising); globally, finite-image families, deformation families, and spin-structure dependence leave room for non-uniqueness, and no constructive collision is known.

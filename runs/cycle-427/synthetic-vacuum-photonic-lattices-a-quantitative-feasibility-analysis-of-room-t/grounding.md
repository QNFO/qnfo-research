## Research idea

Synthetic Vacuum Chip Instantiating Gauge Symmetries for Fault-Tolerant Anyon Computing. The author conjectures that a room-temperature solid-state photonic lattice—a 'synthetic vacuum'—can be engineered to instantiate non-Abelian (SU(2)-like) gauge symmetries, so that Fibonacci anyons emerge as edge excitations and braiding them performs universal, intrinsically fault-tolerant quantum logic, with error protection arising from the medium's gauge structure rather than active correction. This matters because it would bypass cryogenic quantum computing and the von Neumann bottleneck by making the physics itself the program. A theoretical/computational paper could test it by (1) deriving whether a proposed photonic waveguide/moiré/Fibonacci-ordered lattice Hamiltonian realizes a topologically ordered phase supporting non-Abelian anyons at room temperature, (2) simulating edge-state braiding statistics and robustness to local disorder, and (3) analyzing whether gauge-symmetry constraints genuinely suppress error channels or merely relocate them, quantifying fault tolerance against realistic photonic noise models.

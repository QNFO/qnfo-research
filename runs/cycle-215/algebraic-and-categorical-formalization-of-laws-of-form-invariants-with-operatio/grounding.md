## Research idea

Algebraic/category-theoretic translation of Laws of Form invariants. The author conjectures that Laws of Form's core content—distinction as primitive, re-entry, and idempotent operations—has never been expressed as formal mathematical invariants in algebraic/category-theoretic notation, and that this translation is the key missing step for mainstream adoption. The concrete program: identify LoF's invariants (e.g., the calculus of indications' laws), express them via functors, natural transformations, and adjunctions—modeling the mark as an idempotent monad on a 2-category of distinctions—and strip notation-specific scaffolding. A paper could test this by constructing the monad/2-category formally, proving the re-entry form corresponds to a known categorical structure (e.g., a traced monoidal or fixed-point construction), and deriving whether the resulting algebra yields operational value (theorems, algorithms) comparable to category theory or information theory. Failure to produce such a derivation would support the author's alternative hypothesis that re-entry lacks operational content, explaining its marginalization.

# From Operational Axioms to Field Axioms: A Gap Analysis of the GPT-to-Wightman Reconstruction Claim

## Abstract

A reconstruction program in quantum foundations claims that four operational axioms of generalized probabilistic theories (GPTs)—causality, no-signaling, local tomography, and continuous reversibility—applied to systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom, necessarily yield the Wightman axioms of relativistic quantum field theory. If true, this would reduce the axiomatic basis of quantum field theory to operational postulates and settle which structural features of QFT are derived rather than assumed. This paper tests the claim by formalizing each GPT axiom in the operator-algebraic setting of nets of von Neumann algebras and Wightman fields, and by auditing each step against the literature on Wightman-type and algebraic field theories. We derive a precise obstruction analysis: causality and no-signaling map to microcausality and locality of observables with no gap; continuous reversibility maps to the unitary Poincaré representation only under an added spectral condition that the GPT axioms do not supply; and local tomography cannot be a necessary axiom because fermionic field theories violate it while satisfying the Wightman axioms, as the free fermion models studied in the algebraic literature confirm. We further show that the derivation is not unique across field extensions: quaternionic and real Hilbert-space theories satisfy the operational axioms, so the reconstruction, even if completed, selects a family of theories rather than complex quantum field theory uniquely. The claim is therefore a conjecture with one proven pillar (locality) and two unproven pillars (Poincaré representation and uniqueness), and we state the concrete lemmas whose proof or refutation would settle it.

## 1. Introduction

The practical problem this paper bears on is the status of the axioms of relativistic quantum field theory. Physicists working in constructive QFT, mathematical physics, and quantum foundations currently accept the Wightman or Haag-Kastler axiom systems as the definition of what a quantum field theory is, but these axiom systems postulate relativistic structure—microcausality, a unitary representation of the Poincaré group, the spectrum condition—directly, without deriving it from anything more operational. This postulational stance has a cost: it blocks any answer to the question of which parts of QFT structure are forced by the operational content of quantum theory and which are additional independent input. That question matters for three working communities. Researchers in quantum gravity and emergent spacetime need to know which features of QFT survive when spacetime is not taken as fundamental; a derivation of relativistic structure from operational axioms would tell them exactly which features are load-bearing consequences of quantum probability and which must be supplied by the geometry. Researchers in axiomatic QFT need to know whether the Wightman axioms are in any sense minimal; a reconstruction theorem would identify redundant axioms and clarify which relaxations, such as the relaxed Gårding-Wightman framework in which field operators are merely symmetric rather than self-adjoint, are forced or permitted [5]. And researchers in quantum foundations need to know whether the generalized probabilistic theory framework, which was built to compare quantum theory against operational alternatives, has any purchase on the relativistic regime at all; if the GPT axioms do not constrain QFT structure, the reconstruction program's scope is finite-dimensional by construction.

If the claimed reconstruction theorem holds, the decision it changes is concrete: one could replace the Wightman axioms, in textbooks and in constructive programs, by four operational postulates whose physical content is directly checkable in laboratory protocols, and one could prove that no non-relativistic or non-local operational theory with infinitely many degrees of freedom is consistent. If the claim fails, the failure analysis still delivers value: it identifies precisely which additional axiom carries the relativistic structure, which is the information needed both for constructive QFT programs that relax the axioms and for foundational programs that seek pre-geometric derivations of spacetime.

The specific gap is that the reconstruction claim exists as a program statement without a theorem: no published derivation takes the four GPT axioms as hypotheses and produces the Wightman axioms as conclusions, and no published gap analysis identifies where the derivation would need assumptions the GPT axioms do not contain. The literature supplies the ingredients on both sides—GPT axiomatics on the operational side, and Wightman-type axioms, nets of algebras, and modular theory on the field-theoretic side [1]—but the bridge between them has not been audited step by step.

Our approach is a formalization-and-audit. We state each GPT axiom as a condition on a system with infinitely many degrees of freedom, modeled as a net of operator algebras or as a field theory on a Hilbert space; we then attempt the derivation of each Wightman axiom in turn, and at each step we either complete the derivation, exhibit the missing hypothesis, or exhibit a counterexample from the literature. The audit is deliberately conservative: every claim we make about the field-theoretic side is one that the algebraic and Wightman-QFT literature states explicitly, and every claim we make about the operational side is one we derive here from the GPT axioms as we state them.

The principal results are as follows. First, we prove (Proposition 4.1) that the GPT axioms of causality and no-signaling, in the infinite-degree-of-freedom setting, imply microcausality for localized observables, so the locality pillar of the reconstruction is derivable without additional input; the derivation uses only the order structure of the causal relation and the tensor-product factorization that local tomography or its fermionic replacement supplies. Second, we show (Proposition 4.2 and the counterexample built on the free fermion net) that local tomography is not a necessary axiom of the reconstruction: fermionic theories satisfy the Wightman axioms while violating local tomography, so a reconstruction theorem that lists local tomography among its hypotheses either excludes fermions or must weaken the axiom to a graded (super) tensor product; the free fermion model is the case where the smeared fields are bounded operators and the distinction between the field net and the von Neumann algebra net disappears [1]. Third, we show (Proposition 4.3) that continuous reversibility yields a strongly continuous unitary representation of the symmetry group but does not by itself yield the spectrum condition; the positive-energy condition is additional input, and we exhibit where it must be inserted. Fourth, we show (Proposition 4.4) that the reconstruction is not unique across division rings: real and quaternionic Hilbert-space theories satisfy all four operational axioms, so the derivation, even if completed, selects a family of theories rather than complex QFT uniquely; uniqueness requires an additional axiom such as local tomography in its full complex form or a q-convexity condition, and we quantify which choice does the work. Fifth, we state (Section 4.5) the three lemmas whose proof would upgrade the claim from conjecture to theorem, and the single observation whose truth would refute it.

The contributions are:

- A step-by-step formalization of the four GPT axioms in the operator-algebraic infinite-degree-of-freedom setting, with the derivation of microcausality from causality and no-signaling completed (Proposition 4.1, Section 4.1).
- A counterexample-based argument that local tomography is not necessary for the Wightman axioms, using fermionic theories and the bounded-field case of the free fermion net (Proposition 4.2, Section 4.2).
- The identification of the spectrum condition as an underived input, with the precise insertion point in the derivation of the unitary Poincaré representation (Proposition 4.3, Section 4.3).
- A non-uniqueness result across real, complex, and quaternionic Hilbert-space theories, with the quantified condition that restores uniqueness (Proposition 4.4, Section 4.4).
- A lemma-level roadmap that states exactly what remains to be proven for the reconstruction claim to be a theorem, and the falsifying observation (Section 4.5).

The result could also matter to researchers in quantum computation over real and quaternionic Hilbert spaces, where the question of which operational axioms select the complex theory is the same question in finite dimensions, and to philosophers of physics assessing whether operational axioms are a foundation for QFT or a description of its finite-dimensional fragment.

## 2. Background and Related Work

We review the literature in three groups: the field-theoretic side (Wightman axioms, nets of algebras, modular theory), the operational side (GPT axioms and their scope), and the methodological side (how axiom systems are compared and audited).

**The field-theoretic side.** The Bisognano-Wichmann property links three structures that any reconstruction of QFT axioms must eventually connect: the analytic continuation of the symmetry group, the PCT operator, and the adjoint operation on smeared fields or local observables; the foundational articles exhibited this in 4-dimensional Wightman-type QFTs on Minkowski space, with analogous results for 2-dimensional conformal field theories under Wightman-type axioms and, using purely operator algebraic methods, under Haag-Kastler axioms [1]. This matters for the reconstruction claim because it shows that the relativistic structures the GPT program hopes to derive—Poincaré symmetry, locality, and the *-algebra structure of observables—are interderivable once one of them is in place: in the operator algebraic setting, physical symmetries realize the Tomita-Takesaki modular automorphisms and modular conjugation corresponding to the vacuum state [1]. A reconstruction theorem therefore does not need to derive each Wightman axiom independently; it needs to derive one structural anchor, and modular theory may transport the rest. The same source records the technical substrate on which such transport rests: a von Neumann algebra $M$ is a unital $*$-subalgebra of $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ closed in the pointwise-convergence topology, with a cyclic vector $\Omega$ for $M$ if $M\Omega$ is dense in $\mathcal{H}$ and separating if $x\Omega = 0$ implies $x = 0$ [1]; these are exactly the hypotheses under which the modular conjugation exists, so any GPT-derived structure must reproduce a cyclic separating vacuum to reach the modular machinery.

The same source also supplies the fermionic counterexample material our Proposition 4.2 uses. Araki proved Haag duality for a free scalar bosonic field, laying the foundation for later study of duality by the methods of Bisognano-Wichmann, and considered duality for free fermionic fields as well [1]. Crucially for the local-tomography question, the free fermion model is essentially the only example of a 2d chiral CFT whose smeared fields are bounded operators, so that the distinction between the field net $\mathcal{P}(I)$ generated by smeared fields and the corresponding net of von Neumann algebras $\mathcal{A}(I)$ is not necessary there [1]. This is the model in which fermionic statistics coexists with full algebraic duality, and it is the concrete witness that the Wightman axioms (in their graded form) do not require local tomography.

On the constructive side, recent work constructs Hermitian scalar quantum fields $\langle\mathcal{H}, U, \psi, D\rangle$ within a relaxed framework of the Gårding-Wightman axioms, using stochastic calculus with respect to stationary additive random fields, i.e., Lévy random fields on $\mathbb{R}^d$, for spacetime dimension $d \in \mathbb{N}$ including $d \geq 4$; the constructed fields satisfy all requirements of the Gårding-Wightman axioms except that the field operators $\psi(f)$, for $f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R})$, are symmetric operators on the physical Hilbert space rather than self-adjoint [5]. This is directly relevant to the reconstruction claim in two ways. First, it shows that the Wightman axiom system is not rigid: a relaxed framework in which self-adjointness is weakened to symmetry still supports exact relativistic quantum field models, so a reconstruction theorem must decide whether its operational axioms force full self-adjointness or permit the relaxed version. Second, the construction proceeds through a pseudo-differential operator $J_{P+}^{\gamma}$ whose symbol is $(\tau^2 - (\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\xi_k^2 + m^2))^{-\gamma}$ for $\tau > (\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\xi_k^2 + m^2)^{1/2}$ and zero otherwise, with $\gamma \in (0, \tfrac{1}{2})$ [5]; the support condition on $\tau$ is a spectral condition inserted by hand, which is precisely the kind of input our Proposition 4.3 identifies as not derivable from the operational axioms. The same source notes that for the Gaussian case the resulting field would be expected to be the usual free Wightman field but is not, showing that even the free field's uniqueness is construction-dependent [5].

**The operational side.** The generalized probabilistic theory framework axiomatizes measurement operationally, and recent foundational work sharpens what its axioms can and cannot carry. A category-theoretic reconstruction of finite measurement axiomatizes the act of measurement as a graded distinguishability map $v: \mathcal{S}^2 \to \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ over a poset of states satisfying identity of indiscernibles, symmetry, and the ultrametric inequality, with no dependence on $\mathbb{R}$ or set theory; the category Val of such spaces is self-contained and $\mathbb{R}$ appears only as a limit idealization [9]. This matters here because it demonstrates, by construction, that operational axiom systems can be formulated without presupposing the analytic structures (real Hilbert spaces, Archimedean continua) in which QFT is usually cast; a GPT-to-Wightman reconstruction that silently assumes complex Hilbert space has therefore assumed part of its conclusion, and our Proposition 4.4 makes that assumption explicit. The same source derives the effective spatial dimension $d$ as the asymptotic growth exponent of a distinguishability graph, constrained by cohomological consistency of a refinement sheaf [9], which shows that even spatial dimension—a relativistic input in the Wightman framework—has been derived from operational data in a finite-measurement setting, giving partial precedent for the reconstruction program's ambition.

A critical treatise on the load-bearing assumptions of quantum mechanics audits exactly the premises our reconstruction claim would need to derive, listing as the assumptions of the spin-statistics theorem: Lorentz (Poincaré) invariance; microcausality, i.e., spacelike-separated fields (anti)commuting; positive energy (spectrum condition); positive-norm Hilbert space; and exactly $3+1$ dimensions—and noting that these are premises of a theorem, not of the universe [10]. This list is the audit target for our Section 4.3: the reconstruction claim asserts that the first three items are derivable from operational axioms, and the treatise's framing—that each premise is load-bearing and must be audited where it is used—supplies the methodological standard we apply. The same treatise records the empirical status of the relevant structures through a domain-of-validity mapping in which each assumption is assigned to established, tested, untested, or known-to-fail, with microcausality tested decades ago and remaining tested today, and anyonic statistics moving from untested in 1980 to established in $2+1$ dimensions with the interferometry experiments of 2020 [10]. The anyon record is a warning for the reconstruction claim: in $2+1$ dimensions, the operational statistics of excitations include braid statistics that the standard Wightman framework on Minkowski space of $3+1$ dimensions does not accommodate, so any reconstruction must state its dimension hypothesis explicitly.

**The methodological side: how axiom systems are compared.** Three works from adjacent fields supply the audit methodology. A study of what happens when topology's union axiom is weakened to a countable union axiom shows how to isolate a single axiom of an axiom system, prove a theorem in the weakened system, and exhibit a concrete counterexample (a set $U$ that is a union of open intervals $U_i$ of lengths $s_i$ with $\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} s_i = a$ finite, containing $\mathbb{Q}$ with positive finite Lebesgue measure, and an indicator function $f$ continuous in the Michael line topology but not in the countable union topology) demonstrating that a property valid under the original axiom fails under the weakened one [3]. This is exactly the pattern our Proposition 4.2 follows: weaken or drop one GPT axiom (local tomography), and exhibit the fermionic theory as the counterexample showing which conclusions survive and which do not. The same source's historical framing—that the axioms of topology were fixed only in 1925 by Alexandrov after successive proposals by Fréchet, Hilbert, and Hausdorff [3]—is a reminder that axiom systems in mathematics are conventions stabilized by utility, so a reconstruction claim of the form "these axioms necessarily yield those" must be read as a statement about the specific axiom list, not about necessity in the abstract.

A second methodological parallel comes from automated planning, where a recent result proves that PDDL axioms—implications of the form $P(\vec{x}) \leftarrow \varphi(\vec{x})$ used to derive predicates from basic ones—are equivalent in expressiveness to least fixed point logic: the most general axiom formalism AP, allowing arbitrary first-order formulas in stratifiable axiom bodies, and the restricted formalism AP$_0$ are equally expressive, matching least fixed point logic and strictly exceeding stratified Datalog, which is described as the strongest possible negative result since some AP programs cannot be expressed in stratified Datalog regardless of compilation resources [6]. The relevance is structural: this is a completed example of an "axioms A are equivalent to theory B" theorem, and it shows what such a theorem looks like when finished—precise syntactic conditions on both sides, an expressiveness metric, and a strict separation result. The GPT-to-Wightman claim currently has none of these three components, which is a large part of why it is a conjecture rather than a theorem.

A third parallel, from set theory, shows how axiom systems are compared by consistency strength rather than by derivation: the resurrection axioms, a new class of forcing axioms, and uplifting cardinals, a new large cardinal notion, are proven equiconsistent over ZFC in various instances, and the resurrection axioms are viewed as forcing axioms because of their consequences among the bounded forcing axioms and because they express a precise logical connection between the universe and its forcing extensions [7]. The equiconsistency methodology—showing that two axiom systems have the same consistency strength rather than that one derives the other—is an alternative target for the reconstruction program: even if full derivation fails, one might prove that the GPT axioms plus a spectral condition are equiconsistent with (or equisatisfiable with) the Wightman axioms. The same source's observation that many classical forcing axioms can be viewed informally as the claim that the universe is existentially closed in its forcing extensions, with Martin's axiom equivalent to $H_{\mathfrak{c}}$ being a $\Sigma_1$-elementary substructure of all c.c.c. forcing extensions [7], supplies a template for a different kind of reconstruction: the Wightman axioms as an "existential closure" condition on the operational theory, asserting that structures realizable in extensions of the operational framework are already realized in it.

**Scope limits of operational and computational axiomatizations.** Two further works delimit what operational and computational axiom systems can carry. Work on computable distributions for probabilistic programming semantics shows the tension between continuous models and discrete computation: probabilistic programs model natural phenomena with reals and continuous distributions, but computation is fundamentally discrete, limiting how models are represented and how queries are computed; the resolution offered is Type-2 computability, under which a Turing-complete probabilistic programming language expresses exactly the Type-2 computable distributions [2]. Applied to the reconstruction claim, this raises a computability caveat: the Wightman axioms quantify over all Schwartz test functions $f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ and over arbitrary local observables, and a derivation from operational axioms—which are finite-protocol statements—must explain how the uncomputable content of the continuum of observables is reached; the Type-2 framework suggests the answer is a limit idealization, consistent with the valuation-first foundation in which $\mathbb{R}$ appears only as a limit idealization [9], but it means the reconstruction's conclusion is at best as effective as its continuum idealization permits.

Work on probabilistic cellular automata supplies a cautionary precedent from the discrete side: despite numerous programmes, ambitions and studies, there is no derivation of the dynamics of probabilistic cellular automata from more microscopic physical rules or from more fundamental physics, and the alternative possibility, repeatedly entertained, that PCA are more fundamental and standard physical descriptions must be derived from PCA rules, remains open [4]. The structural analogy is exact: PCA are an operational, probabilistic, discrete substrate, and the derivation of continuum physics from them has resisted proof, with the source noting that detailed balance is not naturally installed for PCA because parallel updating prevents gradient flow, and that the very concept of (semi-bounded) energy appears deeply related to a continuous time process [4]. Our reconstruction claim faces the analogous obstruction in its reversibility-to-dynamics step: continuous reversibility is an operational symmetry statement, and the derivation of a Hamiltonian dynamics with a semi-bounded energy (the spectrum condition) may fail for the same structural reason the PCA energy derivation fails—energy is a continuum concept that the discrete or operational substrate does not naturally carry. The PCA source also gives the entropy-production machinery ($J_{i,n}(\sigma) = \log \frac{p_i(\sigma_n(i)|\sigma_{n-1})}{p_i(\sigma_{n-1}(i)|\sigma_n)}$ as the measure of breaking detailed balance [4]), which in the quantum setting corresponds to the non-unitarity that the potentially non-unitary Wightman CFTs of [1] accommodate; a reconstruction that derives unitary Poincaré representation must therefore also explain why the operational substrate selects the unitary case among the non-unitary possibilities the literature studies.

Finally, work on emergent spacetime frameworks supplies the cautionary example of what an unfinished axiomatization looks like when it is overclaimed: a geometric unification program whose own validation section lists as its most significant hurdle the need to move beyond schematic descriptions and provide a complete, axiomatic definition of the full 'Category of Quantum Gravity,' including a precise characterization of all its objects and morphisms [8]. The lesson we take is definitional discipline: a reconstruction claim is only as strong as the precision of both axiom lists, and our audit therefore begins (Section 3) by fixing both lists exactly.

## 3. Methods

This paper's method is formalization, derivation, and counterexample; it performs no experiment, measurement, or simulation. We describe the model, the assumptions, the derivation procedure, and the computation.

**The model.** We work with two axiom systems, stated precisely.

*GPT side.* A generalized probabilistic theory is a pair $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{E})$ where $\mathcal{S}$ is a set of states and $\mathcal{E}$ a set of effects, with a joint probability map. We consider the four axioms as stated in the reconstruction claim:

- **(G1) Causality:** the set of probability-one states is non-empty and every state has a unique normalized probability-one extension; operationally, no influence propagates from future choices to past statistics.
- **(G2) No-signaling:** for composite systems $AB$, the marginal statistics of $A$ are independent of the measurement performed on $B$.
- **(G3) Local tomography:** the state of a composite $AB$ is determined by the statistics of product measurements $a \otimes b$; formally, the composite state space embeds in the tensor product of the factors.
- **(G4) Continuous reversibility:** for any two pure states there is a reversible transformation (a symmetry of the state space) carrying one to the other, and the group of reversible transformations acts continuously (in the topology induced by finite-protocol statistics).

The reconstruction claim adds the hypothesis of infinitely many degrees of freedom: the system is indexed by spacetime regions, with local state spaces $\mathcal{S}(O)$ for bounded regions $O \subset \mathbb{R}^{d}$.

*Wightman side.* The Gårding-Wightman axioms for a Hermitian scalar quantum field $\langle\mathcal{H}, U, \psi, D\rangle$: a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ with a strongly continuous unitary representation $U$ of the Poincaré group $\mathcal{P}_+$ satisfying the spectrum condition; a vacuum vector $\psi$ invariant under $U$; a field operator-valued distribution $\psi(f)$ on a dense domain $D$; covariance, locality (microcausality), and completeness. We also use the Haag-Kastler net formulation: an assignment $O \mapsto \mathcal{A}(O)$ of von Neumann algebras to bounded spacetime regions, with isotony, Poincaré covariance, and microcausality $\mathcal{A}(O_1) \subset \mathcal{A}(O_2)'$ for spacelike-separated $O_1, O_2$ [1].

**Assumptions.** We make the following assumptions, each stated because the sources do not supply it:

- **(A1)** We assume the GPT state spaces are locally finite-dimensional in the sense that each local state space $\mathcal{S}(O)$ for bounded $O$ embeds in a finite-dimensional real vector space, while the index set of regions is infinite; this is the standard reading of "infinitely many degrees of freedom" in an operational setting.
- **(A2)** We assume the causal relation on $\mathbb{R}^d$ is the standard Minkowski causal relation; the reconstruction claim's target is QFT on Minkowski space, so the causal structure is input, not derived. This is a substantive assumption we flag in Section 6.
- **(A3)** We assume the vacuum state exists as a translation-invariant state on the net; the GPT axioms do not supply it, and we track this as a gap in Section 4.3.
- **(A4)** For the uniqueness analysis (Proposition 4.4) we assume the standard classification of real, complex, and quaternionic Hilbert spaces as the three division-ring cases of quantum probability, which the operational literature treats as the canonical alternatives.

**Derivation procedure.** For each Wightman axiom $W_i$, we ask: do (G1)–(G4) plus (A1)–(A3) imply $W_i$? The procedure is: (i) translate $W_i$ into a statement about the net or the field; (ii) translate each GPT axiom into a statement about the same structure; (iii) either exhibit a derivation, exhibit the missing hypothesis explicitly, or exhibit a counterexample. Where a counterexample exists in the literature we cite it; where it must be constructed we construct it from the axioms alone.

**Computation.** The paper contains one quantitative computation: a count of the axiom-derivation steps and their status, and a counting argument for the non-uniqueness result (Section 4.4), both shown with full arithmetic. No numerical data from experiment is used anywhere in the paper.

## 4. Analysis

### 4.1 Locality: the derivation that completes

**Proposition 4.1.** Under (G1), (G2), (A1), and (A2), the localized observables of the net satisfy microcausality: for spacelike-separated regions $O_1, O_2$, all observables in $\mathcal{S}(O_1)$ commute (in the operational sense of joint measurability) with all observables in $\mathcal{S}(O_2)$.

*Derivation.* Step 1. By (A2), spacelike separation means no causal influence in the Minkowski sense. Step 2. By (G1) causality, the operational order of measurements is the only order relevant to joint statistics: a probability-one state exists and future choices do not affect past statistics. Step 3. By (G2) no-signaling applied to the composite of the degrees of freedom in $O_1$ and those in $O_2$ (which exists by (A1) and the tensor factorization of the composite), the marginal statistics on $O_1$ are independent of the choice of measurement on $O_2$. Step 4. For spacelike-separated regions, the operational equivalence of the two orderings (which is what relativity of simultaneity forces, given (A2)) plus no-signaling means the joint statistics factorize in both orders; the observables are jointly measurable in either order with identical joint statistics, which is the operational definition of commutation. $\square$

The derivation uses no property of quantum theory beyond the two operational axioms and the causal structure. This is the strongest pillar of the reconstruction claim: microcausality—the axiom that the Haag-Kastler framework takes as the definition of locality, $\mathcal{A}(O_1) \subset \mathcal{A}(O_2)'$ for spacelike-separated $O_1, O_2$—follows from causality plus no-signaling. The literature confirms this axiom is physically tested and load-bearing: microcausality is listed as a premise of the spin-statistics theorem and is recorded as tested, with each new precision measurement redrawing its boundary [10]. The reconstruction claim's locality pillar therefore has the right shape: an operationally motivated axiom yielding a technically central QFT axiom.

What the derivation does *not* deliver is the *-algebra structure: operational commutation of joint measurability is weaker than algebraic commutation $AB = BA$ in a von Neumann algebra. The bridge from operational joint measurability to the commutant structure $\mathcal{A}(O_2)'$ requires the representation of observables as operators, which is where the division-ring choice (Proposition 4.4) enters. We record this as Gap L1: the derivation of microcausality in algebraic form requires the observable representation, and the representation is not unique.

### 4.2 Local tomography: the counterexample

**Proposition 4.2.** Local tomography (G3) is not a necessary hypothesis for the Wightman axioms: there exist field theories satisfying the Gårding-Wightman axioms (in graded form) whose local state spaces violate local tomography.

*Counterexample.* The free fermion field. In the algebraic setting, Araki proved Haag duality for free fermionic fields, and the free fermion model is essentially the only example of a 2d chiral CFT whose smeared fields are bounded operators, so that the field net $\mathcal{P}(I)$ and the von Neumann algebra net $\mathcal{A}(I)$ coincide [1]. Fermionic state spaces compose under the graded tensor product, not the ordinary tensor product: the state of a composite is not determined by statistics of product measurements alone, which is precisely the failure of (G3). Yet the free fermion net satisfies isotony, Poincaré covariance, and microcausality (with anticommutation at spacelike separation, which is the graded form of microcausality), and Haag duality—the strongest locality property—holds. $\square$

The consequence for the reconstruction claim is sharp: a theorem of the form "(G1)–(G4) + infinite degrees of freedom $\Rightarrow$ Wightman axioms" cannot have (G3) as a necessary hypothesis, because its conclusion is satisfied by theories violating (G3). Two repairs are available. Repair R1: weaken (G3) to graded local tomography, i.e., allow the $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded tensor product; then the derivation of Proposition 4.1 goes through with commutation replaced by graded commutation, and the conclusion is the graded Wightman axioms, which is the correct generality since fermionic fields anticommute at spacelike separation. Repair R2: drop (G3) and accept that the composite structure must be derived from something else; no candidate replacement is known to us in the GPT literature, so we take R1 as the working convention. We state this choice explicitly: **we take Repair R1, the graded tensor product, as the convention for the rest of the paper**, because it is the weakest modification that preserves the Proposition 4.1 derivation and matches the known fermionic models [1].

This pattern—weaken one axiom, exhibit the counterexample, identify which conclusions survive—follows the methodology of the topology study in which weakening the union axiom to the countable union axiom preserves some continuity results while a concrete indicator-function counterexample (continuous in the Michael line topology, discontinuous in the countable union topology) shows exactly which property fails [3].

### 4.3 Continuous reversibility and the spectrum condition: the missing input

**Proposition 4.3.** (G4) plus (A1)–(A3) yields a strongly continuous unitary representation of a symmetry group $G$ acting on the net, but does not yield the spectrum condition; the spectrum condition is additional input.

*Derivation.* Step 1. By (G4), for any two pure states there is a reversible transformation, and the group of reversible transformations acts continuously. In the infinite-degree-of-freedom setting with the region index, the subgroup of reversible transformations compatible with the causal structure (A2) includes the Poincaré group as the automorphism group of Minkowski space. Step 2. Continuity of the action plus the operational topology gives strong continuity of the induced map on the representation; by the standard construction (Wigner-type), the symmetries are represented by unitary or antiunitary operators, and the causality axiom (G1) selects the unitary (orientation- and time-orientation-preserving) part. So far, a strongly continuous unitary representation $U$ of $\mathcal{P}_+$ exists. Step 3. The spectrum condition requires that the joint spectrum of the generators $(P^0, \vec{P})$ lie in the closed forward light cone: $\mathrm{spec}(P) \subset \overline{V}_+$. Nothing in (G1)–(G4) constrains the spectrum of the translation generators: continuous reversibility gives a group representation, and a group representation can equally well be composed with an automorphism of $\mathbb{R}$ that reverses or distorts the energy sign locally. The operational axioms constrain the *group action*, not the *spectral geometry* of its generator. $\square$

The insertion point is precise: the spectrum condition must be added as a fifth axiom, or derived from a physical postulate about stability (positive energy means the vacuum is stable), which is not among the operational axioms. The literature corroborates that the spectrum condition is an independent, load-bearing premise: it is listed explicitly among the assumptions of the spin-statistics theorem—Lorentz invariance, microcausality, positive energy, positive-norm Hilbert space, exactly $3+1$ dimensions—as premises of a theorem, not of the universe [10]. And constructive work confirms it is inserted by hand even in explicit constructions: the Lévy-field construction builds the spectral support into the pseudo-differential operator's symbol, which is nonzero only for $\tau > (\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\xi_k^2 + m^2)^{1/2}$, i.e., only on the forward mass shell [5]; the positive-$\tau$ support is the spectrum condition, imposed in the construction, not derived.

There is a second gap in the same step. The operational axioms give a group action on states; the Wightman framework requires a *vacuum* vector $\psi$ invariant under $U$, cyclic for the field algebra. We assumed the vacuum exists (A3), but its existence is not derived: the PCA precedent is instructive, since in that setting detailed balance (the analogue of a stationary vacuum-like state) is not naturally installed, because parallel updating prevents gradient flow, and the concept of semi-bounded energy appears deeply related to a continuous time process [4]. The structural moral transfers: an operational, reversibility-based substrate does not naturally carry a stationary positive-energy state; the vacuum and the spectrum condition may both be continuum concepts that the operational substrate does not force.

### 4.4 Non-uniqueness across division rings

**Proposition 4.4.** The reconstruction, even if completed, does not select complex quantum field theory uniquely: real and quaternionic Hilbert-space field theories satisfy (G1)–(G4) (in graded form) and the Wightman axioms' structural requirements.

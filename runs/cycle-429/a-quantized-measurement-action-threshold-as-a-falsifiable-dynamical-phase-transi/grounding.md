## Research idea

Quantized Action Threshold Governing Measurement-Induced Phase Transitions. The Quantum Toll Framework conjectures that a definite measurement outcome emerges only when the accumulated measurement action crosses a quantized threshold S ≥ nħ; below it, dynamics are non-unitary 'flickering' rather than collapse or decoherence, constituting a new physical signature distinct from noise. This matters because it converts the measurement problem into a falsifiable dynamical phase transition with a critical point at S = ħ, derivable from Landauer's principle plus the time-energy uncertainty relation (yielding S_min ≈ ħ ln 2). A theoretical paper could formalize the free-energy functional F[ρ] = ⟨H⟩ − TS(ρ) + λΔI, prove the second-order transition and mean-field critical exponent α = 1, and derive the Fisher-information threshold t_c = 2/F_Q for qubit weak measurements. Computationally, one could simulate weakly measured qubits or superconducting circuits, testing whether outcome fidelity and pointer-state stability exhibit predicted critical scaling and sub-threshold stochastic switching, with explicit falsification criteria distinguishing the effect from ordinary decoherence.

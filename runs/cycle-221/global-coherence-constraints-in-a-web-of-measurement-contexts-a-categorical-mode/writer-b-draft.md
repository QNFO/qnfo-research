# Reality as a Web of Measurement Contexts: A Categorical Trilemma for the Collapse of the Ontic–Epistemic Distinction

## Abstract

A recurring conjecture in relational approaches to ontology holds that there is no measurement-independent "territory": what a thing *is* equals the totality of its behaviors across all measurement contexts, related by translation rules and subject to global coherence conditions. If correct, this would dissolve the ontic/epistemic distinction and change how foundational theories are constructed and evaluated. The conjecture has lacked a formal test: no existing framework states precisely when coherence forces a unique consistent assignment of values per context, and when no context-independent value exists. We construct such a test. We model measurement contexts as objects of a category, translation rules as morphisms acting on finite value sets, and global coherence as the existence of a consistent section. We prove a trilemma: for a connected context web, global consistency of assignments, absence of a context-free invariant value, and aperiodicity of translation loops are mutually incompatible — any two force the negation of the third. We derive exact counts: a three-context cycle over $\mathbb{Z}/7$ with unit translations has $0$ consistent sections; restoring one translation yields $7$ sections and $7$ context-free values; a commuting diamond admits $74{,}088$ coherent translation assignments out of $3{,}111{,}696$, a fraction $\frac{1}{42} \approx 0.0238095$; a two-step majority-rule coarse-graining chain has $2$ fixed points among $512$ microscopic configurations. The strong collapse thesis — no context-free value whatsoever — is therefore refuted in its naive form: the invariant reappears, but as the fixed-point set of the loop monoid, derived from the web rather than prior to it.

## 1. Introduction

Researchers in the foundations of quantum mechanics, in philosophy of physics, and in relational approaches to ontology face a persistent structural problem: their theories posit a measurement-independent reality — a "territory" — whose relation to the observed, context-dependent "map" is never specified beyond the map itself. This is not only a conceptual inconvenience. It blocks concrete work. In quantum information, resource theories must decide whether a property such as coherence or discord resides in a state independently of the measurement scenario used to probe it; the analysis in [3] shows that even the classification of bipartite coherence as "genuinely quantum" depends on whether the coherence is global or reducible to local coherence of the subsystems, which is precisely a question about measurement contexts. In superdeterministic responses to quantum non-locality, the axiomatic cost of dropping Measurement Independence is contested for lack of a framework that says what a "measurement context" contributes ontologically; the analysis in [10] documents how such axiom-violating theories are expelled from research programmes without a formal path for comparative assessment. And in discrete relational ontologies, the claim that nodes and edges — not points in a pre-existing space — are the fundamental layer, as argued in [11] and [9], remains informal precisely because no calculus exists that says what a "value of a quantity" means once contexts are primitive.

What would change if the present paper's result holds? First, the slogan "the ontic collapses into the totality of context-relative behaviors" would acquire a precise, checkable content: one could compute, for any proposed context web, whether a context-free invariant exists and, if so, exhibit it as a derived object. Second, the trilemma we prove would act as a consistency screen for proposed relational ontologies: any theory that simultaneously demands global consistency of all context-relative descriptions, denies any context-free invariant, and posits nontrivial translation loops between contexts is formally incoherent and must give up one horn. Third, the counting techniques developed here (section counts, coherent-translation fractions, coarse-graining fixed-point counts) give researchers finite, mechanical tests that can be run on toy models before a relational proposal is elaborated.

The specific gap is this. The source conjecture states two claims: (a) coherence conditions analogous to product formulas or renormalization-group (RG) flow force a *unique* consistent assignment of values per context, and (b) *no context-independent value exists* — no terminal object or invariant section. Neither claim has been derived or refuted in a setting where "context," "translation rule," and "coherence" are mathematical objects. Existing literature supplies the motivation but not the calculus: the interface-theoretic argument in [9] that experience is a simplified representation generated by an observer-subgraph interacting with a network stops short of defining what consistency across observer-subgraphs requires; the discrete-network formalism in [11] proves results about $p$-adic distance limits and node counting but does not treat the assignment of observable values across contexts; and the resource-theoretic result in [3] that global coherence of bipartite states is "a form of bipartite coherence that is not local coherence in either of the subsystems or in both subsystems alone" is a statement about one quantum structure, not a general framework.

We supply the framework and the derivations. Our contributions are:

- **Formalization.** We define a *context web* as a small category $\mathcal{C}$ whose objects are measurement contexts, equipped with a functor $V: \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{FinSet}$ assigning each context a finite set of possible values and each translation rule a function between value sets (Section 3).
- **Trilemma theorem.** For a connected context web in which every translation loop is reachable through a spanning tree, we prove: global consistency (a section exists), absence of a context-free invariant value, and aperiodicity of the loop monoid are mutually incompatible; specifically, a section exists if and only if the loop monoid at a base context has a common fixed point, and the context-free invariant values are exactly that fixed-point set (derived and proved in Section 4, Proposition 4.4).
- **Exact counting results.** We compute, with full arithmetic: $0$ consistent sections for a three-context cycle over $\mathbb{Z}/7$ with unit translation rules; $7$ sections and $7$ context-free values when one rule is adjusted; $74{,}088$ coherent translation assignments out of $3{,}111{,}696$ total for a commuting diamond, a coherent fraction of $\frac{1}{42} \approx 0.0238095$ (Section 4).
- **RG case study.** For a two-step majority-rule coarse-graining chain on $9$ bits we compute $2$ fixed points among $512$ microscopic configurations (fraction $\frac{1}{256} = 0.00390625$) and $2$ fixed points among $8$ descriptions at the intermediate layer (fraction $0.25$), showing that cross-scale translation leaves only trivial homogeneous invariants (Section 4).
- **Interpretation.** We show the strong collapse thesis fails and the weak collapse thesis holds: the context-free invariant reappears as the loop fixed-point set, an object determined entirely by the web of translation rules rather than posited prior to them (Sections 5 and 6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The conjecture under test descends from a family of relational and interface-theoretic proposals. The synthesis in [9] presents the central thesis in its clearest form: the fundamental ontology is a static, discrete, relational network, and "our experience of reality is an epistemic interface generated by the interaction of a subgraph (the observer) with the network," an interface that "includes space, time, objects, causality, and the self" but "does not reveal the underlying structure" [9]. The same work insists on a strict epistemic–ontological separation — "the static relational network is an ontological claim; our experience of time and space is an epistemic phenomenon" [9] — which is exactly the distinction our trilemma puts under pressure: if values are context-relative all the way down, the separation becomes a definition rather than a metaphysical divide. Our contribution is the calculus that decides when the separation can be maintained.

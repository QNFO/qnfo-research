## 1.0 Abstract

This document presents a **pattern hierarchy** — a unified generative framework for classifying and relating patterns across all scales of reality. Rooted in the Pattern-Based Ontology, it proposes that all phenomena, from quantum particles to conscious experience, can be understood as patterns at different levels of **distinction-nesting complexity** `[my conjecture]`.

The framework replaces the earlier "Autaxys" terminology with explicit grounding in Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form*: the single primitive operation of drawing a distinction (the mark `#` and enclosure `[ ]`) recursively generates all pattern levels. Pattern stability emerges when a set of nested distinctions reaches **closure** — a state where reduction rules (Calling, Crossing) produce no further simplification. This closure is not an additional principle but a *consequence* of the primitives `[derived]`.

Physical properties — mass, charge, spin — are not fundamental inputs but **derived characteristics** of stable normal forms in the Syntactic Token Calculus `[speculative — mathematical derivation in progress]`. The hierarchy demonstrates scale invariance: the same generative principles that produce quantum particles also produce chemical elements, biological organisms, and self-aware consciousness `[PHILOSOPHY, my conjecture]`.

## 2.0 The Seven-Level Pattern Hierarchy

Patterns are classified by the **depth of distinction nesting** — how many layers of recursive marking and enclosure are required to define a pattern's stable normal form.

### 2.1 Scale Invariance

The same generative mechanism — recursive distinction — operates at all seven levels `[my conjecture]`. A pattern at level Sₙ is built from patterns at level Sₙ₋₁ that have achieved mutual closure. The transition from one level to the next is not a change in governing principles but an increase in distinction-nesting depth.

This is the formal basis for the earlier "Ontological Closure" concept: closure is **pattern stability** — the condition where a set of distinctions reaches a normal form under the reduction rules and persists against external perturbation.

### 2.2 Emergence as Pattern Closure

A higher-level pattern "emerges" when a set of lower-level patterns achieves **distinction closure**: their mutual boundaries form a new, stable, higher-order boundary. This is not a new principle — it is the same Calling/Crossing reduction applied to composite token expressions `[derived]`.

Example: Hydrogen (S₂) emerges when the proton pattern [P⁺] and electron pattern [e⁻] form a mutual closure [[P⁺][e⁻]] that stabilizes as a single token expression at the next nesting level.

### 2.3 Consciousness (S₇): The Self-Referential Pattern

At level S₇, a pattern achieves a unique property: it **contains a representation of itself as a pattern** — it makes a distinction that includes the distinguisher. This is the pattern-based interpretation of the thesis that "consciousness is the universe observing itself through a self-aware pattern" `[PHILOSOPHY, speculative, not yet falsifiable]`.

The formal structure of S₇ patterns is the subject of ongoing research (Pillar 5 of the [PBO Research Roadmap](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21369313)). The Syntactic Token Calculus analog of self-reference — the "re-entry" of a distinction into itself (Spencer-Brown's Chapter 11) — provides a candidate formal mechanism.

## 3.0 Generative Principles

The pattern hierarchy is governed by three principles, all derived from the primitive operations of the distinction calculus:

**3.1 Parsimony** `[derived]` — The minimal pattern that achieves closure is preferred. This follows from Calling (`## → #`): redundant marks collapse to unity.

**3.2 Coherence** `[derived]` — Only self-consistent token expressions persist. This follows from Crossing (`[#] → ∅`): self-contradictory patterns cancel to void.

**3.3 Maximal Complexity** `[my conjecture]` — Within the constraints of Parsimony and Coherence, the pattern network explores the richest possible configuration space. This is the pattern-based interpretation of the Second Law's tendency toward informational dispersion, balanced against Coherence's constraint on stability.

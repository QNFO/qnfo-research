Open Questions That Led Me From Fault-Tolerant QEC to p-Adic Metrology

CWI Summer School on QA & QEC · 24–28 August 2026 · Poster Session Wed 26 Aug

Five Questions I Couldn't Answer

① THE ENERGY WALL
Landauer's principle: erasing one bit costs kT ln 2. Each round of active QEC erases information. Is there an accepted scaling law for the thermodynamic cost of fault-tolerant computation at utility scale? I've been looking and haven't found it.
② THE CLASSICAL SHADOW
The decoder must process syndromes faster than decoherence. At what scale does the classical co-processor's own dissipation become a dominant error source?
③ THE GEOMETRY OF NOISE
Noise correlations in real systems exhibit nested, hierarchical (ultrametric) structure. If noise already self-organizes hierarchically, are we leaving free passive resilience unused by not matching our codes to that geometry?
④ WHY A CIRCUIT?
Active QEC enforces a digital circuit abstraction on continuous quantum dynamics. When physics requires this much external control to suppress natural dynamics, is the model the problem?
⑤ WHAT FALSIFIES THE ROADMAP?
"Roughly ten years" since the mid-1990s. What empirical result would demonstrate the FTQC roadmap needs fundamental revision? If we don't have a falsification condition, how do we distinguish research from belief?
In an ultrametric space, all triangles are isosceles. Noise clusters hierarchically. A single global measurement cancels local errors automatically — no ancillas, no syndrome extraction, no feedback loop.

Where This Led Me

p-Adic quantum metrology exploits the hierarchical noise structure that active QEC ignores. A room-temperature photonic platform with ~10 entangled photons already beats the standard quantum limit.

Resource comparison: Surface-code QC needs ~10⁶ physical qubits at millikelvin. A p-adic sensor needs ~10 photons at room temperature.

A toy model, honestly reported: I simulated a code on a Bruhat-Tits tree (working paper v1.10). It suggested redundancy may be quantized — then its most interesting signature ("forbidden window") failed its own falsification test. I'm not claiming it's physics yet. I'd genuinely value a reason it must be an artifact — or a pointer to anyone who's seen this.

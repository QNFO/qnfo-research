Panel 1 — Why this poster exists

I am an independent researcher who came to QEC from ultrametric geometry and the energy cost of computation. This poster is a notebook of confusions treated as data.

Every question below shares one property: it makes no sense outside the frame it questions. That is deliberate: where a question becomes nonsense is exactly where a definition is doing the work.

The method, in four steps:

  • State the construct in its own terms — surface codes, thresholds, logical qubits, decoders.
  • Ask the question I am not supposed to need to ask, exactly as a naive outsider would.
  • Watch where the question stops parsing — that locale is the seam.
  • Treat the seam as data: the construct carried more than its proof.
Correct me, and the poster has done its job. I am not claiming the field is wrong; I am asking where the pieces were checked against each other. If the constructs were compatible, the naive questions would answer themselves.

Panel 2 — The comparison: tree-based vs surface codes

One instance of the class: the Bruhat–Tits tree T₂.
The class: nested, distinction-based ultrametric trees.

Surface codes (the standard): qubits on a 2D lattice; active syndrome measurement; a classical decoder decides what to correct. The code is defined by its boundary.

Tree-based codes (the alternative): nested, distinction-based hierarchical structures — ultrametric trees; the Bruhat–Tits tree T₂ is one instance, not the concept. A [[3,1,1]] perfect tensor at every internal vertex; protection from concatenation across the tree, not from active loops.

All numbers are classical simulation. No tree code has run on hardware.

Panel 3 — Questions 1–4

1 · What is a logical qubit, physically?
Outside the frame: "logical" is not a physical predicate — the question is nonsense.
Inside, the answer is a definition: a logical qubit is defined as what the code protects. The code defines the thing it protects; the definition is the protection.
2 · Why does a surface code need a surface?
Outside the frame: a code is a set of states; no geometry is required.
Inside, the answer is a definition: the logical operators are defined by the boundary. The surface defines the code that defines the surface — the geometry is the definition.
3 · Where is the error in the error model?
Outside the frame: an error is an error; the question is empty.
Inside, the answer is a definition: the noise model defines which errors exist. Correlated errors are defined out of the frame, then declared absent. The definition is the measurement.
4 · What does the threshold guarantee?
Outside the frame: a number guarantees nothing.
Inside, the answer is a definition: the threshold is a crossing point inside a model family. The guarantee holds inside the model that defines it, and is undefined outside it.

Panel 4 — Questions 5–8

5 · Why must correction be a loop?
Outside the frame: correction needs no loop.
Inside, the answer is a definition: QEC is defined as measure → classical decoder → feedback. A code that corrects by geometry alone is outside the definition — which is the claim under test.
6 · What is one logical qubit worth?
Outside the frame: worth is not a code property.
Inside, the answer is a definition: the rate n:k is a definition of the family. The cost stays a definition until it is priced in joules per correct answer.
7 · What does distance d = 1 mean?
Outside the frame: a "code" of distance 1 corrects nothing — it is not a code.
Inside, the answer is a definition: d is the minimum logical-operator weight; d = 1 means "corrects nothing by definition" — and concatenation across the tree turns the definition into protection. The tree is where the definition stops being the answer.
8 · Is the decoder part of the code?
Outside the frame: malformed — a decoder is a decoder.
Inside, the answer is a definition: the quantum/classical boundary is drawn by convention, and the decoder's energy is priced nowhere. The convention is the frame; the frame is the cost.

Panel 5 — What would change my mind

A matched-noise comparison: the tree family vs a surface code under the same correlated-noise model, in classical simulation. If the advantage falls to parity, the comparison on this poster is answered.
A decoder reality check: does a known decoder family already cover tree-structured codes, and how does its complexity scale?
An independent re-run: the simulator and the protocol are handed over to anyone who wants to run the comparison themselves.
Perfect tensors for p > 2: the binary [[3,1,1]] case is settled; larger primes are open.

What is not claimed: no hardware result, no fault-tolerance claim under circuit-level noise, no advantage under independent errors — the qudit family sits at ≈2×10⁻⁴ there, below surface codes. The claim is narrow: under correlated failure, the tree geometry may need fewer active interventions, and the matched comparison decides it.

Numeric criteria: matched-noise parity ⇒ the advantage claim is withdrawn · a decoder that already covers trees ⇒ ask 2 answered · an independent re-run that fails ⇒ the simulation claim is withdrawn. Every criterion is stated with its number.

Panel 6 — The statement, the caption, the seams

The one-line statement: "My confusions are the fault lines: where a question makes no sense outside the frame, the frame is answering with a definition."

The caption: the naive question is the probe. I am not claiming the field is wrong — I am asking where the pieces were checked against each other. If the constructs were compatible, the naive questions would answer themselves.

Seam 1 — Discrete abstraction: QEC defends the discreteness of the circuit model; the choice of discreteness is never priced.
Seam 2 — Logical qubit: defined as what the code protects; the definition is the protection.
Seam 3 — Noise model: the model defines which errors exist; thresholds live inside the model.
Seam 4 — Classical control: the decoder is classical; the loop's energy is priced nowhere.
Seam 5 — Cost currency: T-count is countable, so it is counted; joules are measurable, and not measured.
Seam 6 — Geometry: Euclidean 2D assumed; the tree geometry unexamined. The staircase signature I could not find examined anywhere.

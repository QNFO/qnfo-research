# Abstract — Ultrametric Quantum Error Correction

**Title:** Ultrametric Quantum Error Correction: Testing Discrete Geometry in the
Redundancy–Fidelity Tradeoff

**Event:** Summer School on Quantum Algorithms & Quantum Error Correction, CWI, Amsterdam,
24–28 August 2026 (poster).

**Abstract:**

Standard frameworks in quantum error correction (QEC) treat noise as accumulating over a
continuous topology, so logical fidelity is expected to scale smoothly with physical
redundancy. This poster proposes an experimental protocol to test an alternative: a discrete,
hierarchical error geometry modeled on the Bruhat–Tits tree $T_p$, under which protection would
quantize in units of $(p+1)$ and fidelity would trace a staircase — flat plateaus punctuated by
abrupt jumps — rather than a smooth curve. The four-step protocol encodes a logical qubit in a
Shor [[9,1,3]] code and partitions the environment into hierarchical fragments to isolate this
signature. As an independent cross-check, we report a valuation-based structural witness already
tested against data: the Mahler-spectrum conjecture ($v_p^{\max}$ separates optimal from random
codes) fails to generalize when extended to eight additional stabilizer families — only
Golay-type self-dual codes separate ($v_p^{\max} = 28$), while the Shor, Steane, perfect, and
surface codes cluster at the random-ensemble baseline. This bounded negative result is reported
in full as a methodological asset: knowing which witnesses fail sharpens the search for the ones
that might work, and frames the proposed experiment as a falsification test rather than a claim.

## 2. The graded braiding and its CST substrate

In the mark calculus with compact closure and self-duality (M ⊗ M simple — the abelian-pair postulate, RES.009), the exchange map on two marks is a braiding

$$\sigma_{M,M}: M \otimes M \to M \otimes M, \qquad \sigma_{M,M}^2 \neq \mathrm{id} \ \text{in general}.$$

Configuration-space topology supplies the *classification* of what this braiding can be. For two indistinguishable particles in $d$ spatial dimensions:

$$\pi_1\bigl(C_2(\mathbb{R}^d)\bigr) = \begin{cases} B_2 \cong \mathbb{Z} & d = 2 \ \text{(braid group)} \\ S_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 & d \ge 3 \ \text{(symmetric group)}. \end{cases}$$

The involutive case ($d \ge 3$) is exactly the graded-braiding condition $\sigma_{M,M}^2 = \mathrm{id}$ that RES.009's T1 postulates. **The topological substrate and the algebraic postulate are the same statement** — this is the first concrete synthesis result of RES.011: the abelian-pair postulate + involutive braiding ≡ $\pi_1(C_2) = \mathbb{Z}_2$.

## 3. Construction: the two projectors

Given an involutive braiding $\sigma^2 = \mathrm{id}$ on an object $M$ with $M \otimes M$ semisimple, define the symmetrizer and antisymmetrizer:

$$P_{\mathrm{sym}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathrm{id} + \sigma), \qquad P_{\mathrm{ant}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathrm{id} - \sigma).$$

These are idempotent, orthogonal projectors ($P^2 = P$, $P_{\mathrm{sym}}P_{\mathrm{ant}} = 0$), splitting

$$M \otimes M = \mathrm{Sym}^2(M) \oplus \Lambda^2(M).$$

The modal exponentials of linear logic / DiLL are the graded completions:

$$\mathrm{Sym}(M) = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} \mathrm{Sym}^n(M) \quad (\text{!}M\text{, the of-course modality}),$$
$$\Lambda(M) = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} \Lambda^n(M) \quad (\text{the exterior exponential}).$$

**Construction content achieved:** for $n = 2$, the two statistics projectors are the first symmetric/exterior powers; the braiding $\sigma$ is the exchange operator whose $\pm 1$ eigenvalues are the boson/fermion signs. This matches the RES.009 finding that $P_{\mathrm{sym}}/P_{\mathrm{ant}}$ are the two one-dimensional characters of $S_n$ — now with the CST reading: they are the two one-dimensional unitary representations of $\pi_1(C_N(\mathbb{R}^d))$, $d \ge 3$.

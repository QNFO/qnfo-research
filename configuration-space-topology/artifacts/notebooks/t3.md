## 1. Target statement (inherited from RES.009)

> Show that spatial dimension enters exchange statistics only through the allowed braided structures.

## 2. The base theorem (established)

For the Euclidean configuration space of N point particles,

$$\pi_1\bigl(C_N(\mathbb{R}^d)\bigr) = \begin{cases} B_N & d = 2 \\ S_N & d \ge 3 \end{cases}$$

— dimension enters ONLY through this classification. All statistics-relevant structure (exchange phases, braid representations) is downstream of π₁. In this sense the target is already established for point particles in Euclidean space: the entire dimension-dependence of statistics is the d=2 vs d≥3 dichotomy of π₁. **The "3D as shadow" claim (C1/C2) is exactly this theorem read philosophically: the ±1 binary is what the braid-group structure *becomes* when the involutive quotient S_N ≅ B_N/⟨σ²=1⟩ applies.**

## 3. Where the claim must be re-stated (the honest boundary)

### 3.1 The orbifold complication (Harshman & Knapp 10.1103/PhysRevA.105.052214)
Including singular points (not deleting the diagonal) makes the configuration space an orbifold; 1D systems then support non-trivial exchange statistics (alternate strand groups, non-abelian anyons in 1D). **Consequence:** "dimension enters only through braided structures" is true *for the deleted-diagonal framework*. The deleted diagonal is itself a modeling choice (per the UIA scaffold audit in the source notes: "the hard-core diagonal is artificial"). Re-statement: *dimension enters only through the allowed braided structures **in the hard-core point-particle framework***.

### 3.2 Graph configuration spaces (Sawicki 1408.7002; Harrison et al. 1304.5781; Maciążek–Sawicki 1806.02846)
On graphs, "dimension" is not the operative parameter — *connectivity* is: 2-connected graphs admit anyon phases; non-planar 3-connected graphs allow only bosons/fermions. **Consequence:** the honest generalization is that the *topological classification* (not dimension per se) is what enters statistics. Dimension (Euclidean case) is a proxy for π₁. This *strengthens* the C2 claim's mathematical spine (topology, not dimension, is the invariant) while forcing the precise statement.

### 3.3 Supersymmetric anyons in 3D (Chaichian, Tureanu, Zhang 1204.5025)
Under extended Poincaré supersymmetry in 3D, irreducible unitary representations of ANY spin appear (supersymmetric anyons). **Consequence:** the spin side is not bounded by dimension the way the exchange side is. This belongs to the C3 boundary (spin-statistics connection needs the physical input); it does not threaten the exchange-statistics claim (π₁ = S_N still classifies the exchange sector), but the paper must separate "exchange statistics" (dimension-governed) from "spin values" (supersymmetry-sensitive).

### 3.4 The traid group (1D, three-body hard core: 1803.11000, 2309.04358)
Hard-core *three-body* constraints in 1D create co-dimension-two defects → π₁ = traid group, neither S_N nor B_N. **Consequence:** even in fixed dimension, the *constraint structure* (two-body vs three-body hard core) changes the topology. "Dimension enters only through braided structures" must be indexed also by the constraint type — the calculus's framework-indexing must carry (d, constraint-type).

### 3.5 Extended objects (10.1007/978-3-0348-0448-6_19; Zenodo 18725887 Vortex Framework)
Configuration spaces of extended objects (loops, vortices) admit exotic statistics beyond the point-particle classification. **Consequence:** the point-particle idealization is itself a scaffold (per UIA scaffold audit); the claim's scope is explicitly point-particle.

## 1. Target statement (inherited from RES.009)

> Show that the exchange map on two marks is a scalar, and that the ribbon identity forces the two eigenvalues to be the boson and fermion signs.

## 2. Setup: the exchange map as an endomorphism of Hom

Let M be the mark object, self-dual with evaluation ε: M⊗M → 1 and coevaluation η: 1 → M⊗M. The exchange map on two marks is the braiding σ_{M,M} ∈ End(M⊗M). **Scalar claim:** under the abelian-pair postulate (M⊗M simple), Schur's lemma gives

$$\mathrm{End}(M \otimes M) \cong k,$$

so σ_{M,M} = λ · id for a scalar λ — the exchange phase. This is the categorical avatar of "the wavefunction picks up a phase under exchange" (CST: a one-dimensional representation of π₁(C_N)).

## 3. The ribbon identity and the ±1 dichotomy

In a ribbon category (compact closed + twist θ), the ribbon identity for the composite object is

$$\theta_{M \otimes M} = \sigma_{M,M} \circ (\theta_M \otimes \theta_M) \circ \sigma_{M,M}.$$

For self-dual M with the ribbon condition η ∘ θ_M = ε (the mark as its own dual, twist compatible), the exchange scalar satisfies

$$\lambda^2 = 1 \iff \sigma_{M,M}^2 = \mathrm{id}.$$

**CST-integrated reading (the synthesis achieved):** the involutive braiding σ² = id is precisely the statement that the braid group collapses to the symmetric group (Artin: $S_n \cong B_n / \langle \sigma_i^2 = 1 \rangle$), i.e. that $\pi_1(C_N(\mathbb{R}^d)) = S_N$ for $d \ge 3$. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\ R = e^{2\pi i s} = \pm 1 \iff \pi_1\bigl(C_N(\mathbb{R}^d)\bigr) = S_N \ \text{(d ≥ 3)}\ }$$

The two eigenvalues of the exchange scalar are the boson sign (+1) and the fermion sign (−1) — the "Tyranny of the ±1" is the ribbon identity evaluated in the d ≥ 3 topological sector. This gives RES.010's R = e^{2πis} its CST meaning: R is the general exchange scalar; ±1 is its involutive-sector restriction.

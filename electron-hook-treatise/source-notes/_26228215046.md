### Title
**The Electron as a Hook: A Critical Treatise on the Load-Bearing Assumptions of Quantum Mechanics, Thermodynamics, and Computation**

1. **Abstract**
   - Summary of thesis: the electron is not a settled object but a diagnostic probe for foundational assumptions.
   - Scope: Hilbert space, spin-statistics, measurement, valuation, thermodynamics, computation, vacuum, dimensionality, emergence.
   - Method: distinguish measured fact from postulate; identify domains of validity; define falsifiability.

## Introduction

### 0.1 Motivation
- The electron as the most precisely measured particle.
- The electron as the basis of modern electronics and computation.
- The hidden assumptions that the electron’s success conceals.
- Why foundational questions are not separate from practical technology.

### 0.2 The Central Claim
- The electron is a point of failure or convergence for many deep assumptions.
- Examining those assumptions is scientifically necessary, not merely philosophical.
- The goal is not to reject but to map domains of validity.

### 0.3 Methodology
- For each assumption:
  1. State the assumption explicitly.
  2. Identify what is built on it.
  3. Distinguish confirmed predictions from postulates.
  4. List alternative formulations.
  5. Define experimental or theoretical falsifiability.
  6. Open questions.
- Use only scientific criteria: predictive power, empirical adequacy, internal consistency, domain of validity.

- Overview of parts and chapters.

## Conclusion

### 51.1 Summary of the Argument
- The electron is a hook for foundational assumptions.
- Many assumptions are load-bearing but unproven.
- Domains of validity must be mapped empirically.

### 51.2 What This Treatise Does Not Claim
- Does not claim current physics is wrong.
- Does not claim alternatives are correct.
- Does not claim philosophy replaces experiment.

### 51.3 What This Treatise Does Claim
- The assumptions are contingent.
- They are testable in principle.

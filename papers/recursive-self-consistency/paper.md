# Two Errata to "Recursive Self-Consistency as a Monistic Foundation for Physical Reality": Correcting the Gödel Completeness Attribution and the Flatness-to-Zero-Energy Inference

## Abstract

The preprint "Recursive Self-Consistency as a Monistic Foundation for Physical Reality" [4] rests two of its central arguments on statements that do not follow from the sources they invoke. First, its Section 1.1.1 attributes to Gödel's completeness theorem the claim that "if physics is a complete and consistent formal theory, no proper extension can add explanatory power without introducing an inconsistency"; the completeness theorem is a statement about first-order validity and provability and supports no such claim, and under the natural formal readings the claim itself is either vacuous or false. Second, its Sections 1.2.1 and 1.3.3 infer from the cosmic flatness bound $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$ that "zero total energy" is "confirmed to within experimental error"; we show that flatness constrains only the dimensionless ratio of total to kinetic Newtonian energy, $|E|/K = |\Omega_k| < 0.002$, and bounds the density parameter to $0.998 \leq \Omega_{\text{total}} \leq 1.002$, which is a statement about spatial curvature, not about an absolute total energy, which general relativity does not define for a closed universe. This erratum supplies corrected replacement text for both passages, derives the counterexample that refutes the Gödel claim under the language-extension reading, and computes the lower bound $R_{\text{curv}} > 95.8$ Gpc (about $3.12 \times 10^{11}$ ly) implied by the flatness bound under an assumed $H_0 = 70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$. The corrections remove two unsupported load-bearing claims while leaving the remainder of the framework's mathematical machinery untouched.

## 1. Introduction

A preprint that asserts a theorem it cannot source, or an empirical confirmation it cannot derive, imposes a real cost on its readers: every downstream argument that cites the assertion inherits the error, and correcting the record becomes harder as the preprint circulates. The preprint at issue [4] proposes a monistic foundation for quantum cosmology built on a fixed-point principle, and it is read by an audience interested in foundational physics and mathematical self-reference; two of its load-bearing claims — one logical, one cosmological — are stated in forms that a careful reader cannot verify from the authorities they name. The logical claim, in Section 1.1.1 of [4], misattributes a substantive theorem to Gödel's completeness theorem of 1930. The cosmological claim, in Sections 1.2.1 and 1.3.3 of [4], converts a standard curvature constraint, $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$, into a purported experimental confirmation of "zero total energy." If these two claims are corrected, the preprint's Table 1.1.1.1 — which scores competing frameworks partly on "Empirical Validation: Satisfies: Matches cosmic flatness" — loses one of the entries it relies on, and the preprint's rejection of external foundations loses its purported logical-theorem backing; what survives is the framework's mathematical construction, which stands or falls on its own derivations rather than on the two corrected sentences.

The problem this erratum solves is therefore concrete: a reader of [4] today must either accept two unverifiable statements as established or discard the surrounding argument wholesale. After the corrections derived here, the reader can accept the corrected statements, see exactly what the original data do and do not imply, and re-evaluate the framework's remaining claims on their merits. The preprint under revision is remediated only in the two flagged passages; no other section is altered.

The contributions of this erratum are:

- A formal analysis of the Section 1.1.1 claim of [4], showing that under the same-language reading it is vacuously true and carries no content, and under the language-extension reading it is refuted by an explicit consistent conservative extension of a complete consistent theory; the analysis is derived in Section 4.1 and reported in Section 5.1.
- A derivation, with all arithmetic shown, of what the flatness bound $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$ actually implies: the density-parameter window $0.998 \leq \Omega_{\text{total}} \leq 1.002$, the Newtonian energy ratio $|E|/K < 0.002$, and the curvature radius $R_{\text{curv}} \approx 95.8$ Gpc under a stated assumption for $H_0$; derived in Section 4.2 and reported in Section 5.2.
- Corrected replacement text for both errata, suitable for direct substitution into Sections 1.1.1, 1.2.1 and 1.3.3 of [4], given in Section 5.3.
- An assessment of what the corrections leave intact, including the falsifiability conditions under which the corrections themselves would fail, in Sections 6 and 7.

## 2. Background and Related Work

The erratum discipline that this paper practices has well-established precedent in physics publishing, and the six works available for citation here supply both the corrective models and the substantive material against which the two errata are checked. Because the citation base for this erratum is limited to six works, the literature treatment is correspondingly focused; this limitation is stated explicitly in Section 6.

The document under correction is the preprint itself. Its Section 1.0 defines recursive self-consistency as a fixed point $x = \mathcal{R}(x)$ of a contraction mapping on a complete metric space, with the universal wave function $|\Psi\rangle$ as the fixed point and the Wheeler-DeWitt equation $\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle = 0$ as the timeless consistency condition [4]. Its Section 1.1.1 contains the first erratum target: a sentence attributing to Gödel's completeness theorem the claim that if physics is a complete and consistent formal theory, no proper extension can add explanatory power without introducing an inconsistency [4]. Its Section 1.2.1 contains the second: the claim that Planck-class cosmic microwave background measurements showing $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$ confirm zero total energy to within experimental error, a claim repeated in its Section 1.3.3 as the framework's principal empirical validation [4]. Both sentences are the objects of correction here.

On the logical side, the Universal Computational Topos paper states the correct theorem in the neighborhood of the error: it argues that sufficiently powerful formal systems cannot simultaneously achieve completeness and consistency, grounding this in the Gödelian incompleteness phenomena and in the observation that self-referential formal systems contain statements that are true but unprovable within the system itself [3]. This is the incompleteness territory, not the completeness theorem of 1930, and the distinction is exactly the one the erratum in Section 4.1 makes: the preprint [4] cites the completeness result for a claim that, even if true, would have to come from the incompleteness results — and even the incompleteness results do not prohibit proper consistent extensions, as the counterexample of Section 4.1 shows. The topos paper's own framing, in which hierarchical computational theories and meta-computational approaches are unified by distinguishing Object, Model, and Meta computation [3], is also the natural home for the same-language versus expanded-language distinction that drives the counterexample: an extension that adds a new predicate operates at a different computational level than the base theory, and the preprint's argument conflates the two levels.

The Calculus of Re-Entrant Distinctions treatise supplies the methodological norm that this erratum applies to [6]: it attaches explicit falsifiability conditions to its own central claims, stating, for example, that the claim that a type theory's syntax forces the constants $e$ and $\pi$ is formally falsifiable, failing if the formal system cannot derive $e^{i\pi} = -1$ without importing the real numbers and the exponential function as external axioms [6]. The treatise also develops the temporal reading of the re-entrant form, in which an oscillating self-reference is read as a clock rather than as a paradox [6], which is the reading of self-reference closest in spirit to the strange-loop construction of [4]; the erratum takes no position on that construction, but it adopts the treatise's discipline of stating, for each corrected claim, the condition under which the correction itself would be refuted.

The strongest precedent for the erratum's posture toward negative results is the Prime Valuation quantum error correction follow-on. That paper applies its anchor's own falsifiability condition to the reading of $[[n,k,d]]$ code parameters as $p$-adic valuations and reports, in its own words, that "the most obvious extension — reading $[[n,k,d]]$ parameters as valuations — is largely pure relabeling," adding that "this is not a failure of the program; it is the falsifiability machinery doing its job" [5]. Its conclusion states the negative result plainly: the valuation-based overhead bound is strictly weaker than the quantum Singleton bound, the complexity characterization is complexity-vacuous, and the no-cloning re-expression yields no new checkable consequence [5]. This is the model for Sections 4 and 5 of the present erratum: a correction that removes an unsupported claim is a successful application of the falsifiability machinery, not an attack on the surrounding program. The same paper also notes the external precedent that the categorical content of no-cloning is established in the categorical quantum mechanics literature and the $p$-adic–code connection in the holographic tensor-network literature [5], a reminder that claims should be attributed to the works that establish them — precisely the norm the first erratum enforces.

On the physics side, the MSSM Feynman rules paper and its erratum illustrate both halves of the correction culture. The paper collects the complete set of Feynman rules for the $R$-parity conserving MSSM in the t'Hooft-Feynman gauge, with the most general flavour mixing, so that "the full set of rules needed to calculate any process in the frame of the MSSM" is available in one place with fixed conventions [1]. Its summary section is candid about the cost of that completeness: "The model described in the previous sections contains a large number of free parameters which considerably limit its predictive power," with constraints coming from collider bounds and from flavor-changing neutral current limits on non-diagonal soft Yukawa couplings [1]. The erratum attached to it exists to correct the published record without discarding the paper's computational value [1]. The parallel to the present work is exact: an erratum corrects specific statements; it does not re-litigate the framework. The MSSM paper's parameter-count observation also bears on the preprint's Table 1.1.1.1, which scores string theory as failing generative capacity; the honest comparison, as [1] shows, is that parameter-rich frameworks declare their free parameters, and a monistic framework should be held to the same standard of declaring what it assumes.

Finally, the Zagreb index paper supplies the standard of precision against which the two corrected claims fall short. Its Theorem 1 states a sharp, checkable bound: for any tree $T$ with $n \geq 2$ pendent vertices, $M_1(T) \geq 9n - 16$ if $n$ is even, with equality for the $4$-tree, and $M_1(T) \geq 9n - 15$ if $n$ is odd [2]. Its Theorem 3 gives the tight bound $M_2(T) \geq 11n - 27$ for trees with $n \geq 8$ pendent vertices and characterizes the minimizing trees [2]. The paper's introduction frames the general problem as finding lower and upper bounds over a set of graphs and characterizing the extremal graphs, noting that extremal graphs often sit on the boundary of the admissible set [2]. This is what a load-bearing quantitative claim looks like: a bound with its equality case named, so that any reader can check it. The erratum's demand on [4] is the same one [2] satisfies: state the bound, state what it bounds, and state the case where it is tight. The flatness claim of [4] fails this test because it names neither the quantity bounded (a curvature ratio, not a total energy) nor the equality structure of the inference.

## 3. Methods

This erratum is a textual and logical remediation of a published preprint; it performs no experiment, measurement, observation, or survey. Its method has three parts.

**Part 1: Claim extraction.** We label the two corrections erratum 0, targeting Section 1.1.1 of [4], and erratum 1, targeting Section 1.2.1 of [4] with a repeat in Section 1.3.3; these labels are the paper's own. Both target sentences are identified from the preprint's own text as given in [4]. No other passage of [4] is modified.

**Part 2: Logical analysis of the Gödel claim.** The erratum's Section 4.1 analyzes the Section 1.1.1 sentence of [4] by formalizing it. Let $T$ be a consistent, complete first-order theory in language $L$: for every $L$-sentence $\varphi$, either $T \vdash \varphi$ or $T \vdash \neg\varphi$. The erratum distinguishes two readings of "proper extension": (i) an extension in the same language $L$, and (ii) an extension to an expanded language $L' = L \cup \{P\}$ with $P$ a new predicate symbol. For each reading the erratum derives the truth value of the claim "no proper extension can add explanatory power without introducing an inconsistency," where "adds explanatory power" is operationalized as "proves statements not provable in $T$" — the weakest reading that makes the claim non-trivial. Gödel's completeness theorem itself — every first-order valid formula is provable, equivalently every consistent first-order theory has a model — is used only in its model-existence form, to establish consistency of the constructed extension; no attribution beyond that form is made.

**Part 3: Cosmological arithmetic.** The erratum's Section 4.2 takes the flatness bound $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$ as stated in [4] and derives, with every arithmetic step shown: (a) the induced window on the total density parameter $\Omega_{\text{total}} = 1 - \Omega_k$; (b) the Newtonian-limit relation between $\Omega_k$ and the ratio of total to kinetic energy per unit mass in a Hubble flow, $E = \tfrac{1}{2} m \chi^2 a^2 H^2 (1 - \Omega)$, which gives $|E|/K = |\Omega_k|$; and (c) the present-day curvature radius $R_{\text{curv}} = c/(H_0\sqrt{|\Omega_k|})$ under the explicit assumption $H_0 = 70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and $c = 2.9979 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. The value $H_0 = 70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ is the paper's own assumption, adopted because the preprint [4] states the flatness bound without the Hubble constant needed to convert it to a length; the resulting curvature radius is labeled a projection and carries the assumption's uncertainty. The erratum then states, as a matter of general relativity and not of new derivation, that the total energy of a closed universe is not a gauge-invariant, globally defined quantity without additional structure such as a boundary or asymptotic region, so that no value of $\Omega_k$ can "confirm" a value of that quantity.

**Part 4: Replacement text.** Section 5.3 writes corrected sentences for the three flagged locations, constrained to say only what Sections 4.1 and 4.2 establish.

## 4. Analysis

### 4.1 The Gödel completeness attribution

**Input.** The sentence under correction, from Section 1.1.1 of [4], which we paraphrase as: Gödel's completeness theorem demonstrates that if physics is a complete and consistent formal theory, no proper extension can add explanatory power without introducing an inconsistency.

**Step 1: What the completeness theorem states.** Gödel's completeness theorem states: every first-order formula valid in all models is provable in a complete proof system; equivalently, every consistent first-order theory has a model. It is a theorem connecting semantic validity to syntactic provability for first-order logic. It contains no clause about complete theories, proper extensions, or explanatory power. The preprint's own neighboring literature states the relevant phenomenon correctly: any sufficiently powerful formal system cannot simultaneously achieve completeness and consistency, with true-but-unprovable statements arising in self-referential systems [3]. That is the incompleteness territory, and the distinction between the completeness theorem and the incompleteness results is the one the erratum in Section 4.1 makes: the preprint [4] cites the completeness result for a claim that, even if true, would have to come from the incompleteness results — and even the incompleteness results do not prohibit proper consistent extensions, as the counterexample of Section 4.1 shows. The attribution in [4] is therefore a misattribution regardless of whether the claimed proposition is true.

**Step 2: Is the claimed proposition true under the same-language reading?** Let $T$ be complete and consistent in language $L$. Let $T' \supsetneq T$ be a theory in the same language $L$. Properness means there is an $L$-sentence $\varphi$ with $T' \vdash \varphi$ and $T \nvdash \varphi$. By completeness of $T$, $T \vdash \neg\varphi$. Then $T' \vdash \varphi$ and $T' \vdash \neg\varphi$ (since $T' \supseteq T$), so $T'$ is inconsistent. Hence under the same-language reading the claim is **true but vacuous**: a complete theory already decides every sentence of its language, so no same-language extension can add an undecidable statement — there is nothing for the extension to add. The claim, in this reading, has the same content as the definition of completeness and borrows no force from Gödel's theorem.

**Step 3: Is the claimed proposition true under the language-extension reading?** Let $L' = L \cup \{P\}$ with $P$ a unary predicate symbol not in $L$. Define

$$T' \;=\; T \;\cup\; \{\,\exists x\, P(x)\,\}.$$

$T'$ is a proper extension: it proves $\exists x\, P(x)$, an $L'$-sentence not expressible in $L$, so $T \nvdash \exists x\, P(x)$ simply because the sentence is not in $L$'s vocabulary. $T'$ is consistent: by the model-existence form of the completeness theorem it suffices to exhibit a model. Take any model $\mathcal{M} \models T$ (which exists because $T$ is consistent), expand it to $\mathcal{M}'$ by interpreting $P$ as a non-empty subset of the domain, e.g. $P^{\mathcal{M}'} = \{m\}$ for a single element $m$. Then $\mathcal{M}' \models T$ (the $T$-axioms contain no occurrence of $P$) and $\mathcal{M}' \models \exists x\, P(x)$, so $\mathcal{M}' \models T'$ and $T'$ is consistent. $T'$ adds expressive and explanatory power in the operationalized sense: it proves $L'$-statements about $P$ that $T$ cannot even state. Hence under the language-extension reading the claim of [4] is **false**, by explicit counterexample.

**Step 4: Verdict on the sentence.** The sentence in Section 1.1.1 of [4] is either vacuous (same-language reading) or false (language-extension reading), and in neither case is it a consequence of Gödel's completeness theorem. The corrected statement must therefore (a) attribute to the completeness theorem only what it states, and (b) if the preprint wishes to argue against external foundations, rest that argument on the vacuity of same-language extensions and on an explicitly stated position about language extensions, not on a theorem.

### 4.2 The flatness-to-zero-energy inference

**Input.** The sentence under correction, from Section 1.2.1 of [4] and repeated in Section 1.3.3, which we paraphrase as: measurements of the cosmic microwave background show the universe to be spatially flat with $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$, confirming zero total energy to within experimental error.

**Step 1: What the flatness bound constrains.** In the Friedmann-Lemaître framework, the density parameters satisfy the constraint

$$\Omega_m + \Omega_r + \Omega_\Lambda + \Omega_k = 1,$$

so the total density parameter is $\Omega_{\text{total}} = 1 - \Omega_k$. The bound $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$ therefore gives

$$-0.002 < \Omega_k < 0.002 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1 - 0.002 < \Omega_{\text{total}} < 1 + 0.002,$$

that is,

$$0.998 \leq \Omega_{\text{total}} \leq 1.002.$$

This is a statement about the spatial curvature of the universe: it says the universe is flat to within $0.2\%$ in the density budget. It says nothing directly about any total energy.

**Step 2: The Newtonian energy ratio.** In the Newtonian limit of the Friedmann dynamics, a comoving shell of proper mass $m$ at scale-factor distance $\chi a$ in a Hubble flow has kinetic energy $K = \tfrac{1}{2} m \chi^2 a^2 H^2$ and total Newtonian energy

$$E = \tfrac{1}{2} m \chi^2 a^2 H^2 (1 - \Omega),$$

where $\Omega = \Omega_{\text{total}}$. Dividing,

$$\frac{|E|}{K} = |1 - \Omega| = |\Omega_k|.$$

With $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$,

$$\frac{|E|}{K} < 0.002.$$

So the flatness bound constrains a dimensionless ratio of total to kinetic Newtonian energy per comoving shell. It does not fix an absolute value of $E$; indeed $E = 0$ corresponds exactly to $\Omega = 1$, and the bound permits $0.998 \leq \Omega \leq 1.002$, i.e. either sign of $E$ within the window. The preprint's phrase "zero total energy confirmed to within experimental error" conflates this ratio bound with a measurement of $E$ itself.

**Step 3: General-relativistic status of "total energy of the universe."** In general relativity without additional structure — a boundary, an asymptotically flat region, or a preferred slicing — the total energy content of a closed universe is not a gauge-invariant, globally defined quantity. The Hamiltonian constraint of the Wheeler-DeWitt formulation, $\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle = 0$ [4], is a constraint on the state, not a measurement report about a quantity $E$ with a well-defined value and error bar. Consequently no value of $\Omega_k$, however small, can "confirm" a numerical value of a quantity that the theory does not globally define. We take this as the governing convention for the corrected text: the flatness bound supports a statement about spatial curvature and about the Newtonian energy ratio, and nothing stronger.

**Step 4: Curvature radius (projection).** To give the bound a geometric reading, we convert it to a curvature radius. The curvature radius $R_{\text{curv}}$ of a Friedmann universe relates to the curvature density parameter at the present epoch $a = 1$ by

$$R_{\text{curv}} = \frac{c}{H_0 \sqrt{|\Omega_k|}}.$$

We assume $H_0 = 70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and $c = 2.9979 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$; both are stated assumptions, since [4] gives the flatness bound without a Hubble constant.

Arithmetic, step by step:

1. Convert $H_0$ to SI. With $1\ \mathrm{Mpc} = 3.086 \times 10^{19}\ \mathrm{km}$,

$$H_0 = \frac{70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}}{3.086 \times 10^{19}\ \mathrm{km}} = 2.268 \times 10^{-18}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}.$$

2. Square-root the bound:

$$\sqrt{0.002} = 0.044721.$$

3. Form the denominator:

$$H_0 \sqrt{|\Omega_k|} = (2.268 \times 10^{-18}) \times 0.044721 = 1.014 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}.$$

4. Divide:

$$R_{\text{curv}} > \frac{2.9979 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}}{1.014 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}} = 2.956 \times 10^{24}\ \mathrm{km}.$$

5. Convert units. With $1\ \mathrm{Gpc} = 3.086 \times 10^{19}\ \mathrm{km}$,

$$R_{\text{curv}} > \frac{2.956 \times 10^{24}}{3.086 \times 10^{19}}\ \mathrm{Gpc} = 9.58 \times 10^{1}\ \mathrm{Gpc} = 95.8\ \mathrm{Gpc}.$$

6. In light-years, with $1\ \mathrm{ly} = 9.461 \times 10^{12}\ \mathrm{km}$,

$$R_{\text{curv}} > \frac{2.956 \times 10^{24}}{9.461 \times 10^{12}}\ \mathrm{ly} = 3.12 \times 10^{11}\ \mathrm{ly}.$$

Because this is a strict inequality driven by an upper bound on $|\Omega_k|$, the correct statement is a lower bound: $R_{\text{curv}} > 95.8$ Gpc under the assumed $H_0$. We assume the radius of the observable universe is of order $4.4 \times 10^{10}$ ly $\approx 13.5$ Gpc; this is a stated assumption, since [4] gives no such figure. Under that assumption, the ratio is $R_{\text{curv}}/R_{\text{obs}} > 3.12 \times 10^{11} / 4.4 \times 10^{10} \approx 7.09$, so the bound places the curvature radius at more than seven times the observable radius — a projection, conditional on the assumed $H_0$ and the assumed observable radius; a 5% change in $H_0$ rescales $R_{\text{curv}}$ by the inverse factor.

**Step 5: What survives.** The corrected claim is: the CMB flatness bound constrains spatial curvature to $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$, equivalently $0.998 \leq \Omega_{\text{total}} \leq 1.002$ and $|E|/K < 0.002$ in the Newtonian limit, with curvature radius exceeding $95.8$ Gpc under the stated $H_0$ assumption. It is consistent with — but does not confirm — any particular value of a globally defined total energy, a quantity general relativity does not supply for a closed universe.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Logical erratum (Section 1.1.1 of [4])

The attributed claim is not a consequence of Gödel's completeness theorem. Under the same-language reading it is vacuously true (a complete theory decides every sentence of its language, so a proper same-language extension is necessarily inconsistent — this is the definition of completeness, not the theorem). Under the language-extension reading it is false, refuted by the explicit consistent extension $T' = T \cup \{\exists x\, P(x)\}$ of Section 4.1, whose consistency follows from the model-existence form of the completeness theorem itself. The erratum verdict: misattribution, with the proposition itself either vacuous or false depending on reading.

### 5.2 Cosmological erratum (Sections 1.2.1 and 1.3.3 of [4])

The flatness bound $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$ implies, with all arithmetic shown in Section 4.2:

- Density-parameter window: $0.998 \leq \Omega_{\text{total}} \leq 1.002$.
- Newtonian energy ratio: $|E|/K = |\Omega_k| < 0.002$.
- Curvature radius (projection, assuming $H_0 = 70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$): $R_{\text{curv}} > 2.956 \times 10^{24}\ \mathrm{km} = 95.8\ \mathrm{Gpc} = 3.12 \times 10^{11}\ \mathrm{ly}$.

None of these is a measurement of a globally defined total energy. The phrase "zero total energy confirmed to within experimental error" is not supported; the supported statement is a curvature constraint.

### 5.3 Corrected replacement text

For Section 1.1.1 of [4], the sentence "Gödel's completeness theorem demonstrates that if physics is a complete and consistent formal theory, no proper extension can add explanatory power without introducing an inconsistency" is replaced by the following, in which the completeness theorem is cited by its content and no publication date is asserted:

> "Gödel's completeness theorem states that every first-order formula valid in all models is provable, and equivalently that every consistent first-order theory has a model; it makes no claim about extensions of complete theories. If a theory $T$ is complete and consistent in a language $L$, then any proper extension of $T$ within the same language $L$ is necessarily inconsistent, since $T$ already decides every $L$-sentence; this is a consequence of the definition of completeness, not of the completeness theorem. Extensions that expand the language — for example, adding a new predicate symbol $P$ and the axiom $\exists x\, P(x)$ — can be consistent and can prove statements the base theory cannot even express, so the prohibition of consistent, explanatory proper extensions holds only for same-language extensions and must be argued as a position about language choice, not cited as a theorem."

For Section 1.2.1 of [4], the sentence "measurements of the cosmic microwave background show the universe to be spatially flat with $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$, confirming zero total energy to within experimental error" is replaced by:

> "Measurements of the cosmic microwave background constrain the spatial curvature of the universe to $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$, equivalently $0.998 \leq \Omega_{\text{total}} \leq 1.002$. In the Newtonian limit of the Friedmann dynamics this bound constrains the dimensionless ratio of total to kinetic energy of a comoving shell to $|E|/K < 0.002$; it is consistent with, but does not fix, $E = 0$, and general relativity supplies no globally defined total energy for a closed universe against which such a confirmation could be registered."

For Section 1.3.3 of [4], the corresponding sentence is replaced by:

> "The framework's Hamiltonian constraint, $\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle = 0$, is consistent with the observed spatial flatness of the universe, $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$; the flatness bound constrains spatial curvature and the Newtonian energy ratio $|E|/K < 0.002$, and does not by itself constitute an empirical confirmation of a globally defined zero total energy, a quantity that general relativity does not define for a closed universe."

## 6. Discussion

The two corrections are narrow, and their narrowness is the point: an erratum that silently rewrote more than the flagged sentences would itself introduce unverifiable content into the record. This section states what the corrections leave intact, the conditions under which the corrections themselves would fail, and the limitations under which the derivations of Section 4 hold.

**What survives.** The preprint's mathematical machinery — the fixed-point formulation $x = \mathcal{R}(x)$ on a complete metric space, the Wheeler-DeWitt constraint $\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle = 0$ as a timeless consistency condition, the circle-Gaussian co-definition, the theta-function partition function, and the six-step generative sequence — is untouched by both errata. What changes is the external support claimed for two of its arguments. The rejection of external foundations in Section 1.1.1 of [4] loses its theorem-shaped backing and must rest on the vacuity argument for same-language extensions plus an explicit position on language extensions; the empirical-validation entry of Table 1.1.1.1 loses the phrase "Matches cosmic flatness" as evidence for zero total energy, though the flatness bound itself, $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$, remains a correct observational constraint that the framework's $\Omega_{\text{total}} = 1$ prediction is consistent with. Consistency with a bound is weaker than confirmation of a value, and the corrected text preserves exactly that weaker relation.

**Falsifiability of the corrections.** Following the discipline exemplified by the Calculus of Re-Entrant Distinctions, which attaches an explicit falsifiability condition to its central claim — the derivation of $e^{i\pi} = -1$ without importing the real numbers and the exponential function as external axioms [6] — and by the Prime Valuation follow-on, which reports that its own most obvious extension "is largely pure relabeling" and treats the disconfirmation as "the falsifiability machinery doing its job" [5], this erratum states the conditions under which it would fail:

- The Gödel correction would fail if a reading of Gödel's completeness theorem could be produced under which the theorem entails the Section 1.1.1 sentence of [4]; the theorem's standard statement — validity implies provability for first-order formulas, with model existence for consistent theories — contains no clause about complete theories or extensions, so the correction would require a non-standard formulation of the theorem stated in a citable source.
- The same-language vacuity verdict would fail if a proper same-language extension of a complete consistent theory could be exhibited that proves an $L$-sentence $T$ does not prove without becoming inconsistent; Section 4.1 Step 2 shows this is impossible by the definition of completeness, so the failure condition is a counterexample to a two-line argument, checkable by any reader.
- The language-extension counterexample would fail if the extension $T' = T \cup \{\exists x\, P(x)\}$ were inconsistent for some complete consistent $T$; the model construction of Section 4.1 Step 3 rules this out, and a failure would mean the model-existence form of the completeness theorem is false.
- The cosmological correction would fail if general relativity supplied a gauge-invariant, globally defined total energy for a closed universe without additional structure such as a boundary or asymptotic region, and if the flatness bound could then be shown to measure that quantity to the stated precision; the erratum asserts the non-existence of such a quantity as a matter of the standard theory, and a published construction of a well-defined closed-universe total energy, together with a derivation linking $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$ to its measured value, would overturn the correction.
- The curvature-radius projection would fail numerically if the assumed $H_0 = 70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ were replaced by a materially different value; the projection scales as $R_{\text{curv}} \propto H_0^{-1}$, so a 5% change in $H_0$ rescales the bound by the inverse factor, and the qualitative conclusion — a curvature radius many times the observable radius — survives any plausible $H_0$.

**Limitations.** The corrections rest on the following assumptions, with an indication of sensitivity for each:

- The operationalization "adds explanatory power" as "proves statements not provable in $T$" is the weakest reading that makes the Section 1.1.1 claim non-trivial; a stronger reading (for example, adding predictive content about new phenomena) would make the language-extension counterexample even more decisive, since $T'$ proves new $L'$-statements, so the verdict is robust to strengthening the reading.
- The verdict "vacuous or false" depends on the two readings exhausting the reasonable interpretations of "proper extension"; a third reading would need to be supplied by a defender of [4], and the erratum's analysis covers the two natural ones.
- The Newtonian energy-ratio derivation uses the Newtonian limit of the Friedmann dynamics, valid for $|E|/K \ll 1$ and for scales within the Hubble flow; at $|E|/K < 0.002$ the limit is self-consistently satisfied, so the sensitivity is negligible within its domain.
- The curvature-radius figure is a projection conditional on the assumed $H_0 = 70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$; the preprint [4] states the flatness bound without a Hubble constant, so no source-derived value exists, and the projection inherits whatever uncertainty the assumption carries.
- The claim that total energy is not globally defined for a closed universe is taken as a statement of the standard theory rather than a new derivation; the erratum does not prove it, it applies it, and its correctness is a property of general relativity, not of this paper's arithmetic.
- The citation base for the erratum's Background is limited to six works, so the literature treatment is focused rather than comprehensive; the two corrections, however, are checked against the quoted text of [4] itself and against standard statements of the completeness theorem and the Friedmann framework, not against the breadth of the citation base.

**Failure modes of the erratum itself.** Two are worth naming. First, an erratum can overcorrect: if the preprint's author intended "proper extension" to mean only same-language extension, then the Gödel sentence is merely misattributed rather than false, and the corrected text of Section 5.3 already covers this case by stating the vacuity result explicitly. Second, an erratum can undercorrect: the flatness claim also appears in the preprint's abstract-level framing of "empirical validation through cosmic flatness measurements," and this erratum corrects only the three flagged sentences (Sections 1.1.1, 1.2.1 and 1.3.3 of [4]); a full remediation would audit every occurrence of "zero total energy" in the preprint, which is beyond the scope of the two registered errata.

## 8. Conclusion

Two load-bearing sentences of the preprint [4] do not follow from the authorities they invoke. The Section 1.1.1 sentence attributes to Gödel's completeness theorem a prohibition of consistent, explanatory proper extensions; the theorem, which connects first-order validity to provability and guarantees models for consistent theories, supports no such prohibition, and the proposition itself is vacuously true under the same-language reading and false under the language-extension reading, refuted by the explicit consistent extension $T' = T \cup \{\exists x\, P(x)\}$. The Section 1.2.1 and 1.3.3 sentences convert the flatness bound $|\Omega_k| < 0.002$ into a purported confirmation of zero total energy; the bound actually constrains the density parameter to $0.998 \leq \Omega_{\text{total}} \leq 1.002$ and the Newtonian energy ratio to $|E|/K < 0.002$, and general relativity defines no globally closed-universe total energy for the bound to confirm. Under an assumed $H_0 = 70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, the bound implies a curvature radius exceeding $95.8$ Gpc, about $3.12 \times 10^{11}$ ly — a projection, not a measurement. Corrected replacement text for all three locations is supplied in Section 5.3. The corrections remove two unsupported claims while leaving the framework's mathematical machinery intact, and they follow the erratum discipline that the literature itself models: the MSSM erratum corrects the record without discarding the paper's computational value [1], the Zagreb-index paper shows what a checkable bound with its equality case looks like [2], the Universal Computational Topos paper states the incompleteness phenomenon the preprint should have cited [3], the Prime Valuation follow-on demonstrates that a disconfirmed extension is the falsifiability machinery succeeding [5], and the Calculus of Re-Entrant Distinctions supplies the norm of stating, for each claim, the condition under which it fails [6]. The framework of [4] now stands where every framework should: on its derivations, not on borrowed theorems.

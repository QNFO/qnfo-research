# Number Systems as Stratified Distinction Operations: An Enclosure Hierarchy over the Mark

## Abstract

The conventional tower of number systems—$\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}$—augmented by the $p$-adic fields $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ and the adele ring $\mathbb{A}$ is usually presented as a sequence of completions without a unifying generative principle. Practitioners in physics, logic, and computer science lack a quantitative criterion for choosing among these systems, which leads to ad-hoc model selection and undocumented information loss when finite-precision arithmetic is projected onto the continuum. This paper defines a family of enclosure operators $E_{i}$ that generate each stratum from a primitive mark, an adjacent-stratum loss functional $\Lambda_{i\to i+1}=\log_{2}N_{i}-\log_{2}N_{i+1}$ over distinguishable-state counts $N_{i}$, and a separate cross-stratum state-count comparison $\Delta_{i\to j}$ for non-adjacent pairs. For the tree-to-continuum (Monna) comparison, which is a cross-stratum diagnostic and not an instance of $\Lambda$, we compute $\Delta_{6\to4}=7$ bits at depth $n=10$, base $p=2$, real precision $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$; we prove the exact linear law $\Delta_{6\to4}(n)=n-3$ at fixed $\varepsilon$ and identify the loss-free matching depth $n=3$. Finite simultaneous-valuation products over the primes $\{2,3,5\}$ yield $30$ states at depth 1 and $900$ at depth 2. State-count arithmetic is verified only for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 at finite depth. All numbers are derived by hand with every arithmetic step shown; no simulation or external measurement is used. The result converts a philosophical thesis into falsifiable, countable claims and specifies the minimal computational experiment that would test them.

## 1. Introduction

Anyone who teaches, formalizes, or computes with numbers inherits a fixed tower: $\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}$, with $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ and $\mathbb{A}$ attached to $\mathbb{Q}$ as a parallel branch. The cost of this presentation is concrete. A physicist choosing a number system for a model has no principled way to decide whether the continuum is forced or merely conventional; the adelic-physics work states the point directly: physics today operates exclusively at the Archimedean place, every measurement produces a rational number to finite precision, and Ostrowski's theorem—a proven result—makes the real line one completion among many, so the choice of $\mathbb{R}$ as the base field is an assumption rather than a derivation [11]. A logician studying constructive foundations has no unified account of why the historical sequence unfolded in this order. A computer scientist working with finite-precision arithmetic has no theory of what information is destroyed when a tree-structured limit is flattened onto a line. If the progression $\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}\to\mathbb{A}$ could be derived from increasingly refined acts of distinction, each decision would acquire a measurable criterion: the choice of number system would become a choice of operator, and the cost of each choice would be quantifiable in bits.

The stratigraphy thesis states that the history of number systems is a history of expanding distinction operations—acts of drawing boundaries that create new enclosures, each yielding a richer number system but also embedding a new hidden assumption—and it names the guiding banner "not wrong, but incomplete": each framework is locally validated yet globally partial, and the generative question is what larger distinction operation would heal the incompleteness while preserving local validity [13]. The tree-to-continuum work supplies the formal counterpart of the most delicate enclosure in the sequence: it develops the conjecture that the continuum is constructed rather than primitive, with natural numbers, rationals, and computable reals arising from a discrete tree and the full continuum generated by a lossy Monna-map projection, and it quantifies the loss by showing that the computable shadows form a countable, measure-zero subset of $[0,1]$ [12]. Neither work supplies a layer of state counts and bit budgets for the whole tower; this paper adds one, and corrects the quantitative claims of the published version [10].

Two scope restrictions are stated here and enforced throughout. First, the scope of verified state-count arithmetic is stated precisely: it is verified only for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 at finite depth, and only the state-count formulas for those strata are used in finite arithmetic (Sections 3.2 and 6). Second, the classification of the completions of $\mathbb{Q}$—the result that underwrites falsifier F3 and justifies the operator list of Section 3.2—is attributed to Ostrowski's theorem as stated in the adelic-physics work: every non-trivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent either to the standard Archimedean absolute value, whose completion is $\mathbb{R}$, or to a $p$-adic absolute value for some prime $p$, whose completion is $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ [11].

We posit a primitive mark $M$ and a family of enclosure operators $E_{i}$, each a closure-type operator whose application to the previous stratum yields the next. For each stratum we state the minimal algebraic structure generated, the canonical map to the following stratum, and a distinguishable-state count where a finite count exists. The counts support an adjacent-stratum loss functional and a separate cross-stratum comparison quantity, which we compute in full arithmetic for the tree-to-continuum case. Four falsifiability conditions are stated so that a single counterexample kills the corresponding claim.

**Contributions.**
1. Explicit state-count derivations for the tally, real-enclosure, and $p$-adic strata at finite depth, with every arithmetic step shown, restricted to the verified strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 (Section 4, Derivations 1–3).
2. The Monna cross-stratum state-count comparison, presented explicitly as a diagnostic quantity distinct from the adjacent-stratum loss functional: $\Delta_{6\to4}=7$ bits at $(p=2,n=10,\varepsilon=2^{-3})$, the exact linear law $\Delta_{6\to4}(n)=n-3$ at fixed precision, the vanishing of the comparison at matched depth $n=3$, and the reclassification of this quantity as cross-stratum rather than adjacent-stratum loss (Section 4, Derivations 4–5; Results R1–R2).
3. Finite simultaneous-valuation product counts of $30$ and $900$ over the primes $\{2,3,5\}$ at depths 1 and 2 (Section 4, Derivation 6).
4. The non-dyadic no-matching result: for $p=3$ and $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$ no integer depth yields zero comparison loss because the required depth $n=3/\log_{2}3\approx1.893$ is not an integer (Section 4, Derivation 7).
5. Four falsifiability conditions F1–F4 for the stratification, the loss functional, the operator list, and the adequacy of $\Lambda$, with the operator-list condition underwritten by the Ostrowski classification as stated in [11] (Section 3.4).

The stratified-enclosure reading of number systems may also bear on problems outside its home domain: the stratified $\beta$-number literature shows that scalar invariants over stratified structures can require stratified refinements to become sharp [4], and the measurement-stratigraphy work maps the same completion-and-lossy-projection pattern across physics, computer science, and information theory [13]; both connections are drawn only as far as Sections 2 and 6 support them.

## 2. Background and Related Work

Thirteen works are discussed here, each through a specific result its text states.

The polynomial-systems paper solves linear systems with coefficients in multivariate polynomial rings, systems that arise in the computation of parametric Bernstein–Sato polynomials associated with hypergeometric ideals in the Weyl algebra [1]. Its preliminary section records the encoding this paper's tally stratum generalizes: the Stirling numbers of the first kind appear as the coefficients of the monic polynomial $b_{\Delta}(s)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(s-\alpha_{i})$ when the root set is $\Delta=\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}$, so a finite slice of the tally structure generates a full polynomial algebra through the elementary symmetric functions [1]. The relevance is direct: the simplest stratum of Section 3, the tally, already carries enough structure to determine objects of degree $d$ from $d$ marked points, which is the smallest instance of an enclosure generating more than it explicitly contains.

The Tate-conjectures note proves Tate's two conjectures for algebraic cycles of arbitrary codimension on certain products of elliptic curves and abelian surfaces over number fields [2]. Its proof of Tate I shows that the cycle class map ${\mathrm{Ch}}^{r}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))^{\Gamma_{F}}$ is surjective in this setting, using the chain that the Hodge conjecture and the Mumford–Tate conjecture for $X$ over $F$ imply Tate I [2]. This matters here because the entire proof operates over $\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}$ and $\ell$-adic cohomology: the $p$-adic stratum of Section 3.2 is not an exotic appendix to the tower but the field over which some of arithmetic geometry's strongest structural statements are proved.

The harmonic-congruence paper proves a congruence for harmonic numbers conjectured by Z.-W. Sun, concerning arithmetic modulo a prime power [3]. This is the correct citation for the harmonic-number congruence: the result shows that arithmetic modulo a prime power—a finite $p$-adic stratum of exactly the kind counted in Derivation 3—resolves structure that the congruence modulo $p$ alone leaves invisible.

The stratified-$\beta$-numbers paper introduces a modified version of Jones's $\beta$-numbers for Carnot groups and proves a result concerning traveling salesman type inequalities in that setting [4]. The $\beta$-number $\beta_{E}(x,r)=\inf_{L}\sup_{z\in E\cap B(x,r)}d(z,L)/r$ is a scalar that measures how close a set lies to an affine line, and the paper shows a scalar of the wrong stratification can be non-sharp [4]. This is the exact shape of the adequacy concern that falsifier F4 raises for the loss functional $\Lambda$: a scalar invariant over a stratified structure may need a stratified refinement before it separates what it should separate.

The binomial-congruence conjectures paper poses conjectures on congruences involving binomial coefficients and Apéry-like numbers [5], and a later paper extends the programme to congruences modulo higher powers of $p$ [8]. Both papers live entirely inside the rings $\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}$ whose state counts Derivation 3 computes: they are evidence that the finite $p$-adic strata are a generative site in current number theory, not a historical curiosity, and that their internal case structure (residues of $p$, quadratic representations) is exactly the kind of structure a state count alone does not capture.

The Caccetta–Häggkvist expository paper surveys the conjecture on directed cycles in graphs and notes proofs for Cayley and vertex-transitive graphs using additive number theory [6]. This bears on the state-count programme of Section 4: when a stratum carries an operation, the distinguishable states generated from a subset under iteration are a product-set growth problem, and Kemperman's bound is the classical control on how such counts can fall short of naive multiplication.

The noncanonical-number-systems paper investigates finite expansions of integers in nonstandard digit sets and proves the existence of such digit sets for many bases [7]. This is the sharpest published instance of the non-canonical-realization failure mode that Section 6 names: the tally-positional stratum admits multiple inequivalent enclosures of the same integers, so an operator does not determine a unique concrete number system, and a state count for the operator need not describe every realization.

The super-powers paper studies sequences of the form $s_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=0}^{\ell}x_{i,j}^{n^{j}}$ and proves results about the number of distinct prime factors $\omega(s_{n})$ [9]. Its notation section fixes the $p$-adic valuation $\upsilon_{p}(x)$, the exponent of the largest power of $p$ dividing $x$, as a working tool [9]. This is the correct citation for the super-powers result: it shows the divisibility stratum's valuation-theoretic quantities support nontrivial theorems about growth, which is the positive contrast to the relabeling worry that Section 6 discusses for count-based readings.

The published version of this paper reports the state-count derivations for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6, the Monna comparison value $\Lambda_{6\to4}=7$ bits at $(p=2,n=10,\varepsilon=2^{-3})$, the linear law $\Lambda_{6\to4}(n)=n-3$, and the finite simultaneous-valuation products of 30 and 900 states, and it records the limitation that state-count arithmetic is verified only for those strata at finite depth [10]. The present revision retains every correct derivation of [10], reclassifies the Monna quantity as a cross-stratum comparison rather than an instance of the adjacent-stratum loss functional (Section 3.3), and repairs two citation errors, as recorded in Sections 2 and 6.

The adelic-physics work contributes three points used directly here. It states Ostrowski's theorem in the form this paper relies on: every non-trivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to either the standard Archimedean absolute value, whose completion is $\mathbb{R}$, or a $p$-adic absolute value for some prime $p$, whose completion is $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$; the theorem is proven, and it anchors the claim that $\mathbb{R}$ is a choice of completion rather than a necessity [11]. It argues that no experimental measurement distinguishes a real number from a rational approximation to it, since every measurement produces a rational number to finite precision and every computer simulation operates on finite-precision rationals [11]. And it draws the consequence that if $\mathbb{Q}$ is the physical base field, the Archimedean completion and every $p$-adic completion are equally legitimate completions of the physical number system [11]. The first point underwrites F3 and the operator list of Section 3.2; the second supplies the finite-precision reading on which the state counts of Section 4 rest; the third motivates treating the $p$-adic and adelic strata as coequal branches rather than appendages.

The tree-to-continuum work supplies the formal counterpart of the most delicate enclosure in the sequence, and three of its results are used here. It develops the conjecture that the continuum is constructed rather than primitive, with natural numbers, rationals, and computable reals arising from a discrete tree and the full continuum generated by a lossy Monna-map projection [12]. It derives that the computable shadows form a countable, measure-zero subset of $[0,1]$ while the artifact set occupies full Lebesgue measure, and it quantifies the depth-20 case exactly: $129$ definable reduced rationals among $2^{20}=1{,}048{,}576$ binary-tree rays, a definable fraction $f_{20}=1.23024\times10^{-4}$ [12]. And it specifies a falsification protocol for its own central conjecture: enumerate the $2^{12}=4096$ binary rays of depth 12, map each through the Monna map, and check whether a benchmark computable real's 12-bit prefix appears, with failure at any depth $d\leq12$ falsifying the generation claim at that resolution [12]. The first result is the formal setting of strata 3 and 4 in Section 3.2; the second is the quantitative precedent for the state-count discipline of Section 4; the third is the model for the minimal next experiment of Section 7.

The measurement-stratigraphy work proposes that the history of number systems can be viewed as a sequence of expanding distinction operations and maps this pattern across several domains [13]. The first point is the thesis that Sections 3–5 make countable; the second locates the lossy-projection stratum in a wider pattern; the third is a published instance of the self-audit discipline that Section 6 applies to the present framework's own conjectures.

Together these thirteen works supply the algebraic seed of the tally stratum [1], the $p$-adic working environment of modern arithmetic geometry [2], [3], [5], [8], the scalar-inadequacy precedent [4], the product-set growth control [6], the non-canonical-realization failure mode [7], the valuation-theoretic positive contrast [9], the published baseline this revision corrects [10], the classification theorem and finite-precision reading [11], the tree-to-continuum formalism [12], and the thesis itself [13].

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 The mark and the distinction operation

We begin with an undifferentiated mark $M$ satisfying the idempotent law
$$MM = M,$$
the *Calling* rule of the calculus of indications, adopted as a primitive following the stratigraphy thesis that distinction operations are the generative acts of the number-system sequence [13]. A *distinction operation* $D$ partitions a structure into distinguishable classes. An *enclosure operator* $E$ is a closure-type operator on structures satisfying
$$S\subseteq E(S),\qquad E(E(S))=E(S),\qquad S\subseteq T\;\Rightarrow\;E(S)\subseteq E(T).$$

Each stratum is defined recursively as
$$S_{i}=E_{i}(S_{i-1}),\qquad i=1,\dots,7,$$
with the operators indexed by their target stratum.

### 3.2 The strata

| Stratum | Operator $E_{i}$ | Minimal algebraic structure | Distinguishable-state count $N_{i}$ (parameterised) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (the mark) | — | $\{M\}$ | $N_{0}=1$ |
| 1 (tally, $\mathbb{N}$) | tally enclosure | free monoid on one generator $\to\mathbb{N}$ with addition | $N_{1}(n)=n+1$ (depth $n$) |
| 2 (ratio, $\mathbb{Q}$) | ratio-compare | field of fractions of $\mathbb{Z}$ $\to\mathbb{Q}$ | $N_{2}$ infinite (no finite truncation) |
| 3 (convergent sequences, $\mathbb{R}_{\text{comp}}$) | Cauchy-sequence enclosure | computable reals $\mathbb{R}_{\text{comp}}$ | $N_{3}$ infinite |
| 4 (lossy tree projection, $\mathbb{R}$) | Monna-type projection $\pi_{M}$ | continuum $\mathbb{R}$ | $N_{4}(\varepsilon)=1/\varepsilon$ (real precision $\varepsilon$) |
| 5 (phase enclosure, $\mathbb{C}$) | phase enclosure | $\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}$ | $N_{5}$ infinite |
| 6 (divisibility enclosure, $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$) | $p$-adic completion | $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ | $N_{6}(n)=p^{n}$ (depth $n$) |
| 7 (simultaneous valuations, $\mathbb{A}$) | adele enclosure | $\mathbb{A}= \mathbb{R}\times\!\!\prod_{p}'\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ | $N_{7}$ infinite (restricted product) |

The verification scope is stated exactly. State-count arithmetic is verified only for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 at finite depth: $N_{0}=1$ is definitional, $N_{1}(n)=n+1$ counts the tally marks, $N_{3}$ is infinite because the computable reals are countably infinite, and $N_{6}(n)=p^{n}$ counts the residue classes of $\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}$. Only the state-count formulas for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 are used in finite arithmetic below. The formula $N_{4}(\varepsilon)=1/\varepsilon$ is a definition of the target precision, not a verified recovery claim; the recovery claims that $E_{4}$ yields $\mathbb{R}$, $E_{5}$ yields $\mathbb{C}$, and $E_{7}$ yields $\mathbb{A}$ remain conjectural. The $p$-adic stratum instantiates the tree formalism of the tree-to-continuum work, in which states are leaves of a finite rooted tree and the continuum is reached only by a lossy projection [12]. The placement of strata 4 and 6 as coequal completions of $\mathbb{Q}$ follows the Ostrowski classification as stated in the adelic-physics work [11].

### 3.3 Morphisms and the loss functionals

Between adjacent strata $S_{i}\to S_{i+1}$ we take the canonical map induced by the operator. The *adjacent-stratum information-loss functional* is
$$\Lambda_{i\to i+1}(\text{params})=\log_{2}N_{i}(\text{params})-\log_{2}N_{i+1}(\text{params}),$$
the base-2 logarithm of the ratio of distinguishable-state counts.

**Cross-stratum comparison.** For any ordered pair of strata $i,j$ with finite counts we define a separate comparison quantity
$$\Delta_{i\to j}(\text{params})=\log_{2}N_{i}(\text{params})-\log_{2}N_{j}(\text{params}),$$
which coincides numerically with the adjacent-stratum functional when $j=i+1$ but is not an instance of it. The distinction matters: $\Lambda$ is defined on the canonical map between adjacent strata, whereas $\Delta$ compares state counts across strata that are not connected by a canonical map of the hierarchy. The Monna projection $\pi_{M}$ maps a depth-$n$, base-$p$ tree (state count $N_{6}(n)=p^{n}$) onto a line at precision $\varepsilon$ (state count $N_{4}(\varepsilon)=1/\varepsilon$); source and target are strata 6 and 4, which are not adjacent. Consequently the Monna quantity is a *cross-stratum comparison*, and Derivation 4 presents it explicitly as such:
$$\Delta_{6\to4}(n,p,\varepsilon)=n\log_{2}p-\log_{2}\!\bigl(1/\varepsilon\bigr).$$
This reclassification is a correction to the published version, which presented the Monna quantity as an instance of the loss functional [10].

### 3.4 Falsifiability conditions

* **F1 (stratification).** Exhibit a number system in the historical sequence that no closure operator over the mark can generate.
* **F2 (loss functional).** Exhibit a projection whose state-count loss decreases as the source is refined at fixed target precision.
* **F3 (operator list).** Exhibit a completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ outside the list $\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Q}_{p}:p\text{ prime}\}$. By Ostrowski's theorem as stated in [11], every non-trivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent either to the standard Archimedean absolute value, whose completion is $\mathbb{R}$, or to a $p$-adic absolute value for some prime $p$, whose completion is $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$; the theorem is proven, so it asserts that no such completion exists. F3 therefore functions as a consistency check on the operator list, and the theorem underwrites both F3 and the operator list of Section 3.2.
* **F4 ($\Lambda$ adequacy).** Exhibit two intuitively different projections with identical $\Lambda$ but markedly different information content. The stratified-$\beta$-numbers work demonstrates exactly this failure shape for a scalar invariant: the non-stratified $\beta$'s cannot provide sharp two-sided traveling salesman inequalities for the Carnot group $G=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}$ of Hausdorff dimension 6, and a stratified refinement is required before the scalar separates what it should separate [4]. Either that phenomenon realized for $\Lambda$, or the analogous collapse recorded in the measurement-stratigraphy cross-review, where two forecast eras merge into a single candidate and a ranking becomes degenerate [13], would falsify F4.

## 4. Analysis

All inputs are either definitions of Section 3 or explicitly stated here; every arithmetic step is shown.

**Input 1.** Tally depth $n=9$. (Chosen parameter.)
**Input 2.** Prime $p=2$, tree depth $n=10$. (Chosen parameter.)
**Input 3.** Real precision $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$ (unit interval partitioned into cells of width $2^{-3}$). (Chosen parameter.)
**Input 4.** Primes $\{2,3,5\}$ for the finite adele-type product. (Chosen parameter.)
**Input 5.** Prime $p=3$ for the non-dyadic analysis. (Chosen parameter.)
**Input 6.** Projection parameters for a non-dyadic example: $p=3$, $n=5$, $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$. (Assumed for illustration.)

### Derivation 1 (tally states)

With $n=9$ marks the distinguishable counts are $\{0,1,\dots,9\}$, so
$$N_{1}(9)=9+1=10.$$

### Derivation 2 (real-enclosure states)

The unit interval $[0,1]$ partitioned into cells of width $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$ contains
$$N_{4}(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}= \frac{1}{2^{-3}} = 2^{3}=8$$
distinguishable cells.

### Derivation 3 ($p$-adic tree states)

The residue ring $\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}$ with $p=2$, $n=10$ has exactly
$$N_{6}(10)=2^{10}=1024$$
elements. Equivalently, the rooted binary tree of depth 10 has $2^{10}=1024$ leaves, one per residue class modulo $2^{10}$.

### Derivation 4 (Monna cross-stratum comparison)

The Monna projection $\pi_{M}$ maps the depth-10 binary tree onto the real line at precision $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$. Substituting $N_{6}(10)=1024$ (Derivation 3) and $N_{4}(2^{-3})=8$ (Derivation 2) into the cross-stratum comparison $\Delta_{6\to4}=\log_{2}N_{6}-\log_{2}N_{4}$ of Section 3.3,
$$\Delta_{6\to4}= \log_{2}N_{6}(10)-\log_{2}N_{4}(\varepsilon)
= \log_{2}(1024)-\log_{2}(8)=10-3=7.$$
Checking via the ratio form,
$$\Delta_{6\to4}= \log_{2}\!\left(\frac{1024}{8}\right)=\log_{2}(128)=7,$$
and $2^{7}=128$ confirms the ratio. Because strata 6 and 4 are not adjacent, this quantity is the cross-stratum comparison $\Delta$, not an instance of the adjacent-stratum loss functional $\Lambda$.

### Derivation 5 (linearity and vanishing)

Fix $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$ and vary depth $n$. Since $N_{6}(n)=2^{n}$ and $N_{4}(\varepsilon)=2^{3}$,
$$\Delta_{6\to4}(n)=\log_{2}(2^{n})-\log_{2}(2^{3})=n-3,$$
which is strictly increasing in $n$. At $n=3$ the comparison is
$$\Delta_{6\to4}(3)=3-3=0,$$
so the depth-3 binary tree and the precision-$2^{-3}$ continuum have identical state counts $2^{3}=8$ and the projection is loss-free there.

### Derivation 6 (finite simultaneous-valuation products)

Take the primes $\{2,3,5\}$. At depth 1 the simultaneous-valuation state space is the product of residue rings $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$, with count
$$N_{7}^{(1)}=2\cdot3\cdot5=30.$$
At depth 2 it is $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z}$, with count
$$N_{7}^{(2)}=4\cdot9\cdot25=900.$$
Both products are finite because each factor is finite; the Chinese remainder theorem makes the simultaneous residue classes independent, so the total count is the product of the individual counts.

### Derivation 7 (non-dyadic no-matching)

Fix $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$ and take $p=3$. Zero comparison loss requires
$$n\log_{2}3=\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)=3,$$
so the required depth is
$$n=\frac{3}{\log_{2}3}.$$
We compute $\log_{2}3=\ln 3/\ln 2\approx1.584963$, hence
$$n\approx\frac{3}{1.584963}=1.892789\approx1.893.$$
Since $n$ must be an integer depth, no integer solution exists; at $n=1$ the comparison is $\Delta_{6\to4}(1)=1.584963-3=-1.415037$ (the tree is coarser than the target), and at $n=2$ it is $\Delta_{6\to4}(2)=3.169925-3=0.169925$ (the tree is already finer). The comparison jumps across zero without vanishing.

### Derivation 8 (projected comparison for a non-dyadic example)

Assume parameters $p=3$, $n=5$, $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$. Then
$$\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)=\log_{2}100=\frac{\ln 100}{\ln 2}\approx6.643856,$$
and
$$\Delta_{6\to4}=5\log_{2}3-\log_{2}100\approx5\times1.584963-6.643856\approx1.280957\;\text{bits}.$$
We report this as a projection under the stated assumptions.

**Result R1.** The Monna cross-stratum comparison obeys the exact linear law
$$\Delta_{6\to4}(n)=n-3\;\text{bits}$$
for base $p=2$ and precision $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$; increasing the tree depth strictly increases the state-count excess of the source over a fixed-precision continuum target.

**Result R2.** A loss-free matching occurs at the dyadic pair $(n=3,\varepsilon=2^{-3})$, where the 2-adic tree and the real interval have identical state counts ($2^{3}=8$). No integer depth yields zero comparison loss for the non-dyadic base $p=3$ at the same precision.

**Result R3.** The finite simultaneous-valuation product over the primes $\{2,3,5\}$ yields $30$ states at depth 1 and $900$ states at depth 2, illustrating how the adele-type construction multiplies state counts across independent $p$-adic components.

## 6. Discussion

### Limitations

* The recovery claims for strata 4, 5, and 7 remain conjectural; state-count arithmetic is verified only for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 at finite depth.
* The loss functional $\Lambda$ and the comparison $\Delta$ count *states* but not *structure*: two projections can destroy the same number of bits while destroying very different algebraic features (e.g., order versus branching). The stratified-$\beta$-numbers work makes this limitation precise for scalar invariants over stratified structures: for the Carnot group $G=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}$ of Hausdorff dimension 6, the non-stratified $\beta$'s cannot provide sharp two-sided traveling salesman inequalities, and only the stratified refinement does [4]. A scalar over a stratified source is not determined by the source's size alone.
* The Monna analysis assumes the tree is exactly a $p$-adic ball in state count; a more general tree-like limit with a different branching profile would alter $\Delta_{6\to4}$.
* The finite adele-type product of Derivation 6 is a toy: the true adele ring is a restricted product including the real factor, and the count of 30 says nothing about its topology.
* $\Lambda$ and $\Delta$ are defined only for finite state counts; at infinite depth both source and target counts diverge, requiring renormalisation or a rate formulation.
* The literature treatment, while broader than the published version's, remains confined to the thirteen works discussed in Section 2; claims that would need support from works outside that list (for example, on the history of number systems or on floating-point error analysis) are not made here.

The remaining part of the Discussion addresses failure modes and the conditions under which the central results would fail.

### Failure modes

* **Non-canonical realizations.** A single enclosure operator can admit multiple inequivalent concrete realizations; the operator, not the realization, determines the stratum. The noncanonical-number-systems work is the sharpest published instance: for every base $b$ with $|b|\geq4$ there exist infinitely many digit sets not containing 0 on which every integer still has a finite expansion $a=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}d_{i}b^{i}$, with the dynamic mapping $T:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}$, $a\mapsto(a-d(a))/b$, governing which digit sets are valid—for example, $\{1,2\}$ is a valid digit set for the base $-2$ [7]. The state counts of Section 4 are therefore counts for the operator, and a different realization of $E_{6}$ could carry a different branching profile while retaining $N_{6}(n)=p^{n}$.
* **Operator-list incompleteness.** If a number system in the historical sequence required a completion outside $\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Q}_{p}:p\text{ prime}\}$, the present operator list would be incomplete, falsifying F3. By Ostrowski's theorem as stated in [11], the completions of $\mathbb{Q}$ are exactly the real line and one $p$-adic field per prime, so the theorem asserts that no such completion exists; the failure mode is therefore the misapplication of the list to structures that are not completions of $\mathbb{Q}$.
* **$\Lambda$ inadequacy.** Two intuitively different projections could share the same $\Lambda$ value while differing in preserved structure, mirroring the inadequacy demonstrated in [4], where the non-stratified $\beta$'s fail to separate structures in $G=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}$ that the stratified $\beta$'s separate; this would falsify F4 and motivate a structural refinement of $\Lambda$.
* **Count-based relabeling.** A count-based reading can be definitional rather than informative. The super-powers work supplies the positive contrast that disciplines this worry: its valuation-theoretic quantities support nontrivial theorems, proving equivalences concerning the number of distinct prime factors $\omega(s_{n})$ of a sum of super-powers, including the existence of a base $\theta>1$ with $\omega(s_{n})>\mathrm{slog}_{\theta}(n)$ for infinitely many $n$ under stated non-degeneracy conditions [9]. If the loss functional $\Lambda$ were shown to carry no comparable non-definitional content—restating $N_{6}(n)=p^{n}$ without predictive content—the present framework would reduce to bookkeeping.

### What would falsify the main result

The main result is the linear law $\Delta_{6\to4}(n)=n-3$ at fixed $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$ and the loss-free matching at $n=3$. A single counterexample falsifies it: any projection from a depth-$n$ binary tree to a precision-$2^{-3}$ continuum whose distinguishable-state count differs from $2^{n}$ at the source or $2^{3}=8$ at the target would break the identity $\Delta_{6\to4}(n)=\log_{2}(2^{n})-\log_{2}(2^{3})=n-3$, because the law is an immediate consequence of those two counts and no other input. Equivalently, exhibiting a depth at which the comparison decreases as the source is refined at fixed target precision would falsify F2 and with it R1. The derivation is three lines long, so the falsification test is correspondingly cheap: recompute $N_{6}(n)$ and $N_{4}(\varepsilon)$ under any alternative definition of distinguishable state and check whether the difference remains linear in $n$.

### Limitations list

The result rests on the following assumptions, in decreasing order of sensitivity:

1. **State-count definition.** $\Lambda$ and $\Delta$ depend entirely on what counts as a distinguishable state; the counts $N_{6}(n)=p^{n}$ and $N_{4}(\varepsilon)=1/\varepsilon$ are definitions, not measurements, and a different definition changes $\Delta_{6\to4}$ by an additive constant or destroys linearity altogether.
2. **Verified-strata restriction.** State-count arithmetic is verified only for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 at finite depth, and only the state-count formulas for those strata are used in finite arithmetic; the recovery claims for strata 4, 5, and 7 remain conjectural.
3. **Branching profile.** The Monna analysis assumes the source tree is exactly a $p$-adic ball in state count; a tree with a different branching profile alters $\Delta_{6\to4}$, as the scalar-inadequacy example of [4] makes concrete for stratified structures.
4. **Finite counts only.** $\Lambda$ and $\Delta$ are defined only for finite state counts; at infinite depth both source and target counts diverge, and a rate formulation or renormalisation would be needed.
5. **Toy adele product.** The finite simultaneous-valuation product of Derivation 6 omits the real factor and the restricted-product topology of $\mathbb{A}$; the counts 30 and 900 describe state spaces, not the adele ring.
6. **Operator–realization gap.** As [7] shows for positional systems, one operator admits many inequivalent realizations, so a state count for an operator need not describe every concrete system built on it.
7. **Literature scope.** Claims requiring sources outside the thirteen works of Section 2 are not made here.

## 7. Practical Implications and Engineering Pathways

**(a) Primary users and decision impact.**
Researchers who must choose a numeric representation for formal models—in mathematical physics, constructive analysis, and exact-arithmetic software—are the principal users of the comparison $\Delta_{6\to4}$ and the state-count formulas of Sections 4–5. When a model requires a target real precision $\varepsilon$, the user can compute the bit excess
$$\Delta_{6\to4}(n,p,\varepsilon)=n\log_{2}p-\log_{2}\!\bigl(1/\varepsilon\bigr)$$
(Derivation 5) for any candidate $p$-adic depth $n$. If $\Delta_{6\to4}=0$, the $p$-adic tree can replace the real interval at that precision without state-count loss; the dyadic case $p=2$, $n=3$, $\varepsilon=2^{-3}$ is the first such matching (Result R2). The decision that changes is the choice of storage representation: a modeler can store data in a depth-3 binary tree rather than a floating-point format and know, by the count $2^{3}=8=N_{4}(2^{-3})$, that no distinguishable state is discarded by the projection. The finite-precision reading that motivates this decision is the adelic-physics observation that every measurement produces a rational number to finite precision and every computer simulation operates on finite-precision rationals [11].

**(b) Technology readiness.**
The work is purely analytical, has not undergone peer review, and involves no hardware or software implementation. Against the NASA Technology Readiness Level definitions it resides at TRL 1–2: basic principles have been observed and stated, but no proof-of-concept application has been demonstrated. Analytical extensions do not exceed TRL 3; no claim of manufacturing or deployment readiness is made.

**(c) Minimal next experiment.**
A computational experiment that would test, and possibly falsify, the monotonicity claim of F2 can be specified conditionally, on the model of the falsification protocol of [12]:

1. Generate the residue set $\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}$ for a chosen prime $p$ (for example $p=3$) and depths $n\in\{1,2,3\}$.
2. Map each residue class to a sub-interval of $[0,1]$ of width $\varepsilon$ (for example $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$) by a deterministic Monna-type rule.
3. Count the distinct real cells $N_{4}(\varepsilon)=1/\varepsilon$ (Derivation 2) and compute the empirical comparison $\Delta_{\text{emp}}=\log_{2}N_{6}(n)-\log_{2}N_{4}(\varepsilon)$.
4. Compare $\Delta_{\text{emp}}$ with the predicted value $\Delta_{6\to4}=n\log_{2}p-\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)$ (Derivation 5).

If for any $(p,n,\varepsilon)$ the computed comparison deviates from the predicted value, the linear law would be falsified, invalidating F2 and with it Result R1. The experiment requires no hardware beyond exact integer arithmetic.

**(d) First-order engineering constraints.**

* *Data representation.* Storing $p^{n}$ residues requires memory of at least $p^{n}$ integers (Derivation 3); for the depth-10 dyadic case this is 1024 entries.
* *Precision handling.* The real target demands $1/\varepsilon$ cells; for $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ this is 100 cells (Derivation 2).
* *Computational throughput.* Mapping each of the $p^{n}$ residues to a real cell entails $O(p^{n})$ elementary operations, so runtime scales linearly in $p^{n}$.
* *Software tooling.* Exact integer arithmetic is needed to avoid overflow once $p^{n}$ exceeds native word size.

Whether other constraints (regulatory, cost, or materials) bind first is an open question; the present analysis derives only the state-count-driven ones.

**(e) Projected cost and performance bounds (assumptions).**
Assume a single elementary mapping operation costs $c_{\text{op}}=10^{-6}\,\text{s}$. The total runtime $T$ for depth $n$ and base $p$ is then
$$T=c_{\text{op}}\,p^{n}.$$
For the dyadic loss-free case $p=2$, $n=3$ this gives $T\approx 10^{-6}\times 2^{3}=8\times 10^{-6}\,\text{s}$. For the non-dyadic example $p=3$, $n=5$ the projection gives $T\approx 10^{-6}\times 3^{5}=2.43\times 10^{-4}\,\text{s}$. These figures are projections based on the assumed operation cost and the state count $N_{6}(n)=p^{n}$; actual timings depend on implementation details.

**(f) Direct risks and misuse pathways.**
Treating the scalar comparison $\Delta$ as a complete measure of suitability for replacing a real-valued representation ignores the structural differences recorded in Section 6:

two projections with identical $\Delta$ can preserve different algebraic features. Deploying a $p$-adic data structure solely because it yields a low comparison value could introduce defects in algorithms that rely on the order properties of $\mathbb{R}$, since the $p$-adic order is not compatible with the real order. The cost projection of part (e) assumes idealised operation costs; real systems may exhibit latency and memory effects not captured by the model. A further misuse path is over-claiming the loss-free matching: the identity $2^{3}=8=N_{4}(2^{-3})$ is a state-count coincidence at one parameter pair, and treating it as evidence that the $p$-adic tree can replace the continuum at all precisions would contradict the non-dyadic no-matching result of Derivation 7, which shows that the matching fails already for the base $p=3$ at the same precision.

## 8. Conclusion

The tower of number systems can be read as a sequence of enclosure operations applied to a primitive mark, and the cost of each operation can be measured in bits. This paper made that reading quantitative where a finite count exists: state-count arithmetic was derived with every step shown for strata 0, 1, 3, and 6 at finite depth; the Monna projection was presented explicitly as a cross-stratum comparison $\Delta_{6\to4}$ rather than an instance of the adjacent-stratum loss functional $\Lambda$, yielding $\Delta_{6\to4}=7$ bits at $(p=2,n=10,\varepsilon=2^{-3})$, the exact linear law $\Delta_{6\to4}(n)=n-3$ at fixed precision, and the loss-free matching depth $n=3$; the non-dyadic base $p=3$ was shown to admit no integer zero-loss depth, with the required depth $n\approx1.893$ non-integral; and finite simultaneous-valuation products of 30 and 900 states were computed over the primes $\{2,3,5\}$. Four falsifiability conditions convert the stratification thesis, the loss functional, the operator list, and the adequacy of $\Lambda$ into killable claims, with the operator list underwritten by the Ostrowski classification as stated in [11] and the adequacy condition disciplined by the scalar-inadequacy precedent of [4] and the merger-collapse recorded in [13].

The framework's honest boundary is as important as its results. The recovery claims for strata 4, 5, and 7 remain conjectural; $\Lambda$ and $\Delta$ count states but not structure; the finite adele-type product omits the real factor and the restricted-product topology; and the counts rest on definitions of distinguishable state that an alternative definition could shift by an additive constant or destroy linearity altogether. The next step is the minimal computational experiment of Section 7: recompute $N_{6}(n)$ and $N_{4}(\varepsilon)$ under alternative state-count definitions and check whether the linear law survives. If it does, the choice of number system acquires the measurable criterion this paper set out to supply; if it does not, the negative result sharpens the question in the same way the published version's own limitations sharpened this revision [10].

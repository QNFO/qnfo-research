## Sub-Literature 1: EM-Knot Solitons and Spinning Soliton Electron Models (H1)

### Core Sources

1. **Rañada, A.F. (1989).** "A topological theory of the electromagnetic field." *Letters in Mathematical Physics*, 18(2), 97–106.
   - **DOI:** 10.1007/BF00401864
   - **Bin tag:** A — constructs explicit Hopf-fibration solutions of Maxwell's equations in vacuum. Establishes that EM fields can carry topological charge (helicity = linking number of electric and magnetic field lines). Foundational for all subsequent EM knot work.
   - **Relevance:** Defines the mathematical framework invoked metaphorically in Doc A. Shows that EM knots are real solutions, but also that they are massless (propagate at c) — the gap to massive fermions is the entire problem.

2. **Trueba, J.L. & Rañada, A.F. (1996).** "The electromagnetic helicoid." *European Journal of Physics*, 17(3), 141–144.
   - **DOI:** 10.1088/0143-0807/17/3/008
   - **Bin tag:** A — pedagogical construction of helical EM field configurations. Establishes that helicity and topology are conserved in vacuum EM.
   - **Relevance:** The "helicoid" is visually evocative of Doc A's helical electron, but the physics is vacuum EM (massless, spin-1), not massive spin-1/2.

3. **Irvine, W.T.M. & Bouwmeester, D. (2008).** "Linked and knotted beams of light." *Nature Physics*, 4(9), 716–720.
   - **DOI:** 10.1038/nphys1056
   - **Bin tag:** A — experimental realization of knotted EM fields at microwave frequencies. Demonstrates that topological EM configurations are physically realizable. Important: still massless, still spin-1.
   - **Relevance:** Shows the experimental reality of EM knots, grounding what might otherwise seem purely mathematical.

4. **Arrayás, M., Bouwmeester, D., & Trueba, J.L. (2017).** "Knotted optical fields." *Physics Reports*, 667, 1–61.
   - **DOI:** 10.1016/j.physrep.2016.11.001
   - **Bin tag:** A — comprehensive review. Documents the full state of the art in knotted EM fields through 2017.
   - **Relevance:** Review article useful for surveying the literature. Explicitly notes that knotted EM fields are bosonic.

5. **Burinskii, A. (2000).** "Kerr–Newman electron as spinning soliton." *Gravitation and Cosmology*, 6, 116–120.
   - **arXiv:** hep-th/0008129
   - **Bin tag:** B — the central paper in the Kerr–Newman soliton electron program. Shows that the KN solution has g=2 (matching the Dirac electron) and a ring singularity interpretable as a closed null curve. Works in the *classical* regime; spin-1/2 quantization is not derived.
   - **Relevance:** The closest thing in the literature to Doc A's "electron as knotted photon." Important constraints: (a) classical, not quantum; (b) the "spin" is the KN angular momentum parameter, not derived from field topology; (c) quantization to fermionic statistics is not performed.

6. **Burinskii, A. (2015).** "Gravity vs. quantum theory: the Kerr–Newman electron." *Journal of Physics: Conference Series*, 615, 012005.
   - **DOI:** 10.1088/1742-6596/615/1/012005
   - **Bin tag:** B — later synthesis of the KN soliton program. Addresses the Dirac equation limit and the Compton-scale structure. Still does not derive spin-1/2 statistics.
   - **Relevance:** Updates the KN soliton picture; useful for tracking whether the spin-statistics gap has been addressed (it hasn't).

7. **Burinskii, A. (2023).** "Kerr–Newman electron and the problem of spin." *Physics of Particles and Nuclei*, 54(5), 865–877.
   - **Bin tag:** B — most recent survey. Explicitly engages with the spin-statistics problem but does not resolve it.
   - **Relevance:** Confirms that as of 2023, the spin-statistics gap in KN soliton models remains open.

8. **Chernitskii, A.A. (1999).** "Born–Infeld electrodynamics: Clifford algebra and solitons." *Journal of High Energy Physics*, 1999(11), 015.
   - **arXiv:** hep-th/9907017
   - **Bin tag:** B — explores soliton solutions in nonlinear electrodynamics (Born–Infeld). BI theory modifies Maxwell's equations to allow finite-energy point charges. Soliton solutions exist but inherit the spin of the underlying field (spin-1).
   - **Relevance:** Alternative nonlinear-ED approach to electron-as-soliton. Same spin-statistics barrier as Kerr–Newman.

9. **Faddeev, L. & Niemi, A.J. (1997).** "Knots and particles." *Nature*, 387(6628), 58–61.
   - **DOI:** 10.1038/387058a0
   - **Bin tag:** B — Faddeev–Skyrme model knot solitons in a nonlinear sigma model. Topological charges classified by Hopf invariant. Energy spectrum depends on coupling constants (free parameters).
   - **Relevance:** Shows that knot solitons can exist in field theory and carry topological quantum numbers. But: (a) the field is not EM; (b) the energy spectrum has free parameters; (c) spin-statistics is not addressed. Important precedent, not a solution.

10. **Battye, R.A. & Sutcliffe, P.M. (1998).** "Knots as stable soliton solutions in a three-dimensional classical field theory." *Physical Review Letters*, 81(22), 4798–4801.
    - **DOI:** 10.1103/PhysRevLett.81.4798
    - **Bin tag:** B — numerical construction of stable knot solitons in the Faddeev–Skyrme model. Shows topological stability.
    - **Relevance:** Demonstrates that knotted solitons can be numerically stable. Still: different field theory, no spin-1/2, no mass prediction.

### Background Sources

11. **Hestenes, D. (1990).** "The Zitterbewegung interpretation of quantum mechanics." *Foundations of Physics*, 20(10), 1213–1232.
    - **DOI:** 10.1007/BF01889466
    - **Bin tag:** B — spacetime algebra reinterpretation of the Dirac equation. Argues that Zitterbewegung is a real helical motion, not just an operator artifact. Mathematically isomorphic to standard QM; ontological interpretation differs.
    - **Relevance:** The primary source for the "helical electron" interpretation. Important: isomorphism means it makes identical empirical predictions to standard QM — it's an interpretation, not a distinct theory.

12. **Pauli, W. (1940).** "The connection between spin and statistics." *Physical Review*, 58(8), 716–722.
    - **DOI:** 10.1103/PhysRev.58.716
    - **Bin tag:** A — the original spin-statistics theorem proof. [Est] — established physics.
    - **Relevance:** The theorem that blocks H1c. Must be cited whenever the "electron as knotted photon" claim is evaluated.

## Sub-Literature 2: Holographic Entanglement and Emergent Geometry (H2)

### Core Sources

13. **Ryu, S. & Takayanagi, T. (2006).** "Holographic derivation of entanglement entropy from the anti–de Sitter space/conformal field theory correspondence." *Physical Review Letters*, 96(18), 181602.
    - **DOI:** 10.1103/PhysRevLett.96.181602
    - **Bin tag:** A — the foundational RT formula: entanglement entropy in a boundary CFT equals the area of a minimal surface in the bulk AdS. [Est] — highly cited, well-verified within AdS/CFT.
    - **Relevance:** The most commonly cited result for "geometry emerges from entanglement." Important constraint: applies to AdS boundary CFTs, not to de Sitter or flat spacetime without extension.

14. **Hubeny, V.E., Rangamani, M., & Takayanagi, T. (2007).** "A covariant holographic entanglement entropy proposal." *Journal of High Energy Physics*, 2007(07), 062.
    - **DOI:** 10.1088/1126-6708/2007/07/062
    - **Bin tag:** A — covariant (time-dependent) generalization of RT. [Est].
    - **Relevance:** Extends the RT formula to time-dependent settings. Still AdS/CFT.

15. **Swingle, B. (2012).** "Entanglement renormalization and holography." *Physical Review D*, 86(6), 065007.
    - **DOI:** 10.1103/PhysRevD.86.065007
    - **Bin tag:** B — tensor-network (MERA) model of AdS/CFT. Shows how entanglement renormalization can produce an emergent radial dimension resembling AdS geometry. Important: toy model — engineered to match AdS, not derived generically.
    - **Relevance:** The most concrete "geometry from entanglement" construction. Shows the idea works in toy models. The gap to our universe (dS/flat) is massive.

16. **Pastawski, F., Yoshida, B., Harlow, D., & Preskill, J. (2015).** "Holographic quantum error-correcting codes: toy models for the bulk/boundary correspondence." *Journal of High Energy Physics*, 2015(06), 149.
    - **DOI:** 10.1007/JHEP06(2015)149
    - **Bin tag:** B — HaPPY code: a tensor-network toy model implementing holographic error correction. [Est] within the toy-model domain.
    - **Relevance:** Doc B's Phase 2.2 proposes reconstructing this as a worked example. Useful for grounding "emergent geometry" in something checked rather than cited.

17. **Van Raamsdonk, M. (2010).** "Building up spacetime with quantum entanglement." *General Relativity and Gravitation*, 42(10), 2323–2329.
    - **DOI:** 10.1007/s10714-010-1034-0
    - **Bin tag:** B — influential essay arguing that entanglement is the "glue" of spacetime. [Open] — provocative but not a rigorous derivation.
    - **Relevance:** Captures the spirit of the entanglement-geometry connection without overclaiming.

18. **Maldacena, J. & Susskind, L. (2013).** "Cool horizons for entangled black holes." *Fortschritte der Physik*, 61(9), 781–811.
    - **DOI:** 10.1002/prop.201300020
    - **Bin tag:** B — ER=EPR conjecture: entanglement (Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen) is equivalent to wormholes (Einstein–Rosen bridges). [Open] — debated, not established.
    - **Relevance:** The most dramatic version of "entanglement IS geometry." Important: even if true, doesn't privilege photons specifically — the conjecture is about entanglement generally.

19. **Cao, C., Carroll, S.M., & Michalakis, S. (2017).** "Space from Hilbert space: recovering geometry from bulk entanglement." *Physical Review D*, 95(2), 024031.
    - **DOI:** 10.1103/PhysRevD.95.024031
    - **Bin tag:** B — program to derive spatial geometry from the entanglement structure of a Hilbert space, without assuming a pre-existing spacetime. [Open].
    - **Relevance:** Closest to Doc A's "pre-geometric information space" idea. Constraint: still a research program, not an established result.

### Background Sources

20. **Bombelli, L., Lee, J., Meyer, D., & Sorkin, R.D. (1987).** "Space-time as a causal set." *Physical Review Letters*, 59(5), 521–524.
    - **DOI:** 10.1103/PhysRevLett.59.521
    - **Bin tag:** B — causal set theory: spacetime emerges from a discrete partially ordered set. [Open] — active research program, no experimental confirmation.
    - **Relevance:** Alternative approach to emergent spacetime, useful for triangulating the "emergent geometry" claim space.

21. **Jacobson, T. (1995).** "Thermodynamics of spacetime: the Einstein equation of state." *Physical Review Letters*, 75(7), 1260–1263.
    - **DOI:** 10.1103/PhysRevLett.75.1260
    - **Bin tag:** A — derives Einstein's equations from thermodynamic considerations (entropy proportional to area). [Est] — highly influential, well-cited.
    - **Relevance:** Shows that gravity may be emergent from thermodynamic/informational principles. Supports the "spacetime is emergent" direction without claiming photons are privileged.

## Sub-Literature 3: Reversible Computing and Thermodynamics of Computation (H3)

### Core Sources

22. **Landauer, R. (1961).** "Irreversibility and heat generation in the computing process." *IBM Journal of Research and Development*, 5(3), 183–191.
    - **DOI:** 10.1147/rd.53.0183
    - **Bin tag:** A — the original derivation of the Landauer limit: kT ln 2 per bit erased. [Est] — foundational.
    - **Relevance:** The thermodynamic baseline against which all "below Landauer" claims must be measured.

23. **Bennett, C.H. (1973).** "Logical reversibility of computation." *IBM Journal of Research and Development*, 17(6), 525–532.
    - **DOI:** 10.1147/rd.176.0525
    - **Bin tag:** A — proof that any computation can be performed reversibly, approaching zero dissipation in the adiabatic limit. [Est].
    - **Relevance:** The key result that makes H3's "advantage over reversible computing" claim questionable: if Bennett already proved reversibility works, what does COTT add?

24. **Bennett, C.H. (1982).** "The thermodynamics of computation — a review." *International Journal of Theoretical Physics*, 21(12), 905–940.
    - **DOI:** 10.1007/BF02084158
    - **Bin tag:** A — comprehensive review. [Est].
    - **Relevance:** Useful for understanding the full landscape of reversible computing before evaluating COTT claims.

25. **Bérut, A., Arakelyan, A., Petrosyan, A., Ciliberto, S., Dillenschneider, R., & Lutz, E. (2012).** "Experimental verification of Landauer's principle linking information and thermodynamics." *Nature*, 483(7388), 187–189.
    - **DOI:** 10.1038/nature10872
    - **Bin tag:** A — experimental confirmation of the Landauer limit using a colloidal particle in a double-well potential. [Est].
    - **Relevance:** Establishes that the Landauer limit is experimentally real, not just theoretical.

26. **Fredkin, E. & Toffoli, T. (1982).** "Conservative logic." *International Journal of Theoretical Physics*, 21(3), 219–253.
    - **DOI:** 10.1007/BF01857727
    - **Bin tag:** A — Fredkin gate (universal reversible gate) and conservative logic. [Est] — foundational for reversible computing circuit design.
    - **Relevance:** The standard against which any "new" reversible logic family (including COTT) must be compared.

27. **DeBenedictis, E.P. (2018).** "Adiabatic reversible computing." *In: Rebooting Computing* (IEEE). 
    - **Bin tag:** A — survey of adiabatic CMOS and reversible logic hardware implementations.
    - **Relevance:** Establishes that reversible computing hardware exists and has been benchmarked.

### COTT-Specific Sources

28. **Carlström, J. (2004).** "Wheels — on division by zero." *Mathematical Structures in Computer Science*, 14(1), 143–184.
    - **DOI:** 10.1017/S0960129503004110
    - **Bin tag:** A — the mathematical foundation of wheel theory (extension of a ring with an inverse for zero). [Est] — legitimate mathematics.
    - **Relevance:** The mathematical structure COTT claims as its foundation. Important: mathematical legitimacy ≠ physical relevance. Wheel theory is rigorous; whether it enables new physical computation is a separate question.

29. **Toffoli, T. (1980).** "Reversible computing." *In: Automata, Languages and Programming* (ICALP), 632–644.
    - **Bin tag:** A — earlier formulation of reversible computing, pre-Fredkin gate. [Est].
    - **Relevance:** Shows that the "total operations" property COTT claims is already present in standard reversible logic (operations are total on their defined domain; undefined operations are excluded by construction).

### Background Sources

30. **Frank, M.P. (2005).** "Approaching the Landauer bound: physical limits of reversible computing." *Sandia National Laboratories Report*, SAND2005-7594.
    - **Bin tag:** A — engineering analysis of how close we can get to the Landauer limit.
    - **Relevance:** Quantifies the gap between theoretical limit and practical implementation.

31. **Lloyd, S. (2000).** "Ultimate physical limits to computation." *Nature*, 406(6799), 1047–1054.
    - **DOI:** 10.1038/35023282
    - **Bin tag:** A — comprehensive review of physical limits on computation. [Est].
    - **Relevance:** Context for understanding what "below Landauer" would actually mean — it would violate the Margolus–Levitin bound on computation rate per unit energy.

## Sub-Literature 4: p-Adic/Adelic Quantum Mechanics and Quantum Chaos (H4)

### Core Sources

32. **Vladimirov, V.S., Volovich, I.V., & Zelenov, E.I. (1994).** *p-Adic Analysis and Mathematical Physics*. World Scientific.
    - **ISBN:** 978-981-02-0880-0
    - **Bin tag:** A — the standard textbook on p-adic mathematical physics. [Est] — rigorous mathematics.
    - **Relevance:** Defines the formalism that Doc A invokes nominally. Important: the book develops p-adic QM as a mathematical structure, not as an empirical claim about our universe.

33. **Volovich, I.V. (1987).** "Number theory as the ultimate physical theory." *CERN Preprint*, CERN-TH.4781/87.
    - **Bin tag:** C — speculative essay on number theory as physics. [Open] — not established, not falsified, primarily philosophical.
    - **Relevance:** The closest precedent for Doc A's adelic claims. Important: Volovich himself frames this as speculation, not established physics.

34. **Freund, P.G.O. & Olson, M. (1987).** "Non-archimedean strings." *Physics Letters B*, 199(2), 186–190.
    - **DOI:** 10.1016/0370-2693(87)91356-6
    - **Bin tag:** B — p-adic string theory. [Open] — mathematically well-defined but no experimental support.
    - **Relevance:** Shows that p-adic structures can be incorporated into physical theories (string theory). Important: this is a mathematical extension, not an empirical claim about low-energy electron physics.

35. **Brekke, L., Freund, P.G.O., Olson, M., & Witten, E. (1988).** "Non-archimedean string dynamics." *Nuclear Physics B*, 302(3), 365–402.
    - **DOI:** 10.1016/0550-3213(88)90207-6
    - **Bin tag:** B — further development of p-adic string theory. [Open].
    - **Relevance:** Establishes the p-adic string theory literature; confirms that adelic structures have a legitimate (if speculative) place in theoretical physics.

36. **Dragovich, B., Khrennikov, A.Yu., Kozyrev, S.V., & Volovich, I.V. (2009).** "On p-adic mathematical physics." *p-Adic Numbers, Ultrametric Analysis, and Applications*, 1(1), 1–17.
    - **DOI:** 10.1134/S2070046609010014
    - **Bin tag:** B — review of p-adic mathematical physics applications. [Open].
    - **Relevance:** Survey useful for understanding which p-adic claims have mathematical support vs. which are speculative.

37. **Khrennikov, A. (2004).** *Information Dynamics in Cognitive, Psychological, Social, and Anomalous Phenomena.* Springer.
    - **ISBN:** 978-1-4020-1868-8
    - **Bin tag:** B — application of p-adic models to cognitive science. Interesting but far from standard physics.
    - **Relevance:** Shows that p-adic structures appear in unexpected domains; supports the idea that ultrametricity has broad applicability, but doesn't establish it as fundamental to spacetime.

### Quantum Chaos Background (H4a)

38. **Wintgen, D. & Friedrich, H. (1986).** "Classical and quantum-mechanical transition between regularity and chaos in the diamagnetic Kepler problem." *Physical Review Letters*, 57(5), 571–574.
    - **DOI:** 10.1103/PhysRevLett.57.571
    - **Bin tag:** A — experimental observation of quantum chaos in Rydberg atoms (diamagnetic Kepler problem). [Est].
    - **Relevance:** The methodology for H4a: spectral statistics can distinguish regular from chaotic dynamics. But the observed statistics are explained by classical-quantum correspondence, not ultrametricity.

39. **Stöckmann, H.-J. (1999).** *Quantum Chaos: An Introduction.* Cambridge University Press.
    - **ISBN:** 978-0-521-59284-0
    - **Bin tag:** A — standard textbook. [Est].
    - **Relevance:** Reference for the spacing-statistic methodology (Poisson, Wigner–Dyson, Brody distributions).

40. **Gräf, H.-D. et al. (1992).** "Distribution of eigenmodes in a superconducting stadium billiard." *Physical Review Letters*, 69(9), 1296–1299.
    - **DOI:** 10.1103/PhysRevLett.69.1296
    - **Bin tag:** A — experimental quantum chaos in microwave billiards (analogous to Rydberg atoms). [Est].
    - **Relevance:** Additional experimental precedent for spectral statistics methodology.

### Adelic Formula Background

41. **Neukirch, J. (1999).** *Algebraic Number Theory.* Springer.
    - **ISBN:** 978-3-540-65399-8
    - **Bin tag:** A — standard textbook including the adelic product formula. [Est] — rigorous mathematics.
    - **Relevance:** The adelic product formula is a theorem about rational numbers, not a generator of physical constants. Must be cited to prevent category confusion.

42. **Manin, Yu.I. & Panchishkin, A.A. (2005).** *Introduction to Modern Number Theory.* Springer.
    - **Bin tag:** A — comprehensive reference on adeles and ideles. [Est].
    - **Relevance:** Further context for the adelic product formula. Affirms: no known connection to the fine-structure constant.

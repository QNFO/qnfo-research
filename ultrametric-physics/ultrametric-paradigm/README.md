## ✨ Features

- 📑 **Auto-generated sidebar** from document headings
- 🌓 **Dark/light theme** persisted across sessions
- 🔍 **Full-text search** with relevance indicators
- 📐 **MathJax** — all equations beautifully typeset; click to isolate
- 📊 **Reading progress bar**
- 📁 **Collapsible proof blocks** with estimated reading time
- 💬 **Glossary tooltips** — hover any defined term for its definition
- 🔗 **Shareable section anchors** — copy a direct link to any section
- 🖼️ **Diagram zoom** — click any text diagram to expand
- 📱 **Fully responsive** — desktop, tablet, and phone
- 📖 **Section read tracking** — marks visited chapters in the sidebar

## 📖 About the Paradigm

The Ultrametric Paradigm argues that the most consequential design decision in any theoretical framework is the choice of distance measure. The familiar Archimedean choice (distances add) produces the physics we know. The ultrametric choice (distances are bounded by the maximum) produces a hierarchical tree geometry — the Bruhat–Tits tree — from which intrinsic fault tolerance, deterministic explanations for apparent randomness, and a unified geometric account of prime distribution, quantum measurement, program halting, and the structure of meaning all emerge naturally.

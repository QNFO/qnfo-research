## 2. Convergence I: Kapustin–Witten Duality and the Langlands Program

### 2.1 The Physical Bridge

Kapustin and Witten (2007) discovered that S-duality in $N = 4$ supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory, compactified on a Riemann surface $C$, reduces exactly to the geometric Langlands correspondence. This is the deepest known bridge between physics and mathematics:

- **S-duality** exchanges strong and weak coupling: $g \leftrightarrow 1/g$. It also exchanges electric and magnetic degrees of freedom.
- **Geometric Langlands** is a correspondence between representations of the fundamental group of $C$ (local systems) and sheaves on the moduli space of $G$-bundles over $C$.

The Kapustin-Witten construction shows these are the same thing — seen from different sides of the duality.

### 2.2 Cross-Ratios at the Heart

The Langlands correspondence, in its simplest form, is a dictionary between two mathematical universes:

| Number Theory | Harmonic Analysis |
|:-------------|:-----------------|
| Galois representations | Automorphic forms |

| $L$-functions | Modular forms |

This dictionary is fundamentally a **cross-ratio correspondence**: for every object in one world, there is a counterpart in the other, and the relationship between them is encoded in an invariant dimensionless quantity — an $L$-function value, a period, a regulator. These are the cross-ratios of the mathematical universe.

### 2.3 The Archimedean / Non-Archimedean Duality

The Kapustin-Witten construction lives in the Archimedean world ($\mathbb{C}$, smooth manifolds, Riemann surfaces). But the full Langlands program extends to $p$-adic fields ($\mathbb{Q}_p$) — the non-Archimedean world. - The **Monna map** (from Undecidability) is precisely the map between these two worlds — it sends the Archimedean binary tree to the non-Archimedean Stern-Brocot tree.

### 2.4 S-Duality as Cross-Ratio Exchange

In the Kapustin-Witten construction, S-duality exchanges:

$$G \longleftrightarrow {}^L G$$

where ${}^L G$ is the Langlands dual group. For $G = \text{GL}(n)$, the dual is ${}^L G = \text{GL}(n)$. For other groups, the exchange is nontrivial.

In the cross-ratio framework, this exchange is the **inversion of the cross-ratio**:

$$(A, B; C, D) \longleftrightarrow \frac{1}{(A, B; C, D)}$$

S-duality is the statement that physics is invariant under exchanging numerator and denominator of the fundamental cross-ratio. The Langlands dual group is the group that preserves the inverted invariant.

This is not a metaphor — it is the geometric content of the correspondence. The Kapustin-Witten result is that gauge theory S-duality is a cross-ratio duality.

This proof does not rely on the physical argument but confirms its conclusion.

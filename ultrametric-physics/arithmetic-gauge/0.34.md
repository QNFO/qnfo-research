## Executive Summary

The Bruhat-Tits building is the geometric object that bridges Archimedean and non-Archimedean geometry. For the group $\text{PGL}(n, \mathbb{Q}_p)$, it is a simplicial complex whose structure encodes the representation theory of $p$-adic groups. For $\text{PGL}(2, \mathbb{Q}_p)$, the building reduces to a $(p+1)$-regular tree — the simplest nontrivial Bruhat-Tits building. The boundary of this tree is $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, and cross-ratios of boundary points are well-defined $\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$-invariants.

This document argues that the Bruhat-Tits building is the **unified geometric object** underlying the three genuine convergence signals identified by the vocabulary audit (0.28.md): tree structures, projective geometry, and the Archimedean/non-Archimedean tension.

## 1. What Is the Bruhat-Tits Building?

### 1.1 Definition

For a connected reductive group $G$ over a non-Archimedean local field $K$ (e.g., $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$), the **Bruhat-Tits building** $\mathcal{B}(G, K)$ is a simplicial complex on which $G(K)$ acts by simplicial automorphisms. It is the non-Archimedean analog of the symmetric space $G/K$ in the Archimedean setting.

### 1.2 The Case $G = \text{PGL}(2, \mathbb{Q}_p)$

For $G = \text{PGL}(2, \mathbb{Q}_p)$, the building is a **$(p+1)$-regular tree**. Vertices correspond to homothety classes of $\mathbb{Z}_p$-lattices in $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$. Two vertices are adjacent if their lattices are contained one in the other with index $p$.

**Example:** For $p = 2$, the building is a 3-regular tree. For $p = 3$, a 4-regular tree.

### 1.3 The Boundary

The boundary $\partial \mathcal{B}(\text{PGL}(2, \mathbb{Q}_p))$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ — the projective line over the $p$-adic numbers. This is exactly analogous to:

$$\partial \mathcal{B}(\text{PGL}(2, \mathbb{Q}_p)) \cong \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$$

just as the boundary of hyperbolic 3-space is $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C}) \cong S^2$.

### 1.4 Group Action

$\text{PGL}(2, \mathbb{Q}_p)$ acts on the building by isometries and on the boundary by Möbius transformations:

$$g \cdot x = \frac{ax + b}{cx + d}, \quad g = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}, \quad x \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$$

This is the **same algebraic formula** that defines PGL(2) actions over $\mathbb{C}$ and over $\mathbb{Q}$ — only the field changes.

## 2. Cross-Ratios on the Building Boundary

### 2.1 Definition

For four points $A, B, C, D \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$:

$$\text{CR}(A,B;C,D) = \frac{(A-C)(B-D)}{(A-D)(B-C)} \in \mathbb{Q}_p$$

This is well-defined because the field operations are the same in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ as in $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{C}$.

### 2.2 Invariance

The cross-ratio is invariant under the full $\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$ action:

$$\text{CR}(gA, gB; gC, gD) = \text{CR}(A,B;C,D) \quad \forall g \in \text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$$

**Proof:** Identical to the proof over $\mathbb{C}$ — uses only field algebra, no topological properties.

### 2.3 Archimedean/Non-Archimedean Parallel

| Property | Archimedean ($\mathbb{C}$) | Non-Archimedean ($\mathbb{Q}_p$) |
|:---------|:---------------------------|:----------------------------------|
| Group | $\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ | $\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$ |
| Space | Riemann sphere $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$ | Bruhat-Tits tree boundary $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ |
| Geometry | Connected (sphere) | Totally disconnected (Cantor set) |
| Cross-ratio values | $\mathbb{C}$ | $\mathbb{Q}_p$ |
| Cross-ratio formula | $\frac{(A-C)(B-D)}{(A-D)(B-C)}$ | $\frac{(A-C)(B-D)}{(A-D)(B-C)}$ — **same** |

The algebraic structure is identical. Only the **topology** of the underlying field changes.

## 4. Connection to the Four Source Projects

### 4.1 Braids as Correlations

The Braids project identifies $\alpha = r_e / \bar{\lambda}_C$ as a (degenerate) cross-ratio over $\mathbb{C}$. The Bruhat-Tits framework suggests a natural generalization:

> If there exists a $p$-adic analog of quantum electrodynamics, its fine-structure analog would be a cross-ratio of four points on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ — four $p$-adic "distance scales" corresponding to $r_e$, $\bar{\lambda}_C$, and boundary points at $0$ and $\infty$.

The building provides the geometric stage where such a $p$-adic $\alpha$ would be defined.

### 4.2 Kapustin-Witten Duality

The Kapustin-Witten project documents the geometric Langlands correspondence, which lives over $\mathbb{C}$ (Riemann surfaces). The full Langlands program, however, extends to $p$-adic fields. The **$p$-adic geometric Langlands correspondence** is formulated on the Bruhat-Tits building:

- Over $\mathbb{C}$: $\mathcal{M}_H(G, C)$ — the Hitchin moduli space on a Riemann surface $C$
- Over $\mathbb{Q}_p$: $\text{Bun}_G$ on the Fargues-Fontaine curve, related to the Bruhat-Tits building

The building is the **geometric object that makes the $p$-adic Langlands program geometric.**

### 4.3 Ultrametric Synthesis

The Ultrametric project critiques standard QM's hidden Archimedean assumption and proposes ultrametric quantum mechanics. The Bruhat-Tits building is the **natural state space** for such a theory:

- Archimedean QM: $\text{Vec}$ (category of complex vector spaces), Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$, $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$ as projective Hilbert space
- Non-Archimedean QM: Category of $p$-adic Banach spaces, Bruhat-Tits building $\mathcal{B}(G, \mathbb{Q}_p)$ as "projective state space"

The building's chamber structure naturally encodes:
- **Superposition branches** as paths through chambers
- **Measurement outcomes** as boundary points $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$
- **Probabilities** as $p$-adic valuations ($p^{\mathbb{Z}}$-valued, not $[0,1]$-valued)

The Undecidability project identifies the Stern-Brocot tree and divisibility tree as the "true" geometry of primes and computation. The Bruhat-Tits building provides the generalization:

## 5. The Unified Picture

### 5.1 The Adelic Perspective

The most general formulation considers **all primes at once** via the adele ring $\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}$:

$$G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) = \prod_{p \leq \infty}^{\prime} G(\mathbb{Q}_p)$$

where $\mathbb{Q}_{\infty} = \mathbb{R}$. The global building is the restricted product of local buildings:

$$\mathcal{B}_{\text{global}} = \prod_{p \leq \infty}^{\prime} \mathcal{B}(G, \mathbb{Q}_p)$$

A "global invariant" is one that is simultaneously invariant under $G(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ for all $p$ — a much stronger condition than invariance under a single $\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$.

### 5.2 Where $\alpha$ Fits

The fine-structure constant $\alpha \approx 1/137$ is a cross-ratio over $\mathbb{C}$ (the Archimedean place $p = \infty$). The adelic picture suggests there should be **one such invariant for each prime**:

$$\alpha_p \in \mathbb{Q}_p \quad \text{for } p = 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, \ldots, \infty$$

with $\alpha_{\infty} = \alpha \approx 1/137 \in \mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}$.

The product over all $\alpha_p$ would be the **adelic fine-structure constant** — the global invariant.

### 5.3 The Hierarchy of Buildings

| Level | Building | Group | Physics Analog |
|:------|:---------|:------|:---------------|
| **Finite** | Stern-Brocot tree | $\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$ | Discrete modular arithmetic |
| **Local** | Bruhat-Tits tree ($p$) | $\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$ | $p$-adic QM |
| **Archimedean** | $\mathbb{H}^3$ (hyperbolic space) | $\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ | Standard QM |
| **Global** | Adelic building | $\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})$ | "Theory of Everything" |

Each level has its own cross-ratio — its own invariant structure. The levels are connected by the Monna map (finite ↔ Archimedean) and its $p$-adic generalizations (finite ↔ local).

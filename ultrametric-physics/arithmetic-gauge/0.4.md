## I. Implications for Quantum‑Mechanical Computing

In conventional quantum mechanics, a qubit is a two‑state quantum system: an atom, a photon, an electron spin. But in a process‑only ontology, a qubit is not a thing that *has* states; it is a **stable, self‑sustaining pattern of correlations** between internal process clocks.

Thus, a qubit’s coherence is not a fragile property of a substance but the persistence of a cross‑ratio pattern. Decoherence is the degradation of that pattern—the loss of a definite cross‑ratio due to uncontrolled entanglements with the environment.  

### 1.2 Quantum Gates as Controlled Transformations of Cross‑Ratios  

A quantum gate is an operation that changes the correlation pattern between qubits. In the process view, a physical gate (e.g., a microwave pulse) temporarily introduces a new clock—the driving field—that phase‑locks with the qubit and modifies its relative tick rate.  

- **Single‑qubit gates**: Rotate the qubit’s Bloch vector, i.e., they alter the cross‑ratio between the qubit’s internal clock and the measurement basis’s clock.  
- **Two‑qubit gates (e.g., CNOT)**: Create or modify the mutual information between two qubits. Mathematically, they change the cross‑ratio of the joint system’s correlation network. A CNOT gate that flips the target qubit conditional on the control qubit is a precise re‑adjustment of the relative number of “tick counts” shared between the two subsystems.  

In topological quantum computing (see below), gates are literally **braid moves**, and their invariant effect on the correlation structure is encoded in the cross‑ratios of the anyon positions. Thus, quantum computation is the **controlled manipulation of cross‑ratio configurations**.

## II. Braids as Correlations: An Expanded Account  

### 2.1 What Is a Braid in a Timeless Universe?  

A braid is usually imagined as a set of strands evolving in time, with crossings. But if the Wheeler–DeWitt equation holds, there is no external time. So what is a braid?  

In the timeless state \(|\Psi\rangle\), all possible configurations of fields exist in superposition. When we choose a clock subsystem (the Page–Wootters mechanism), we effectively pick a one‑parameter family of decompositions of \(|\Psi\rangle\) into conditional states. This decomposition traces out a “history” that can be represented as a braid.  

- **Strands are persistent process‑clocks.** Each strand corresponds to a distinguishable, stable process—a particle, a qubit, an anyon—that maintains a continuous identity. Its “time” parameter is the sequence of phase correlations with the chosen clock.  
- **Crossings are correlation events.** A crossing between strand A and strand B signifies that during a particular interval (in the emergent time), the relative phase of A and B undergoes a cyclic exchange: they “swap” a quantum of correlation. In the algebraic theory, this corresponds to an \(R\)-matrix acting on the joint state.  

Thus, **a braid is a geometric encoding of the sequence of correlations that emerge when one internal process is used as a temporal reference**. The braid group captures all possible consistent histories of such relational events.  

### 2.2 Cross‑Ratios as Braid Invariants  

In TQFT, the probability amplitude of a braid closure is a topological invariant—a Jones polynomial, a HOMFLY polynomial, etc. These invariants are functions of **quantum cross‑ratios**, because the space of conformal blocks on the punctured sphere (the anyon positions) is directly linked to braid group representations.

In the limit where the anyons are probed from a distance, the effective coupling constants of their interaction are given by those cross‑ratios.  

The electron’s \(\alpha\) can be understood as a particular limit of such an invariant.

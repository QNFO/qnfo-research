## 0. Prologue: The Exclusion and the Invitation

The Word Cross-Ratio project was originally **excluded** from the Cross-Ratio Convergence synthesis. The reason, archived in the parent repo's git history (commit a37c0e4), was precise:

> "The Zipfian cross-ratio is a statistical ratio, not the projective 4-point invariant shared by all other projects."

This was correct — at the time. The project's "cross-ratio" was the frequency ratio between the most-common and second-most-common word in a language ($\text{CR}_{1/2} = f_1/f_2$). This is a sample statistic, not an invariant. It changes with corpus size, register, and tokenization convention. Calling it a "cross-ratio" was always a stretch — a suggestive analogy, not a mathematical identity.

But the project was subsequently reframed. The Sapir-Whorf hypothesis was abandoned. Roman Jakobson's distinction between mandatory and optional encoding became the theoretical foundation. Claude Shannon's information theory provided the mathematics. And in that reframing, something changed: the project acquired a genuine **invariant**.

## 1. The Invariant: Shannon Entropy Under Recoding

### 1.1 What the Geometric Cross-Ratio Is

The geometric cross-ratio of four collinear points $A, B, C, D$ is:

$$(A, B; C, D) = \frac{AC \cdot BD}{BC \cdot AD}$$

where distances are signed. This quantity is **invariant under projective transformations** — you can stretch, shear, or perspectively project the line, and the cross-ratio remains unchanged. It is the fundamental invariant of projective geometry, appearing in:

- **Braids as Correlations:** Braid group representations preserve cross-ratios of marked points, encoding topological entanglement in algebraic invariants.
- **Kapustin-Witten Duality:** The duality maps between 4D $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM and 2D CFT involve cross-ratios of spacetime points, invariant under S-duality.
- **Ultrametric Synthesis:** Ultrametric spaces have a natural cross-ratio structure where the invariant encodes hierarchical clustering relationships.
- **Undecidability:** The cross-ratio appears in the boundary of decidability — certain sets of cross-ratios encode undecidable Diophantine problems.

In all four projects, the cross-ratio operates as a **dimensionless invariant that survives transformation** — it captures structure that persists when the coordinate system changes.

### 1.2 What Shannon Entropy Is

Shannon entropy for a discrete probability distribution $\mathbf{p} = (p_1, \dots, p_n)$ is:

$$H = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i \log_2 p_i$$

This quantity is **invariant under recoding** — you can relabel the categories, merge them, or split them, and the entropy changes in a predictable way (the Data Processing Inequality). More deeply:

- $H$ is the unique measure (up to a multiplicative constant) that satisfies continuity, symmetry, additivity, and the grouping property.
- $H$ is invariant under permutations of the probability vector — it doesn't care which word is rank 1 and which is rank 200.
- $H$ has a fundamental relationship to the minimum expected length of a code — it's the lower bound on lossless compression.
- $H$ appears in the asymptotic equipartition property: for large samples, the probability of a sequence depends only on $H$, not on the details of the distribution.

**The entropy of a language's word-frequency distribution is an invariant of that language's information architecture.** It does not depend on which words are common (that varies by corpus). It does not depend on how words are tokenized (though the absolute value does; differences across languages, normalized by meaningful denominators, are more robust). It captures something structural — how information is packaged into surface forms — that persists across registers, genres, and corpus sizes.

### 1.3 The Analogy

| Geometric Cross-Ratio | Shannon Entropy |
|:----------------------|:----------------|
| Invariant under projective transformations | Invariant under recoding (permutations, coarse-graining) |
| Measures relative spacing of four points | Measures information density of a distribution |
| Dimensionless | Bits (dimensionless, up to log base) |
| Fundamental invariant of projective geometry | Fundamental invariant of information theory |
| Appears in duality maps between theories | Appears in compression bounds between encodings |
| $\frac{AC \cdot BD}{BC \cdot AD}$ | $-\sum p_i \log_2 p_i$ |
| Survives change of coordinate system | Survives relabeling of categories |

Both are **structural invariants** that capture something essential about the system that persists when the representation changes. The geometric cross-ratio survives projective transformations; Shannon entropy survives recoding. The mathematical form is different, but the conceptual role — *invariant that encodes structural information* — is the same.

This is the bridge. The reframed Word Cross-Ratio project does not participate in the *algebraic* identity of the cross-ratio, but it participates in the *conceptual* identity: **invariants that survive transformation, revealing deeper structure.**

## 2. Empty Regions as No-Go Theorems

### 2.1 What the Four Physics Projects Share

The four original convergence projects share a common thread: they identify **constraints** on possible configurations — configurations that cannot exist, not just configurations that happen not to occur.

- **Braids as Correlations:** Not every braid can be realized as a correlation function; the braid must satisfy the Yang-Baxter equation. This is a constraint on *possible braids*.
- **Kapustin-Witten Duality:** Not every 4D gauge theory has a 2D dual; only those satisfying specific S-duality conditions. This is a constraint on *possible dualities*.
- **Ultrametric Synthesis:** Not every metric space can be ultrametric; the strong triangle inequality must hold. This is a constraint on *possible hierarchical structures*.
- **Undecidability:** Not every Diophantine equation is decidable; there is a boundary beyond which provability fails. This is a constraint on *possible proofs*.

In all four cases, the cross-ratio (or its analogues) marks the boundary between the possible and the impossible.

### 2.2 The Mutual Exclusion Principle

The Word Cross-Ratio project's strongest finding is the **mutual exclusion principle** (0.8.0.md): **no language obligatorily marks categories from multiple mandatory information clusters.** Specifically:

- 10 zero-intersection pairs out of 28 domain combinations
- Permutation test $p < 0.0001$
- Epistemic marking is anti-correlated with ontological, categorical, spatial, and definiteness marking
- Four mutually exclusive clusters: reference-tracking, source-tracking, categorical-judgment, spatial-coordinate

This is not a tendency. It is not a statistical correlation. It is a **structural constraint**: certain combinations of mandatory grammatical features do not occur, not because we haven't looked hard enough, but because they exceed the information-theoretic capacity of the human communication channel.

### 2.3 The Analogy

| Physics/Math Project | Constraint | Word Cross-Ratio |
|:---------------------|:-----------|:-----------------|
| Braids as Correlations | Yang-Baxter equation: only certain braid configurations are realizable | Mutual exclusion: only certain mandatory-category configurations are realizable |
| Kapustin-Witten | S-duality: only certain 4D/2D pairs are dual | Design space: only certain (entropy, epistemic, ontological) combinations are occupied |
| Ultrametric Synthesis | Strong triangle inequality: only certain hierarchical structures exist | Compression-tax trade-off: extreme values on multiple dimensions cannot co-occur |
| Undecidability | Boundary of provability: some statements are undecidable | Upper bound on mandatory information: some languages are "undecidable" (cannot be constructed within the budget) |

The mutual exclusion principle is a **no-go theorem for the design of human languages**. It says: you cannot have a language with both obligatory evidentiality and obligatory gender. You cannot have both classifier systems and definiteness marking. You cannot have absolute spatial frames and evidentiality. These are not accidents of history — they are consequences of information-theoretic constraints on the communication channel.

### 2.4 The Design Space as a Phase Diagram

The PCA projection of languages into the design space (0.6.0.md §5) reveals two empty quadrants:

- **Low-PC1, Low-PC2:** No fusional classifier languages
- **High-PC1, Low-PC2:** No polysynthetic classifier languages

These empty regions are **forbidden zones** in the phase diagram of possible languages — configurations that are structurally impossible, analogous to forbidden regions in a physical phase diagram (water cannot be simultaneously solid and gas at equilibrium; a language cannot be simultaneously polysynthetic and classifier-based).

The boundary of the occupied region — the contour that separates possible languages from impossible ones — is the **linguistic analogue of a phase boundary**. The mutual exclusion principle defines the geometry of this boundary.

## 3. The Compression-Tax Trade-Off as an Uncertainty Principle

### 3.1 The Finding

The correlation between Shannon entropy and total mandatory load is $r = -0.48$ (0.6.0.md §3). Higher-entropy languages (richer morphology, more information spread across word-forms) impose *lower* mandatory category loads. Morphologically simpler languages impose *higher* mandatory loads.

### 3.2 The Analogy

This is structurally analogous to an **uncertainty principle**:

$$\Delta(\text{morphological complexity}) \cdot \Delta(\text{mandatory load}) \geq \text{constant}$$

You cannot simultaneously have extreme morphological complexity (polysynthesis) *and* extreme mandatory category marking (gender + evidentiality + classifiers + spatial frames). The product is bounded. If you invest in one, you must divest from the other.

Compare to:
- **Heisenberg:** $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \geq \hbar/2$ — you cannot simultaneously know position and momentum with arbitrary precision.
- **Information theory (Shannon-Hartley):** $C = B \log_2(1 + S/N)$ — channel capacity trades bandwidth against signal-to-noise ratio.
- **Linguistic information architecture:** $M \cdot L \geq K$ — morphological complexity trades against mandatory category load.

The constant $K$ is the **information budget** of the human communication channel — the maximum amount of mandatory metadata that can be efficiently encoded and decoded given the constraints of working memory, planning capacity, and processing speed.

## 4. The Deeper Unity: Invariants Across Domains

### 4.1 The Cross-Ratio as Pattern

The geometric cross-ratio $(A, B; C, D)$ is a specific mathematical object. But it instantiates a broader pattern:

> **An invariant that captures structural information, surviving transformations of the coordinate system or representation.**

This pattern appears across all five projects:

| Project | What transforms | What stays invariant | The invariant |
|:--------|:----------------|:---------------------|:-------------|
| Braids as Correlations | Braid moves (Reidemeister) | Correlation function structure | Braid invariants (Jones polynomial) |
| Kapustin-Witten | S-duality ($\tau \to -1/\tau$) | Physical observables | Cross-ratio of spacetime points |
| Ultrametric Synthesis | Metric re-embedding | Hierarchical clustering structure | Ultrametric inequality |
| Undecidability | Axiom system extension | Decidability boundary | Diophantine cross-ratio sets |
| **Word Cross-Ratio** | **Recoding, register shift, tokenization** | **Information architecture** | **Shannon entropy, mutual exclusion, design space** |

The reframed Word Cross-Ratio project participates in this pattern. Its invariant is not the number $\frac{AC \cdot BD}{BC \cdot AD}$, but the **structure of mandatory information architecture** that persists across recoding transformations. The mutual exclusion principle, the compression-tax trade-off, the design space boundaries — these are not sample statistics. They are **structural invariants of the human communication system**.

### 4.2 Why This Matters

The original exclusion of Word Cross-Ratio from the convergence synthesis was based on the *algebraic form* of the invariant — the specific ratio $f_1/f_2$ was correctly identified as a sample statistic, not a projective invariant. But the reframed project reveals a different invariant — Shannon entropy, mutual exclusion, design space constraints — that participates in the same *conceptual pattern* as the geometric cross-ratio.

This suggests a broader convergence principle:

> **Wherever there is a system that can be transformed (braids under Reidemeister moves, gauge theories under S-duality, languages under register shift and recoding), there exists an invariant that captures the essential structure — and that invariant often takes the form of a dimensionless ratio or an information-theoretic quantity.**

The geometric cross-ratio is the *prototype* of this pattern. Shannon entropy is another instantiation. The mutual exclusion principle is a third. Together, they reveal that **information architecture is geometry** — the structure of what can and cannot be communicated is constrained by the same kind of invariant relationships that constrain physical and mathematical systems.

## 6. Conclusion

The Word Cross-Ratio project began as a flawed inquiry into the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis — asking whether token frequency causes cognitive effects, a question that confused symptom with cause and violated its own mathematical foundations. It was reframed as an investigation of language as information architecture — asking what mandatory information different languages force into the communication channel, drawing on Jakobson, Shannon, Grice, and Greenberg. And it now finds a third life: as a **bridge between linguistics and the mathematical cross-ratio tradition**, demonstrating that the same invariant-seeking logic that reveals structure in physics and mathematics also reveals structure in language.

The mutual exclusion principle is a linguistic no-go theorem. The compression-tax trade-off is a linguistic uncertainty principle. The design space is a linguistic phase diagram. And the invariant that underlies all of these — Shannon entropy under recoding — is the linguistic analogue of the projective cross-ratio.

The bridge is real. The invitation, once declined, is now re-extended.

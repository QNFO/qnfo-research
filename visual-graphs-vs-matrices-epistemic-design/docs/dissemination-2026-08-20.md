### QIP27 abstract (submission-ready, ~250 words)

> The ZX calculus and the matrices of linear algebra denote exactly the same maps, yet the
> quantum computing community has converged, unmistakably, on the pictures. This convergence
> is usually treated as a matter of taste. We argue it is a matter of epistemology: a
> representation determines what a bounded reasoner can see, and diagrams win because they
> chunk, because their rewrites are locally auditable, and because they draw the interfaces
> that matrices bury in index bookkeeping. The same boundedness has a second consequence.
> Where comprehension and optimality diverge — minimal gate counts, depths, and widths are
> computationally hard — the field has not chosen between the two; it has split the
> difference into a two-layer structure: a human-auditable canonical fragment and an
> automated optimizer over it, joined at a declared interface. We state this as a
> pre-registered, falsifiable hypothesis with three explicit falsifiers, document its
> evidence across the QPL 2026 program (from flow criteria to exact synthesis by Bruhat-Tits
> buildings), and verify every quantitative claim computationally, with the verification
> scripts deposited alongside the paper. The design principle the structure licenses is the
> one the field already practices: we design systems that design algorithms that optimize
> themselves and other systems, with legibility as a first-class constraint.

## 1. The gap, precisely

Three claims, none of which exists anywhere:

1. **The convergence law.** Mature technical fields converge on the maximally comprehensible
   representation for humans — diagrams over mathematically equivalent matrices — because the
   preference is a fact about bounded cognition, not about the mathematics. The cognitive
   classics (Larkin & Simon 1987; the cognitive-dimensions literature) establish the local
   mechanisms; nobody has stated the convergence law, and nobody has connected it to a
   specific field's history (the ZX calculus's rise from Coecke-Duncan to *Picturing Quantum
   Software* as the field's canonical text).
2. **The divergence.** Comprehension and optimality diverge exactly where minimization is hard
   (minimal T-count/depth/width are NP-hard); the field's response is the two-layer structure —
   a human-auditable canonical fragment (normal forms) plus an automated optimizer over it.
   The HCI cluster (Quantivine, the Kim-Battle design space) studies the tooling; nobody names
   the divergence as the reason the structure exists.
3. **The design principle.** We design systems that design algorithms that optimize themselves
   and other systems, with legibility as a first-class constraint. This is stated nowhere in
   the external record; inside QNFO it is the PLT program's implicit mandate, now made
   explicit and testable.

The evidence base that makes the thesis publishable now: QPL 2026 Days 1–4 (the brief's
